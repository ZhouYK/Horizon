## [Nintendo DMCA Takedown Removes 400+ Switch Emulator Repos from GitHub](https://torrentfreak.com/nintendo-wipes-out-400-switch-emulator-repos-in-single-day-github-sweep/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Nintendo filed 7 DMCA anti-circumvention notices with GitHub on the same day, resulting in the takedown of over 400 Switch emulator repositories and forks. The sweep included 311 repositories in the suyu network and 29 repositories of the discontinued Skyline Android emulator. This is one of the largest single-day DMCA enforcement actions against emulator projects on GitHub, reflecting Nintendo&\#x27;s aggressive legal strategy to protect the Switch ecosystem. It could set a chilling precedent for open-source emulator development and raise ongoing debates about the legality of emulation under anti-circumvention law. Nintendo&\#x27;s notices claim the emulators use unauthorized keys to decrypt Switch games, which it says violates DMCA anti-circumvention provisions. The notices cite the Yuzu settlement case as precedent, although neither the Yuzu suit nor related cases have been resolved through a substantive court ruling.

**Background**: Nintendo Switch emulators like yuzu and its fork suyu are open-source programs that let users play Switch games on PCs and mobile devices. DMCA anti-circumvention provisions in the U.S. prohibit bypassing technological protection measures, and Nintendo has argued that emulators require circumventing encryption to run commercial games. This legal pressure follows Nintendo&\#x27;s 2024 settlement with the creators of yuzu, which led to the emulator being discontinued and its source code removed.

## [Pew: 35% of New Web Pages Since ChatGPT Contain AI Traces](https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/ai-webpages-internet-dead-internet-theory-b3037019.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Pew Research Center analyzed nearly 500,000 English-language web pages and found that 10% overall show clear signs of AI-generated content, while 35% of pages published after ChatGPT&\#x27;s release contain AI traces. This is one of the first large-scale, data-driven measurements of AI&\#x27;s penetration into web content, providing empirical evidence for concerns like the &\#x27;dead internet theory.&\#x27; It signals that AI-generated text is becoming a dominant force in the information ecosystem, affecting how people discover and trust online information. The study detected AI traces through linguistic markers: em-dashes roughly doubled, Oxford comma usage rose 63%, and typical chatbot vocabulary doubled. AI traces were also about twice as common on .com sites as on .org sites, and ten times more common than on .edu or .gov domains.

**Background**: The &\#x27;dead internet theory&\#x27; is an idea that emerged in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, suggesting that much of the internet is generated by bots and algorithms rather than humans. It has gained renewed attention as AI tools like ChatGPT make it easier to produce content at scale. Cloudflare&\#x27;s monitoring has also shown that bot traffic accounts for about 31% of all application traffic, supporting concerns about automated content. This study provides new quantitative evidence about how quickly AI is reshaping the web.

## [Open-Source AI Models Halve Catch-Up Time Each Generation](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/are-open-models-catching-up) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SemiAnalysis reports that open-source AI models are catching up to closed-source frontier models faster with each generation, with the time to parity now halving every generation. In the agent era, Kimi K2.6 overtook Opus 4.5 in 4.8 months, and GLM-5.2 surpassed GPT-5.2 in 6 months. This accelerating catch-up signals a growing commoditization of the model layer, undermining the proprietary advantages of frontier labs. Open models like GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 can already handle coding and agent tasks that helped Anthropic reach over $65 billion in annualized revenue, threatening its business model despite its productization strengths. SemiAnalysis divides LLM history into three eras—early scaling, inference, and agents—and finds the capability gap between open and closed models fluctuates cyclically rather than monotonically. The report cautions that benchmark results are not everything, and Anthropic&\#x27;s productization remains a competitive edge.

The report concerns open-weight models such as Moonshot AI&\#x27;s Kimi series and Z.ai&\#x27;s GLM series, which compete with proprietary models like GPT and Opus. Historically, frontier proprietary models stayed ahead, but the pace at which open models match them has accelerated. ## [US Groups Urge FTC to Investigate AI Firms Over Destroying Books](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/21/ftc-ai-companies-book-destruction-investigate) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On August 21, 2026, more than a dozen U.S. civil society groups, including Demand Progress Education Fund and Consumer Federation of America, wrote to the Federal Trade Commission urging an investigation into AI companies that buy, scan, and destroy physical books to train models. The groups argue the practice may violate Section 5 of the FTC Act as an unfair method of competition. If the FTC takes up the case, the dispute over AI training data would move beyond copyright into competition regulation, creating a new antitrust avenue for challenging how AI companies acquire data. A formal investigation could pressure AI labs to change their data-sourcing practices and affect the broader LLM ecosystem. The letter cites Anthropic&\#x27;s multi-million-dollar purchases of books whose spines were cut off so pages could be scanned for Claude, while Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI face similar copyright suits over training data. The groups do not ask to restrict AI training itself, but contend that hoarding and destroying scarce works raises rivals&\#x27; costs and builds a moat; Anthropic&\#x27;s internal scan-and-destroy effort was reportedly called &\#x27;Project Panama.&\#x27;

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 22, 15:40

**Background**: AI companies train large language models by copying massive corpora of text; copyright lawsuits claim this copying is direct infringement under U.S. copyright law. Section 5 of the FTC Act, originally added in 1914, lets the FTC challenge &\#x27;unfair methods of competition,&\#x27; and the 1938 Wheeler-Lea Act also added jurisdiction over unfair or deceptive acts. This complaint seeks to treat the destruction of physical books as a competition problem rather than only a copyright problem.

## [Take-Two Subpoenas Microsoft for Device IDs in GTA 6 Leak Hunt](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/console-gaming/take-two-subpoenas-microsoft-for-windows-device-ids-of-everyone-in-three-discord-servers-in-gta-6-leak-hunt) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Take-Two filed two DMCA subpoenas on August 20 in the Southern District of New York, demanding Microsoft and Discord reveal the identity of the GTA 6 leaker known as CyberLeek. The subpoenas request Windows MachineGuid device IDs, IP addresses, phone numbers, and even OneDrive contents for all accounts that posted in three named Discord servers since June 1. This case underscores the sweeping reach of DMCA subpoenas in the gaming industry, where companies can compel platforms to hand over personal data of entire communities, not just alleged infringers. It raises significant privacy and due-process concerns for Discord users, especially those with no connection to the leak. The subpoenas target three specific Discord servers, including DarkViperAU&\#x27;s server run by Australian creator Matthew Judge, who has denied any knowledge of the leak. The deadline for compliance is September 4. Notably, the request goes well beyond identifying a single user, demanding data on all posters since June 1.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 22, 11:41

**Background**: A DMCA subpoena \(under Section 512\(h\) of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act\) is a legal tool that allows copyright holders to compel online service providers to disclose the identity of an alleged infringer. MachineGuid is a unique identifier stored in the Windows registry, generated during installation, which can be used to tie accounts to specific devices. However, MachineGuid is not always unique in practice, as duplicates have been observed across machines.
