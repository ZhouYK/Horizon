## [NASA&\#x27;s Roman Space Telescope Launches on Falcon Heavy, Boosters Land](https://weibo.com/6560646233/RfOLkeG70) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NASA&\#x27;s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched on August 30, 2026, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket from Florida. Both side boosters successfully returned and landed in synchronization at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The Roman Space Telescope is NASA&\#x27;s next flagship observatory, with a field of view about 100 times larger than Hubble&\#x27;s, enabling wide-area surveys of the universe. It is expected to advance studies of dark energy, galaxy evolution, and exoplanets. The telescope uses a 2.4-meter mirror donated by the National Reconnaissance Office and carries two instruments: the Wide-Field Instrument, a 300.8-megapixel visible and near-infrared camera, and the Coronagraph Instrument for high-contrast imaging. It will operate from a Sun-Earth L2 halo orbit.

**Background**: The Roman Space Telescope is named after Nancy Grace Roman, NASA&\#x27;s first chief astronomer. Its design uses a Hubble-class mirror but with a much wider field of view, allowing it to survey large regions of the sky quickly. One of its key scientific goals is probing dark energy, the unknown force driving the accelerating expansion of the universe. The telescope was recommended as the top priority for the next decade of astronomy by a 2010 National Research Council decadal survey.

## [Sony Music and Publishers Sue Anthropic Over Pirated Training Data](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/files/2026/08/COMPLAINT-in-Sony_Music_Publishing_US_LLC_e.pdf) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Multiple music publishers, including Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell Music, filed a lawsuit in California federal court against Anthropic and its founders, alleging that Anthropic illegally downloaded over 7 million pirated books from LibGen and PiLiMi and scraped lyrics, removing copyright management information, to train its Claude models. The plaintiffs seek up to $150,000 per infringed work and a permanent injunction. This legal action escalates the copyright dispute over AI training data, following earlier similar lawsuits that resulted in settlements worth $1.5 billion. If successful, it could force AI companies to more carefully vet their training sources and set a precedent for copyright liability in AI development. The complaint specifically names LibGen and PiLiMi as pirate libraries used, and alleges removal of copyright management information \(CMI\) in violation of the DMCA. The case is filed in the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California.

**Background**: LibGen \(Library Genesis\) is a shadow library project that provides free access to millions of scholarly articles and books without authorization from copyright holders. PiLiMi \(Pirate Library Mirror\) is an anonymous project that mirrors shadow libraries, and was previously used in Anthropic&\#x27;s early training data based on court findings. Music publishers have been actively pursuing AI companies for using their lyrics without licenses, and earlier suits have led to large settlements.

## [California Passes Bill Exempting Open-Source OS from Age Verification Law](https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/california-lawmakers-unanimously-pass-linux-exemption-from-age-verification-law-software-distributed-under-the-gpl-mit-bsd-and-apache-licenses-are-exempt) ⭐️ 7.0/10

California lawmakers unanimously passed AB 1856, exempting operating systems distributed under GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache licenses from the state&\#x27;s age verification law. The bill now heads to the governor; the law was originally set to take effect on January 1, 2027. This exemption protects Linux distributions and other open-source OSes from burdensome age-verification requirements, preserving user privacy and open-source development. It also sets a precedent for how privacy and security legislation treats open-source software differently from proprietary operating systems. Debian, Fedora, Ubuntu, Arch, and BSD variants fall outside the law&\#x27;s scope, while Windows, macOS, iOS, and Android must still collect age information at account setup. It remains unclear whether SteamOS, an Arch-based distribution, qualifies for the exemption.

**Background**: Open-source licenses such as GPL, MIT, BSD, and Apache allow software to be freely used, modified, and distributed. California&\#x27;s age verification law, scheduled for 2027, would have required certain operating systems to verify user ages; AB 1856 carves out OSes distributed under those open-source licenses. SteamOS, built on Arch Linux by Valve, may or may not be exempt depending on how it is distributed.
