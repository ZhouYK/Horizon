## [China&\#x27;s new e-bike 3C rules end separate frame-and-battery sales](https://www.caixin.com/2026-11-02/102500072.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

China&\#x27;s Certification and Accreditation Administration \(CNCA\) put its revised mandatory product certification implementation rules for electric bicycles \(CNCA-C11-16:2026\) into force on November 1, 2026, requiring that complete vehicle assembly and inspection be finished inside the factory so that products leave the plant fully assembled. The rule closes the main channel through which e-bikes were illegally modified, since dealers could previously pair a certified frame with an uncertified or higher-capacity battery, so it reshapes retail models, compliance costs and product liability across the entire e-bike industry. With hundreds of millions of e-bikes in use in China and frequent fire and traffic safety incidents, the change affects manufacturers, dealers and consumers alike. The new rules were published on May 29, 2026, giving the industry a five-month transition in which certification bodies could issue certificates under either the old or new rules; from November 1 only the new version applies. The regulation also reinforces requirements that consumers look for the CCC mark and the product conformity certificate when buying an e-bike.

**Background**: 3C certification, or China Compulsory Certification \(CCC\), is the mandatory market-access certification for products deemed relevant to safety, health and the environment, and e-bikes have been on the catalogue for years. Under the old model, a certified frame could be sold with a battery chosen separately at the shop, which made it easy to swap in larger or non-compliant batteries and defeat speed limiters, creating what regulators call &quot;product consistency&quot; problems — the vehicle on the road no longer matches the vehicle that was certified. The revised rules are the latest step in a multi-year campaign to tighten e-bike safety oversight.
