## [EU Adds €2 Per-Item Handling Fee on Small Parcels From November 2026](https://www.caixin.com/2026-10-29/102498498.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Council of the EU has updated its &quot;EU action on the influx of small parcels&quot; page to confirm that, starting November 1, 2026, all member states will charge a €2 handling fee per item on small parcels imported from outside the bloc through cross-border e-commerce and other distance sales. The fee is part of the customs reform the Council finally approved in September 2026, with the rate set by the European Commission, and it comes on top of a €3 duty that applies to all e-commerce parcels valued under €150 from July 2026. The concrete fees and fixed dates materially raise the landed cost of low-value cross-border e-commerce, squeezing the thin margins that platforms such as Shein, Temu and AliExpress rely on and forcing sellers, logistics providers and warehousing operators serving the EU market to reprice or restructure. Because 91% of the 4.6 billion sub-€150 parcels imported into the EU in 2024 came from China, Chinese exporters and their European fulfillment partners are the most directly affected. The €2 is charged per item rather than per parcel, so a multi-item order could face a much larger cumulative bill, and it stacks with the €3 low-value duty introduced in July 2026; the Commission updated its guidance on both charges on October 7, 2026. Full per-item customs-debt calculation and standard customs procedures for small parcels are not expected until the EU Customs Data Hub becomes operational around 2028, so the current regime is effectively an interim flat-fee approach.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 29, 12:44

**Background**: The EU has long exempted imports valued under €150 from customs duty — the so-called de minimis threshold — while still applying VAT since July 2021, which meant most low-value e-commerce parcels entered with minimal checks. The volume of such parcels exploded to roughly 4.6 billion in 2024, which Brussels says strains customs authorities, undercuts EU-based sellers and raises product-safety, counterfeit and environmental concerns. The EU is therefore phasing in charges ahead of a broader customs reform, following the United States&\#x27; removal of its own de minimis threshold in 2025.
