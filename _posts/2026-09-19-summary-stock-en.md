## [Fed raises rates 25bps, first hike in three years, signals one more in 2026](https://www.caixin.com/2026-09-18/102486235.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The U.S. Federal Reserve raised its policy interest rate by 25 basis points, its first increase after a three-year pause, and Fed officials projected that one more hike would come before the end of 2026. Caixin reported the decision on September 17, 2026, alongside video coverage of the announcement. This marks the end of a three-year hold and the start of a new tightening cycle, a shift that ripples through global financial markets, currency values, bond yields and borrowing costs far beyond the United States. Emerging markets and economies with heavy dollar-denominated debt, including China&\#x27;s exporters and property sector, are especially sensitive to U.S. rate moves. The move is a 25-basis-point increase \(one quarter of a percentage point\), and policymakers paired it with guidance pointing to one additional hike within 2026, suggesting a gradual rather than aggressive path. **Background**: The Federal Reserve is the central bank of the United States, and it steers the economy mainly by setting a short-term policy interest rate. It is the first time such alerts have been sounded in the capital since the rebels reignited hostilities. Air-raid warnings over the Saudi capital mark a visible escalation of a conflict that had largely been contained, raising Middle East geopolitical risk and putting oil markets on notice given Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s role as one of the world&\#x27;s largest crude exporters. Any actual strike on Saudi territory or energy infrastructure could affect global supply expectations and insurance and shipping costs in the region. Riyadh is the seat of the Saudi government and central bank, which makes warnings over the capital symbolically and politically significant even absent confirmed hits.

**Background**: Yemen&\#x27;s Houthi rebel movement has been fighting a civil war against a Saudi-led coalition that intervened in Yemen in 2015, and over the years the rebels have repeatedly fired ballistic missiles and drones at Saudi territory, including a 2019 strike that temporarily knocked out roughly half of Saudi Aramco&\#x27;s crude output. ## [US, Denmark and Greenland Reach Security Deal Barring Rival Bases](https://international.caixin.com/2026-09-19/102486654.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 18, the United States, Denmark and Denmark&\#x27;s autonomous territory Greenland announced a trilateral agreement to strengthen security cooperation in Greenland, with the US saying it bars rival countries from establishing military bases or making sensitive-sector investments there. The deal is to be signed during the UN General Assembly in New York in late September. The agreement extends US influence over a strategically vital Arctic territory and its critical minerals, and signals that Washington is willing to formalise control over high-latitude choke points and resources amid growing competition with Russia and China. It also touches the sensitive question of Danish sovereignty and Greenlandic self-determination, making it a template for how Arctic security is being reshaped. The Danish and Greenlandic joint statement stresses that the agreement recognises Denmark&\#x27;s sovereignty and territorial integrity as well as the Greenlandic people&\#x27;s right to self-determination, and frames it as Arctic and North Atlantic security cooperation — notably omitting the references to military presence and sensitive investment that the US side highlighted. The divergence in the two accounts suggests the parties are describing the same document with different emphases, and the final text will be closely watched when it is signed at the UN General Assembly.

**Background**: Greenland is a vast, sparsely populated island that is an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark and sits between North America and Europe, astride emerging Arctic shipping routes and atop significant deposits of rare earths and other critical minerals. US interest in the island is long-standing: Washington already operates the Thule \(Pituffik\) air base there, and President Trump revived the idea of the US buying Greenland during his first term and has returned to the theme in his second. On September 7 he posted an AI-generated map on social media in which Canada, Greenland, Iceland, Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean were draped in US flags and labelled together with the existing United States as &quot;the United States of America&quot;.

## [Caixin Warns A-Share &\#x27;New Stocks Never Lose&\#x27; Mania May Backfire](https://weekly.caixin.com/2026-09-19/102486452.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Caixin Weekly published a special report on September 19, 2026 warning that A-share IPOs are overheating, citing Wind data showing 113 new listings in 2026 through September 15 with zero first-day breaks, an average first-day gain of 262.68% and a median of 172.86% — a ten-year high. The report highlights AI chipmaker Enflame Technology \(688801.SH\), which rose 179.22% to 397 yuan on its September 11 STAR Market debut, and robotics firm Unitree \(688836.SH\), which surged 460.34% on its first day three weeks earlier. The persistence of guaranteed first-day gains suggests new-share pricing, liquidity and leverage are distorting the market, and Caixin warns of a potential reversal that could hit retail investors who treat IPO subscription as a risk-free trade. It also raises the likelihood of regulatory tightening around IPO pricing and trading rules, with spillover effects on the broader A-share sentiment cycle. The wide gap between the 262.68% average and the 172.86% median gain shows the returns are skewed by a handful of extreme debuts rather than reflecting typical performance. The report frames the risk as a &quot;one-sided leveraged market&quot; \(单边杠杆市\) in which new stocks spike on day one only to slump the next day, and notes the pattern has persisted for years: 116 IPOs in 2025 averaged 259.33% first-day gains with none breaking issue price, and 100 IPOs in 2024 averaged 252.76% with only one breaking.

rss · 财新网 · Sep 19, 09:58

**Background**: Under China&\#x27;s registration-based IPO reform, stocks listed on the STAR Market \(科创板\) and ChiNext have no daily price limit for their first five trading days, which allows debut-day gains far larger than the standard 10% or 20% caps. Because new shares have historically almost always risen on day one, &quot;打新&quot; — subscribing to IPOs and selling into the first-day pop — became a popular low-risk strategy for retail investors. Caixin&\#x27;s report asks how A-share new stocks can escape this one-sided leveraged dynamic and avoid the day-one-spike, day-two-crash trap; Enflame Technology is a Chinese AI chipmaker backed by Tencent, while Unitree is Hangzhou-based maker of quadruped and humanoid robots.
