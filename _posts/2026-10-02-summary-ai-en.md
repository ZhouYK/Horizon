## [AI-triggered rapid response alerts linked to lower inpatient mortality](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi5wFBVV95cUxNMUc0NWhSWE1JMWVBRk5SNk5UWDNrNzRpSVZVaXRlb0hYUFNncnFkdm92OGhOX3JGY3RXUGtJdGQxT0Y4d2N3NUV3U0h2WjdfZGV0RGdZYUxEeXVtSnF5RGlMay1EQmtrM2ZENEJSaUhmTVp0T3NFbndtZVBnallGeTRKZEliVjV3WTItdWRKc3VsR0Mxa3JOR2tWSW1LdlF6dm8yWkxVdnpvZWg0WlN4YS1yWUJJeWlZZnhHM1lIY2hLcW1hY21OUlRBSWl0aWthY1JfNkRpazRTZDBjUEhLaU9GU0FyLWM?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A news summary published by 2 Minute Medicine reports that notifications triggered automatically by artificial intelligence \(AI\) for rapid response were associated with lower mortality among hospitalized inpatients. The item frames this as an association observed in clinical data, not as a controlled trial demonstrating a causal effect of the AI alerts. If automated AI alerts can reliably prompt earlier bedside intervention, they could help address &quot;failure to rescue&quot; — the long-recognized pattern in which deteriorating ward patients go unrecognized until cardiac arrest or ICU transfer — and could become a standard layer of hospital clinical decision support. Because in-hospital mortality is such a hard endpoint, any signal of benefit is likely to draw attention from hospital informatics teams, regulators, and vendors of early-warning systems. The headline describes an association rather than proven causation, and the brief summary does not disclose the study design, sample size, alert thresholds, or how much of the mortality difference might be explained by confounding such as staffing levels or case mix. Prior systematic reviews of conventional rapid response systems found they slightly reduce unexpected cardiac arrests but do not clearly affect overall in-hospital mortality, which makes the methodology of any AI-based result especially important to scrutinize.

google\_news · 2 Minute Medicine · Oct 2, 16:55

**Background**: Rapid response teams \(RRTs\) are hospital teams summoned to the bedside when a ward patient shows signs of imminent deterioration, with the goal of preventing ICU transfer, cardiac arrest, or death; they are now present in most U.S. hospitals, spurred partly by a 2008 Joint Commission patient safety goal. The rationale rests on evidence that up to roughly 70% of in-hospital cardiac arrests are preceded by six to eight hours of documented abnormal vital signs, meaning the deterioration is often visible in the data before anyone acts. Clinical decision support \(CDS\) systems are digital tools that deliver timely, patient-specific information to clinicians, and AI-based early-warning models are an emerging extension of that idea — continuously scanning electronic health record data and pushing alerts to the response team instead of waiting for a human to notice. Much of the debate in this field is about alert fatigue and false positives, which is why evidence on hard outcomes such as mortality matters.

## [Executive Order Renames AI as &quot;Super Intelligence&quot; in US Government](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMixgFBVV95cUxPOFpXejV2VG9MdW9MRjg2d2h3TElQZVhyVzRUU1U5blpPVVYzYWVab0p3WHBYMDlmQXEwODE1SHMzeXFJSlpsMTVFQWR5aENod2F2Q2F1WmwtMTlMM1ZsNFdPTjdwUlZOalJia0hELXF0MzB3cjBuRFhhN2R0My1iR3Z2dWVJeGNOdUFCTGs2bmxpVzJHaV9qTlVGdjhybTNxZlg1eXdjVnNfaFJER2FwNWNuQmhQdF92MEtoOXhqS3QyejFZSHc?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

President Donald J. Trump signed an executive order that replaces the term &quot;Artificial Intelligence&quot; \(&quot;AI&quot;\) with &quot;Super Intelligence&quot; \(&quot;SI&quot;\) throughout the executive branch, according to a White House fact sheet titled &quot;President Donald J. Trump Inaugurates The Era of Super Intelligence.&quot; Wiley Law&\#x27;s report on the order also flags that it signals potential federal legislative changes to how AI is regulated. Changing the official vocabulary used across federal agencies can ripple into procurement rules, funding priorities, standards work and future regulation, so AI developers, researchers and compliance teams that deal with the US government now have to track a shifting policy frame. It also signals that the White House intends a more assertive federal posture on advanced AI, which could set the terms of the debate before Congress acts. The order is a rebranding of terminology inside the executive branch rather than a change in any system&\#x27;s technical capability, and the White House fact sheet frames it as recognizing &quot;the continuously advancing technological frontier and the limitless&quot; potential of the field. In the technical literature, however, &quot;superintelligence&quot; has a specific meaning — philosopher Nick Bostrom defines it as &quot;any intellect that greatly exceeds the cognitive performance of humans in virtually all domains of interest&quot; — which is not the same as today&\#x27;s large language models and other narrow AI systems.

google\_news · Wiley Law · Oct 2, 19:38

**Background**: Artificial intelligence is the broad field of building computer systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as language understanding, image recognition and decision-making; today&\#x27;s systems are typically described as narrow or general-purpose tools rather than autonomous super-minds. Superintelligence is a much stronger and still hypothetical concept, popularized by philosophers such as Nick Bostrom, referring to an intellect far beyond the best human minds in virtually every domain. An executive order is a directive issued by the US president that governs how federal agencies operate, and unlike legislation passed by Congress it does not by itself create new statutory law — which is why reports describe the order as signaling possible, rather than enacting, federal legislative change.
