## [Northwestern traps a single positron for months in a quantum cyclotron](https://www.wired.com/story/scientists-trapped-an-antimatter-particle-for-months-to-study-its-secrets/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Gerald Gabrielse&\#x27;s group at Northwestern University used a one-particle quantum cyclotron to confine a single positron — the antimatter counterpart of the electron — for months in an ultra-high vacuum near absolute zero, using electromagnetic fields, without annihilating it. According to the American Physical Society, the new apparatus could improve antimatter measurement precision by roughly a factor of 30, since the same particle can now be measured repeatedly instead of only once. Repeated measurements on one trapped antiparticle turn antimatter tests from one-shot experiments into precision metrology, sharply tightening constraints on matter–antimatter symmetry. If positrons and electrons ever turn out to respond differently to magnetic fields, the laws describing matter and antimatter would have to be rewritten — a direct probe of one of physics&\#x27; deepest open questions. The technique builds on decades of work with one-electron quantum cyclotron measurements, in which a single charged particle is held in a Penning-style trap and occupies only its lowest cyclotron and spin states, so its cyclotron frequency can be read out via quantum-jump spectroscopy. So far no unexpected difference between positrons and electrons has been observed; the payoff would come from improved statistics and systematic control rather than from a new result announced now.

**Background**: Antimatter particles annihilate on contact with ordinary matter, so studying them requires isolating them in ultra-high vacuum with electromagnetic traps. A quantum cyclotron is essentially a one-particle artificial atom: a lone electron or positron circulating in a strong magnetic field, whose cyclotron frequency depends on the particle&\#x27;s charge-to-mass ratio, making it one of the most precise quantities physics can measure. Comparing that frequency for an electron and a positron is a stringent test of CPT symmetry, the principle that particles and antiparticles should behave identically under the combined operations of charge conjugation, parity and time reversal.

## [OpenAI Releases GPT-6.1 Terra With Halved API Pricing](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra, a new model focused on vision understanding and computer-use tasks, which reportedly scores 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra on benchmarks while pricing its standard API at 50% of the previous generation: $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 per million cached input tokens, and $5 per million output tokens. The model is being rolled out to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users of ChatGPT Work and Codex, and is described as writing more naturally at roughly one-third the cost. A 50% price cut combined with double-digit benchmark gains on a flagship OpenAI model would meaningfully lower the cost of running agentic and multimodal workloads, pressing competitors on both price and capability. Because the model is aimed at vision and computer-use, the release directly targets the emerging market for AI agents that operate browsers and desktop interfaces. OpenAI claims the error rate at low reasoning effort fell from 53.6% to 16.7%, a notable improvement for latency- and cost-sensitive deployments. Caveat: this report comes from a Telegram aggregator rather than a verified primary announcement, so the benchmark figures and pricing should be treated as unconfirmed until OpenAI&\#x27;s own documentation is checked.

He characterizes AI&\#x27;s financial payoff for non-tech US companies as &quot;zero.&quot; This directly challenges the dominant enterprise AI narrative that broad adoption will lift productivity and margins across the wider economy. ## [China Reportedly Drafts Mandatory C-V2X Standard for Vehicles](https://m.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-10-26/4617416.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

According to unidentified sources cited by National Business Daily, Chinese regulators have begun drafting a mandatory national standard requiring vehicles to be equipped with C-V2X \(cellular vehicle-to-everything\) equipment, and a working group involving dozens of organizations has reportedly been formed. Commercial buses and trucks are already subject to mandatory installation requirements first, with the scope expected to expand to passenger cars in the future. If a mandatory standard is finalized, C-V2X would shift from an optional premium feature to a baseline requirement for cars sold in China, creating a large forced market for onboard units and roadside infrastructure. That would accelerate China&\#x27;s &quot;smart car, smart road, collaborative cloud&quot; approach to intelligent transportation and give domestic C-V2X chip and module suppliers a major boost. Penetration remains low: in the first half of 2026, 344,000 passenger cars in China were delivered with factory-installed C-V2X, up 13.63% year-on-year but representing only a 4.03% fitment rate. The report relies on anonymous sources, and the standard is still at the drafting stage, so the timeline, technical requirements and enforcement date remain unknown.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 26, 08:24

**Background**: C-V2X is a wireless communication technology built on cellular standards, spanning LTE-V2X and 5G NR-V2X, that connects vehicles to other vehicles \(V2V\), roadside infrastructure \(V2I\), pedestrians \(V2P\) and the cloud \(V2N\) via direct PC5 links and cellular Uu links. Unlike cameras and radar, which only sense what is in line of sight, C-V2X lets cars exchange information beyond line of sight, enabling blind-spot collision warnings, accident alerts and smart traffic-light notifications. In China, a mandatory national standard \(强制性国家标准\) is developed through a formal process of project proposal, drafting, public consultation, technical review and approval, and once published it must be complied with rather than adopted voluntarily.
