## [LLM 0.32 adds reasoning traces, server-side tools, and redesigned logs](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/4/new-release-of-llm/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

LLM 0.32, released on August 4, 2026, is the project&\#x27;s most significant update since launch. It adds visible reasoning traces, server-side provider tools such as OpenAI CodeInterpreter and WebSearch, out-of-the-box support for the GPT-5.6 model family, and redesigned content-addressable SQLite logs. This matters because it makes reasoning-model transparency and provider-hosted tools accessible from a simple command-line interface, lowering the barrier for developers and power users. The redesigned logs and one-liner OpenAI-compatible endpoint command also improve everyday workflows for anyone building with LLM APIs. Reasoning traces are written to standard error and can be hidden with -R/--hide-reasoning. The new &\#x27;llm openai endpoint&\#x27; command runs one-off prompts against any OpenAI-compatible endpoint without logging them, while the llm-anthropic plugin adds WebSearch, WebFetch, CodeExecution, and AnthropicMCP tools.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 4, 23:58

**Background**: LLM is a command-line tool and Python library by Simon Willison for running prompts against many language models and storing results in SQLite. Reasoning traces are the internal chain-of-thought text a reasoning model produces before answering, and server-side tools are provider-run capabilities such as web search and code execution that are specified in the API request. The OpenAI Responses API, a developer interface released in early 2025, enables stateful agentic interactions and built-in tools, which LLM 0.32 now leverages.

Shieldstral is a lightweight, open-weight safety classifier in a broader trend toward small, efficient moderation models that aim to provide strong performance at a fraction of the compute cost.

## [JD.com Open-Sources JoyAI-Video-Edit: Real-Time 30fps Video Editing](https://www.aibase.com/news/30123) ⭐️ 8.0/10

JD.com has open-sourced JoyAI-Video-Edit, a 16-billion-parameter autoregressive diffusion model for real-time streaming video editing. The model processes frames causally at 30 frames per second, allowing users to edit live camera streams or uploaded videos with natural-language instructions without waiting for the full video. This is a significant advance in interactive video editing, shifting from traditional &\#x27;edit after footage&\#x27; workflows to a &\#x27;watch-and-edit&\#x27; paradigm. By open-sourcing it, JD.com allows researchers and developers to build on this technology, potentially accelerating the adoption of real-time editing tools across the industry. JoyAI-Video-Edit combines chunk-wise causal generation with autoregressive diffusion, enabling low-latency inference without access to future frames or predefined video duration. It was converted from a strong bidirectional editor trained on progressive image/video generation and editing, and achieves world-class performance in real-time streaming video editing.

aibase · AIbase · Aug 5, 11:16

**Background**: Traditional video editing is a batch process: creators record footage first, then edit offline. Real-time video editing, in contrast, requires low-latency causal generation that can edit frames as they arrive while preserving source fidelity and long-term temporal consistency. JoyAI-Video-Edit, with 16 billion parameters, is an autoregressive diffusion framework specifically designed for this task, and it is now available on GitHub and Hugging Face.

## [Anthropic Bets $10B on Startup Volta, Making Power the New AI Bottleneck](https://www.aibase.com/news/30120) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic has placed a $10 billion power order with Volta, a little-known startup, to secure electricity for its future AI data centers, bypassing Amazon, Microsoft, and Google. The deal signals a major shift in how AI companies source infrastructure. This matters because electricity has replaced capital as the critical constraint in AI infrastructure, and startups that can secure power quickly are now able to compete with entrenched cloud giants. The order could reshape how AI companies plan energy procurement and data center capacity. Volta claims it can compress the typical 5-7 year data center infrastructure development cycle into 12-18 months by securing power access, entitled land, and regulatory approvals in advance. The startup has raised $300 million from investors including a16z, Nvidia, and Michael Dell, and the Anthropic order appears to be a power purchase agreement \(PPA\) for long-term electricity supply.

aibase · AIbase · Aug 5, 10:16

**Background**: Data centers require enormous amounts of electricity to power and cool AI servers. Traditionally, hyperscale cloud providers like Amazon, Microsoft, and Google secured land, power, and capital to build data centers, but now funding is ample while grid interconnection and power availability have become the real bottlenecks. Power Purchase Agreements \(PPAs\) are long-term contracts to buy electricity from a specific generator at a fixed or indexed price, giving data center owners price certainty. Startups like Volta aim to secure power capacity and entitled land in advance, collapsing the development timeline and offering an alternative to the cloud giants.
