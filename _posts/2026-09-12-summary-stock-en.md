## [Saudi Arabia halts East-West pipeline after drone attacks](https://www.ft.com/content/3bafb3ae-ba94-44f0-984c-ec7c9f54a5e0?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Saudi Arabia has shut down the 1,201-km East-West Crude Oil Pipeline \(Petroline\) as a precautionary measure after a series of drone strikes launched from Iraqi territory hit the line on 10-11 September 2026. The shutdown removes Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s main alternative export route at a time when the Strait of Hormuz is already effectively closed. The move eliminates the only large-scale pipeline route that lets Saudi crude reach global markets without passing through the Strait of Hormuz, tightening an already severe supply crunch and adding further momentum to fast-rising oil prices. Saudi Arabia built the East-West Pipeline, also known as the Petroline, precisely so it could keep exporting crude if Hormuz were ever blocked. With Hormuz now effectively closed by Iran and the Petroline shut by attacks, both of Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s main export arteries are simultaneously disrupted, which is why the market reaction has been so sharp.

**Background**: The Reserve Bank of India regulates Non-Banking Financial Companies through a scale-based framework that sorts them into Base, Middle, Upper and Top layers, with the Upper Layer reserved for entities that warrant stricter oversight — the ten largest NBFCs by asset size always sit in this layer. Lindsell Train Investment Trust, the UK-listed closed-ended fund associated with fund manager Nick Train, has launched a tender offer for 20% of its shares. The buyback is explicitly aimed at addressing the trust&\#x27;s persistently wide discount to net asset value and supporting its weak share price. This is a material capital-return action for existing shareholders, who can exit part of their holding at a price closer to asset value rather than the depressed market price. It also puts pressure on other UK investment trusts trading at wide discounts to consider similar shareholder-friendly measures, at a time when the sector&\#x27;s discount problem is a hot governance topic. A tender offer typically lets shareholders sell a set proportion of their shares back to the fund, usually at or near net asset value, within a defined time window. **Background**: An investment trust is a closed-ended fund, mostly found in the UK and Japan, structured as a public limited company whose share count is fixed unless shareholders approve changes. A tender offer is a public proposal to buy shares, usually at a premium or at a set valuation, within a specific timeframe.

## [New QDII Quotas Quickly Exhausted, Nasdaq QDII Funds Tighten Subscriptions](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-09-12/102484358.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 10, 2026, Wanji Nasdaq 100 Index \(QDII\) Fund cut its per-investor subscription limit to 100 yuan and set a 22 billion yuan fund-size cap, refunding any amounts above the cap — just one day after raising the limit to 500 yuan on September 9. Similarly, China Universal&\#x27;s Nasdaq 100 ETF feeder fund raised its subscription limit to 10,000 yuan on September 2, then tightened it twice in two days down to 10 yuan. The rapid exhaustion of newly issued QDII quotas shows that domestic demand for overseas, especially US tech, exposure far outstrips the supply of cross-border investment quota, forcing retail investors into tiny regular-investment plans or into exchange-traded products trading at steep premiums. This directly affects Chinese investors seeking cross-border allocation, and pushes them toward riskier on-exchange buying. Wanji&\#x27;s cap means subscriptions above the 22 billion yuan fund size will be refunded, so even buyers who got orders in may only partially receive shares; the extremely low limits of 10–100 yuan effectively turn these funds into small-scale dollar-cost-averaging vehicles. On-exchange QDII products are trading at notably elevated premiums because the underlying quota scarcity limits arbitrage and creation, so buyers pay well above net asset value.

rss · 财新网 · Sep 12, 15:06

**Background**: QDII \(Qualified Domestic Institutional Investor\) is a scheme that lets approved Chinese institutions invest in overseas markets under a quota granted by regulators, since China&\#x27;s capital account is not fully open. QDII funds are therefore limited by quota: when demand is strong the fund company must either reject or shrink new subscriptions, which pushes buyers into exchange-traded \(on-exchange\) versions of the fund where supply is fixed and prices can rise above net asset value — that gap is called a premium. An ETF feeder fund invests mainly in the corresponding ETF rather than directly holding the underlying stocks, and its subscription limits can be adjusted just as quickly.
