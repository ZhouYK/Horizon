## [Google Told NYC Schools to Accept AI or Lose Search Access, Report Says](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMivgFBVV95cUxNa3FSTjd5UHFyZFR4M0lpcEZBWXBybkFybnRJNk5veVFaQUkyWC1UMDBrbHMyR3JhSjRPS3JzLThJVGYyN2pDOW5JdDlwTFZiVUxJb05BSzZCRmc1MFpXVGtZRjVIUzdqU0hJbnFZcGJBVGdTMDRrc3pzWDA1bnRmRmlZb3h1Ti1nbUZ6WEpvUGNQazBtNEhsM2JGTUJ6aV9EV2hqYmcyU0ttRXhLWnV2Sm16Nnd6d0VFWGY2dzNB?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

According to a report, Google told New York City education officials that schools which opt out of the company&\#x27;s AI tools entirely would also lose access to Google Search, as Google rolled out new controls letting schools limit students&\#x27; use of its AI features. The claim lands amid a broader fight over classroom AI: NYC officials recently published a list of 38 education-technology products they say violate the city&\#x27;s new school AI policy. Conditioning access to a ubiquitous service like Google Search on adoption of a separate AI product is the kind of tying arrangement that antitrust enforcers scrutinize, and it could set a precedent for how AI gets pushed into classrooms nationwide. Because so many U.S. districts already depend on Google&\#x27;s education stack, school technology officers have little practical room to refuse. The reported choice is effectively all-or-nothing: schools can use controls to turn off students&\#x27; access to Google&\#x27;s AI tools, but per the report, opting out of AI altogether also removes Google Search. NYC&\#x27;s own AI policy list notably includes a chatbot the city&\#x27;s Education Department developed itself, showing districts are also restricting their own tools, not just vendors&\#x27;.

google\_news · SILive.com · Nov 7, 20:11

**Background**: Google supplies much of the plumbing of American K-12 education through Chromebooks, Google Workspace for Education and Classroom, so search and AI features arrive bundled into the same accounts schools already use. In antitrust law, &quot;tying&quot; or &quot;bundling&quot; describes selling or supplying one product on condition that the buyer also takes a second product; such arrangements can be illegal when a firm with market power uses them to foreclose rivals. Regulators have recently applied this logic to Big Tech — the European Union charged Microsoft in 2024 over bundling its Teams chat app into Office — making school AI rollouts a likely next front.

A Washington Post report states that men working in AI-related roles are seeing greater career advancement, while women are losing ground in the same areas of the workforce. The article frames this as a widening gender gap in how the benefits of AI adoption are distributed among workers. If AI-driven productivity gains concentrate in roles where men are over-represented or are promoted faster, the technology boom could deepen existing pay and seniority gaps rather than narrow them. This matters for employers setting promotion and reskilling policies, and for policymakers who treat AI adoption as a broad economic opportunity. The available material is limited to the headline and a one-line summary, so specific figures, survey methodology, industries covered, and time frames are not confirmed here. The framing suggests the analysis compares men&\#x27;s and women&\#x27;s career trajectories specifically within AI-exposed or AI-related jobs, not across the whole labour market.

google\_news · The Washington Post · Nov 7, 18:00

**Background**: AI adoption has been reshaping job content across white-collar work: some tasks are automated, new AI-specific roles emerge, and remaining roles demand new skills. Because access to training, stretch assignments, and promotion networks is often uneven, researchers and advocates have long studied whether technological change narrows or widens gender gaps at work. This article applies that question to the current wave of generative AI and workplace automation.

## [ARTEX AI Hacking Framework Found on 1,465 Exposed Servers, Linked to South Korean Bank Breaches](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiuwFBVV95cUxQbUp3YnVNZGpTVS1LTW8xelgwc2pBckdfWV85WmdUc21GYlVWaGdmd2M3YVFReGFWdEJZVU45aHYzTHkyVWFoc1ZVVWlSRnNIdklnVENmRTNNRkg4QnlsNENOME1nU3dPT3lqSy0yblpnNGJxVVAxYTFsRU5Xc25QVW1WMHJlbWRzNS1TcUVTamIzbFhBc2d0ZHpyM3pEVFVDMmhmbExtQkkxczBWR091QkJabjNhdWNBajk00gHAAUFVX3lxTE1uUC1Bc0VqTlpVNmxVVjZOYmcyV2ZYSmtlYVRic2VrT3hoMTVPNzQ0RU9uU1p0YThjMmNXTkpET3p5bU10MUJIR09hZWxUT3Rmb2ZpaGgwRmtoOHhYVGtWQ3VGZ3BOZ2tBZFpOZGJBeEhxRjFlQWY4c0NSQ0IteXhyZlJ2ZFVGUVFZZG13elhKNGY1Z29tU05teHdzNFRITW5IeS1vX3Y3cnk3TzFINm9LdG9OVUZwN2wwN1kyUFJRZA?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Security Affairs published a report on ARTEX, a Chinese open-source AI-powered penetration-testing framework, after researchers traced roughly 1,465 publicly exposed servers running the platform and linked its use to intrusions at seven South Korean financial institutions. Hunt.io attributed the exposed infrastructure and the associated AI-driven hacking tools to the campaign, while Check Point Research separately published operator-side artifacts from ARTEX consoles. The case shows how agentic AI tooling originally built for legitimate security testing can be repurposed by a single operator to run intrusions at scale, lowering the skill and manpower needed to hit multiple financial targets. It also illustrates a broader risk shift: the attackers&\#x27; own infrastructure is now leaking operational details such as which models they used and how they prompted their agents. Of the 1,465 exposed ARTEX instances, researchers found additional threat detections on 155 hosts \(about 10.6%\) and 32 open directories exposing tools, implants and operator files; one Hong Kong server contained a state file showing the operator had run Claude Code version 2.1.221 some 510 times since August 4, 2026, although the software was not communicating with Anthropic. The breaches affected seven South Korean financial firms and exposed data belonging to roughly 67,000 individuals and 2,200 companies, and a separate backdoored Docker image of ARTEX was also flagged in circulation.

google\_news · Security Affairs · Nov 7, 20:03

**Background**: ARTEX is described by researchers as a dual-use framework rather than a malware family: it is a Chinese open-source AI penetration-testing platform that uses large language models to coordinate multiple AI agents which plan tests, run security tools and record evidence of potential vulnerabilities, all through a web dashboard. Because it is freely distributed on GitHub and packaged as a Docker image, anyone can deploy it — for authorized assessments or for real attacks — and misconfigured instances expose the operator&\#x27;s files to the open internet. This incident sits within a wider trend of AI-driven attacks that national CERTs, including India&\#x27;s CERT-In, have begun warning about.
