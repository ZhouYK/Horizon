## [Old Supertanker Prices Overtake Newbuilds as Freight Rates Surge](https://www.ft.com/content/a2c22bca-f50a-440d-82f8-1a373859770d?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

According to the Financial Times, surging freight rates have pushed the market value of older supertankers above the cost of brand-new vessels of the same class. In this inverted market, buyers are no longer pricing ships by age or specification but by how quickly a vessel can be delivered to them, a condition the report describes as the market going &\#x27;bananas&\#x27;. This inversion is a strong signal of tight vessel supply: when secondhand tonnage costs more than a freshly built ship, the market is effectively paying a large premium for immediate earning capacity. It implies significant near-term earnings upside for owners who already control tanker fleets, and it may trigger a wave of new orders that shipyards cannot fill for years. The key variable in pricing has shifted from vessel age and technical condition to delivery speed, since ships available now can immediately capture elevated freight rates while newbuilds take years to arrive from the yard. The available excerpt contains no specific freight-rate or vessel-price figures, and older tonnage typically carries more regulatory and compliance risk than modern ships.

rss · FT Markets · Sep 27, 04:00

**Background**: A supertanker is an exceptionally large tanker, generally defined as a vessel of roughly 275,000 deadweight tonnes or more, used to carry crude oil in bulk. Shipowners normally buy either newbuilds, ordered from shipyards with multi-year lead times, or secondhand vessels that trade at a discount reflecting their age and remaining service life. Freight rates — the price paid to move a cargo — are the main driver of tanker earnings, so when rates spike, demand for immediately available tonnage rises sharply and can flip the normal relationship between used and new ship values.

## [Indonesia Slashes Minimum Stock Price to Lift Liquidity](https://www.ft.com/content/04923b0e-a955-4cfa-bd05-cd828807f61d?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Indonesia will allow shares to trade at a tiny fraction of a US cent, cutting the minimum price increment \(tick size\) for equities in an attempt to revive liquidity in what the FT describes as the world&\#x27;s worst-performing stock market. The change directly alters the market&\#x27;s tick structure, letting listed shares be quoted and traded at far finer price increments than before. Tick size is one of the most basic levers exchanges use to shape trading behaviour, so cutting it can narrow bid-ask spreads, lower trading costs and attract more retail and algorithmic flow into a market that has suffered heavy foreign outflows and weak performance. If it works, Indonesia could become a test case for other emerging markets considering sub-penny-style pricing to revive volume; if it fails, it risks adding noise and fragmentation without fixing underlying demand. The report does not specify the exact new tick size, the effective date or whether the change applies to all stocks or only low-priced ones, and sub-penny pricing remains rare because exchanges typically enforce a minimum one-cent \(or local-currency equivalent\) increment to limit quote flickering, message traffic and order-book fragmentation. A finer tick reduces the cost of crossing the spread but can also shrink the queue priority of limit orders and raise the relative cost of trading for market makers in low-priced names.

rss · FT Markets · Sep 27, 21:00

**Background**: The tick size is the smallest increment by which a security&\#x27;s price can move — in most major markets one US cent or the local equivalent — and it matters because wider ticks force buyers and sellers to transact at worse prices, while narrower ticks allow finer price competition. Market liquidity refers to how quickly and cheaply an asset can be bought or sold without moving its price; thin liquidity means investors must accept steep discounts to exit quickly. Indonesia&\#x27;s benchmark has been a chronic underperformer as global funds pulled out of emerging markets, and because the rupiah is a low-value currency, Indonesian share prices are already quoted in amounts \(such as tens of rupiah\) that translate into a fraction of a US cent, making the tick-size regime unusually consequential for trading costs. Regulators in the US and elsewhere have debated sub-penny pricing for years, with studies examining whether it truly lowers trading costs.

## [China&\#x27;s Top Court Backs &\#x27;Parallel Civil-Criminal Proceedings&\#x27;](https://china.caixin.com/2026-09-27/102489023.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

China&\#x27;s Supreme People&\#x27;s Court has issued the Provisions on Several Issues Concerning the Trial of Civil Dispute Cases Involving Criminal Offenses, adopting &quot;parallel civil-criminal proceedings&quot; \(民刑并行\) as a key principle for cases where civil and criminal matters intersect. The interpretation also clarifies that a contract connected to criminal conduct is not automatically invalid. The interpretation targets the long-standing practice of using criminal filings to interfere with or stall ordinary economic disputes, a problem that has chilled private lending and commercial investment. By tightening property-rights protection in the courts, it is intended to raise business confidence and make commercial litigation outcomes more predictable across all sectors rather than in any single industry. The SPC stated that courts must not wrongly assume that a civil suit cannot be filed merely because criminal conduct is involved, or that an already-accepted civil case must &quot;give way&quot; to a related criminal proceeding; when a civil trial turns up leads of suspected crime or improper criminal prosecution, the court should promptly refer them to the relevant authorities. The validity of contracts connected to criminal offenses has long been a contested issue where civil and criminal adjudication practice diverged.

rss · 财新网 · Sep 27, 22:59

**Background**: &quot;Criminal first, civil later&quot; \(先刑后民\) is not an explicit statutory rule but a handling practice derived from litigation rules: when criminal and civil matters overlap, the civil case is often suspended or dismissed while the criminal case runs its course. Critics argue this contradicts the principle of the unity of the legal order and is easily abused by debtors seeking to escape civil liability. The newly endorsed 民刑并行 model instead requires civil and criminal cases to be accepted, tried and decided separately. A related debate concerns contract validity: under China&\#x27;s Civil Code fraud generally makes a contract voidable rather than automatically void, so a criminal conviction for fraud does not by itself invalidate the underlying agreement.
