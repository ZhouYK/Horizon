## [Treasury Bill Issuance Pressures Short-Term Rates, Fed Path](https://www.ft.com/content/f5ca22c7-8340-4189-8c40-224a1e16c5ef) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Financial Times reports that rising US Treasury bill issuance is pushing up short-term rates, with stress at the long end of the US yield curve appearing to migrate into short-term lending markets. This dynamic threatens to complicate the Federal Reserve&\#x27;s policy path by transmitting long-end yield curve strain into the money markets that set short-term funding costs. This matters because short-term lending markets — including repo and money markets — are the plumbing through which the Fed&\#x27;s policy rate transmits to the broader economy, so upward pressure there can tighten financial conditions even without an explicit rate hike. Investors, traders, and anyone sensitive to funding costs should watch for spillover effects on the Fed&\#x27;s ability to ease or hold policy, especially if bill issuance continues to absorb market liquidity. Treasury bills are short-term government securities sold at a discount and maturing at par, so heavier issuance directly competes for short-term cash and raises yields at the front of the curve. The FT analysis flags the transmission of long-end yield curve stress into short-term lending markets, though it does not specify exact issuance sizes or rate moves.

rss · FT Markets · Oct 13, 04:30

**Background**: Treasury bills \(T-bills\) are short-term US government debt instruments that the Treasury issues to finance operations and manage cash flow, and they are considered among the safest investments because they are backed by the full faith and credit of the government. The yield curve is a chart showing the relationship between interest rates and maturities, and it is a key gauge of market expectations for growth and central bank policy. When it is inverted, short-term yields are higher than long-term ones; the concern here is that stress at the long end of the curve is bleeding into the short-term lending markets that underpin day-to-day funding.

## [Miners outbid fertilizer producers for scarce sulphur, threatening food prices](https://www.ft.com/content/e4ad9ed5-76e0-435c-a46f-6fe53ab1cedf?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 6.0/10

According to the Financial Times, deep-pocketed mining companies are now outbidding fertilizer producers for scarce supplies of sulphur, a raw material both industries depend on. The competition has emerged as a new cross-sector commodity squeeze, with the fertilizer industry losing access to volumes it has historically bought by default. Sulphur is the feedstock for sulphuric acid, which is essential to producing phosphate fertilizers such as DAP and MAP, so higher sulphur costs feed directly into fertilizer prices and ultimately into global food prices. The squeeze adds another inflation pressure point for farmers and food-importing countries at a time when agriculture input costs are already a major political and economic concern. The FT report does not give specific price figures, name the companies involved, or quantify the size of the supply gap, which limits how actionable the story is for traders and buyers. Sulphur supply is largely a by-product of oil refining and natural gas processing, so it cannot be ramped up quickly in response to higher demand, and mining demand has been rising partly because sulphuric acid is used in heap-leaching and hydrometallurgical processing of metals. According to the farmonaut.com overview, USGS data put 2024 US domestic elemental sulphur production at about 8.2 million metric tons.

rss · FT Markets · Oct 13, 04:00

**Background**: Sulphur \(chemical symbol S\) is a naturally occurring element that humans use at scale for three things: as a plant nutrient, as the raw material for sulphuric acid used in mining and industrial chemistry, and in hardening rubber and specialty concrete. In practice most sulphur is recovered as a by-product of oil refining and sour natural gas processing rather than mined directly, which ties its supply to energy-industry output rather than to fertilizer or metals demand. On the agricultural side, sulphur is an essential plant nutrient involved in amino acid and protein formation, and balanced sulphur nutrition also improves nitrogen use efficiency, so farmers apply it either as elemental sulphur or as sulphate-containing fertilizers. In mining, sulphuric acid leaches metals out of ore, making it a core input for copper and other metal production.
