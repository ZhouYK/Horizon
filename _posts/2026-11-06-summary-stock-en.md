## [US Tech Rout Sends 16 QDII Funds Into 20%+ Drawdown as Premiums Soar](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-11-06/102501909.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A broad US tech selloff on November 5, 2026 sent the Nasdaq 100 down 6.4% in a single session, erasing about $2.3 trillion in market value — the largest one-day drop since the March 2020 circuit-breaker crash — while the S&amp;P 500 fell 4.4% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 12%. The Magnificent Seven shed roughly $1.04 trillion combined, Nvidia dropped 9% to wipe out its year-to-date gains, SanDisk fell 22% and Western Digital lost 17%. The scale of the one-day destruction makes this a market-wide repricing rather than a sector rotation, and Chinese investors are feeling it directly: 16 QDII funds are now more than 20% below their recent highs. Because QDII funds trade on exchange with a fixed, quota-limited supply of shares, the crash has simultaneously widened on-exchange premiums, creating arbitrage and liquidity risk on top of the market losses for Chinese cross-border fund holders. The selloff is not a one-day anomaly: the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.65% over October and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 15% that month, as investors grew doubtful about the enormous AI capital spending of the big tech firms. Memory and storage names were hit hardest, with SanDisk down 22% and Western Digital down 17%, well beyond Nvidia&\#x27;s 9% decline.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 6, 14:28

**Background**: QDII \(Qualified Domestic Institutional Investor\) funds are the main channel through which mainland Chinese investors can hold overseas assets such as US stocks, and they operate under a foreign-exchange quota set by regulators. Because the quota caps how many fund shares can be created, the on-exchange price of a popular QDII fund can detach from its net asset value and trade at a premium; normally arbitrageurs would close that gap, but the quota blocks them. A premium therefore tends to balloon exactly when overseas markets fall and domestic buyers rush into the limited supply of shares, which is what has happened here.

The document states that roads, green spaces, rivers, and standalone primary and secondary schools around a plot that are designated as government-built public facilities should be organized and built by the government in advance or concurrently with the project. It remains a draft for public comment issued by a single city, so the timing and final scope of implementation are not yet settled.

**Background**: In Chinese land auctions, local governments set a starting price for a parcel but often add ancillary requirements: the winning developer must build adjacent roads, parks, schools or substations and then hand them over to the city free of charge. These obligations raise the real cost of land and tie up capital, and they became a major complaint for developers as margins compressed after 2021. Guangzhou&\#x27;s draft reverses the arrangement by making the government responsible for such facilities, effectively separating land revenue from infrastructure delivery.
