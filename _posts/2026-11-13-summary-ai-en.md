## [Claude Code Helps Developer Complete 194,000-Line Lean4 Formalization of Ternary Goldbach Conjecture](https://www.aibase.com/news/32317) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Independent developer Charlie Reams reportedly used Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude Code to help produce a 194,000-line Lean4 formalization of the ternary Goldbach conjecture with zero \`sorry\` statements, meaning every step is machine-checked. During the work, the AI reportedly identified and patched 11 errors originating from roughly 13 years ago in the underlying argument. Formalizing a major number-theory result is normally a multi-year, multi-person engineering effort, so a single developer plus an AI agent achieving this scale would be a significant milestone for AI-assisted formal mathematics. If independently verified, it would also give machine-checked certainty to a proof whose refereeing has stretched on for over a decade. A zero-\`sorry\` proof means no gaps were left as placeholders, but the claim has not yet been backed by a link to the proof repository, a build log, or independent checking by third parties, and it comes from a brief secondary news report. The claim that the AI found 11 historical flaws is also unverified and would need to be reproduced or confirmed by the original authors.

aibase · AIbase · Nov 13, 17:01

**Background**: Lean 4 is a free, open-source proof assistant and functional programming language in which mathematical statements and their proofs are written as code that a machine can check; a \`sorry\` keyword marks a step left unproven. The ternary Goldbach conjecture, also called the weak Goldbach conjecture, states that every odd number greater than 5 is the sum of three primes, and Harald Helfgott released a proof in 2013 that has been under review and revision for years. Formalizing such a proof in Lean means translating the entire argument, including its computational verifications, into a form the kernel can verify line by line.

## [Zhejiang University and Lenovo Propose EM²Mem Event-Centric Memory for Multimodal Agents](https://www.aibase.com/news/32316) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Zhejiang University and a Lenovo research team jointly proposed EM²Mem, an event-centric long-term memory framework for multimodal agents, presented at EMNLP 2026. The method inverts the usual pipeline by binding heterogeneous multimodal evidence to event anchors at memory-construction time, so that the agent aligns first and only then retrieves. Long-term memory is a well-known weak point for multimodal agents, which tend to forget information seen only once and struggle with inefficient retrieval over mixed text, image and temporal evidence. By moving alignment earlier in the pipeline, EM²Mem offers a reusable design pattern for agent memory that could affect how multimodal assistants and long-horizon agents are built. According to the paper, each event-indexed memory cell aligns multimodal records, temporal context, graph-linked relations, semantic facts and provenance, enabling compact evidence readout over grounded events. The announcement itself is brief and does not disclose benchmark results, model scale, or code release details.

aibase · AIbase · Nov 13, 16:01

**Background**: Multimodal agents are LLM-driven systems that perceive and act using images, video, audio and text, and they need memory to stay coherent across long interactions. Conventional retrieval-augmented pipelines follow a retrieve-then-align order: first fetch candidate chunks by embedding similarity, then try to reconcile them with the query. That order is fragile when evidence is spread across modalities and time, because retrieval happens before the system understands how the pieces relate to a single happening.

## [ElevenLabs launches Scribe v3 and v3 Live speech-to-text with 102 languages](https://www.aibase.com/news/32303) ⭐️ 7.0/10

ElevenLabs released two new speech-to-text models, Scribe v3 for pre-recorded audio and Scribe v3Live for low-latency real-time transcription, both available to developers through ElevenAPI and ElevenAgents. Scribe v3 covers 102 languages, cuts word error rate by roughly 25% versus the previous generation, and notably improves German recognition accuracy, while both models handle harder audio such as multi-speaker conversations, interruptions and background noise. Transcription quality and latency are the two biggest friction points for teams building voice agents, meeting notes tools and call analytics, so a ~25% WER reduction plus a dedicated real-time model makes ElevenLabs competitive with dedicated ASR providers rather than just a text-to-speech vendor. Wider language coverage also expands the addressable market for developers outside English-speaking regions, such as German-language enterprise deployments. Scribe v3 keeps the Scribe v2 feature set — word-level timestamps, speaker diarization and audio tagging — and adds features such as inferred language hints and control over whether numbers are rendered as digits or words; it is exposed through the same POST /v1/speech-to-text endpoint and became the default in the ElevenLabs app. ElevenLabs&\#x27; model table lists 90+ languages while benchmark results are reported across 102 FLEURS languages, so the headline &\#x27;102 languages&\#x27; refers to the evaluated set.

aibase · AIbase · Nov 13, 11:01

**Background**: ElevenLabs, founded in 2022 and headquartered in London with offices in New York, Warsaw and San Francisco, is best known for AI voice generation but now ships three product lines: ElevenCreative for audio/video creation, ElevenAgents for conversational voice agents, and ElevenAPI which exposes the underlying models directly to developers. Speech-to-text accuracy is typically measured by word error rate \(WER\), the percentage of inserted, deleted or substituted words relative to a reference transcript, so a 25% relative WER reduction is a substantial but incremental gain rather than an absolute accuracy guarantee. Real-time or &\#x27;streaming&\#x27; transcription models differ from batch models in that they emit partial text with very low latency, which is required for live captions and voice agents that must respond mid-conversation.
