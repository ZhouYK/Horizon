## [Nature Paper Uses Language-Guided AI for Multiclass Metamaterial Design](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiX0FVX3lxTFAtUjh0Z0FUajhsRGJKa0ZKbFpMX1h5Z3U2YVZQLUszU2tMMXJ6RVJiVUEyY1ZsZ2o3dGRrclRybVpCRWhvc19KTW16NjNZTHl6UWNleDk0NU1zanpwWE5V?oc=5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Nature paper introduces MetaGen-LLM \(Metamaterial generation with large language model\), a language-guided framework that performs inverse design of high-fidelity metamaterials across eight distinct structural classes. Unlike prior approaches conditioned only on numerical targets and limited to a single structural family, this method lets users specify design goals in natural language and explore across multiple metamaterial families. The work lowers the barrier to metamaterial design by replacing specialized numerical target specifications with natural language, potentially making AI-driven materials discovery accessible to non-experts and enabling cross-family exploration that was previously impractical. It signals a broader trend of large language models becoming interfaces for engineering and scientific design workflows. The framework is evaluated across eight structural classes of metamaterials, with language prompts serving as the conditioning signal for generation rather than purely numerical targets. Details on dataset scale, generation fidelity metrics, and the limits of the language conditioning are described in the full Nature article, which was published online recently.

google\_news · Nature · Nov 5, 08:10

**Background**: Metamaterials are artificially structured materials whose properties come from their geometry rather than their chemistry, enabling unusual behaviors such as negative stiffness or tailored wave propagation. Inverse design flips the traditional workflow: instead of proposing a structure and simulating its properties, you specify the desired properties and let an algorithm find a structure that achieves them. Generative AI models such as large language models can be adapted to this task by treating structural descriptions as a kind of language, which is the core idea behind language-guided design frameworks.

## [Baidu Open-Sources 280B ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base Model](https://www.aibase.com/news/32118) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 5, Baidu open-sourced ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base, a 280B-parameter hybrid-attention MoE model with 14B active parameters pretrained on 33.5T tokens, claiming new state-of-the-art results among same-size models on knowledge, reasoning, math, coding, multilingual, and long-context benchmarks, ahead of DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base and Hunyuan-preview-Base. The release puts a large, efficiently routed MoE model into the open-weight ecosystem, giving researchers and companies a serious alternative to DeepSeek and other Chinese open models for further fine-tuning. Its efficiency-focused design \(only 14B active parameters\) also signals that Chinese labs are competing on serving cost and trainability, not just raw scale. The model uses a hybrid-attention MoE architecture with a 128K-token pretraining context that can be extended to up to 1M tokens for fine-tuning and inference, but it is a text-only base model rather than an instruction-tuned chat model, and the SOTA claims are self-reported without independent benchmark verification.

aibase · AIbase · Nov 5, 16:01

**Background**: Mixture-of-Experts \(MoE\) models contain many more total parameters than they activate for any single token: a routing layer selects a small subset of &quot;experts&quot; per token, so ERNIE 5.5 Flash&\#x27;s 280B total but 14B active parameters means it has large capacity while spending compute and memory bandwidth comparable to a much smaller dense model. Context window refers to how many tokens the model can process at once; longer windows raise compute costs roughly quadratically in standard attention, which is why hybrid-attention designs are used to make 1M-token contexts practical. A &quot;base&quot; model is pretrained only on raw text prediction and must be fine-tuned or instruction-tuned before it is useful as a chat assistant.
