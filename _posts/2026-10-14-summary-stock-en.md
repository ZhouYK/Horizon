## [US September Core CPI Rises to 2.6%, Reinforcing Rate-Hike Expectations](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-15/102493478.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

US September unadjusted core CPI rose to 2.6% year-on-year, the highest since June and above the 2.5% market expectation, while the seasonally adjusted core CPI monthly rate came in at 0.3% versus 0.2% expected. After the release, US short-term interest rate futures fell as traders increased their bets on rate hikes. Inflation data are the single most important input into Federal Reserve policy, so a hotter-than-expected core reading directly shifts market pricing for the path of US interest rates. Rising hike expectations lift short-end Treasury yields, support the dollar and tighten global financial conditions, which in turn pressures risk assets and emerging markets. Core CPI strips out volatile food and energy prices to reveal the underlying inflation trend, and the miss here is small in absolute terms — just 0.1 percentage point on both the annual and monthly measures — but it points in the wrong direction for policymakers. The same news round-up also noted another set of September inflation figures showing CPI up 0.9% year-on-year and 0.2% month-on-month, with PPI beating expectations at 5%, underscoring how differently inflation is behaving across economies.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 14, 22:57

**Background**: The Consumer Price Index \(CPI\) measures how the prices of a basket of goods and services purchased by households change over time, and it is usually reported both year-on-year and month-on-month. Because food and energy prices swing widely for reasons unrelated to underlying demand, economists look at &\#x27;core&\#x27; CPI to judge the true inflation trend, and they use seasonally adjusted figures to strip out regular seasonal patterns. The Fed does not target CPI directly — its 2% goal refers to the PCE index — but CPI still heavily influences expectations because it arrives earlier and moves markets through fed funds futures, which price the probability of rate changes. That is why a 0.1-point surprise can move bond yields, the dollar and equities within minutes.
