## [US Moves on Venezuela and DRC Put Critical Minerals at Center of Geopolitics](https://weekly.caixin.com/2025-12-31/102399236.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On January 3, 2026, the United States launched a surprise operation in Venezuela and announced it had taken control of President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, with plans to send the latter to a New York court. Weeks earlier, on December 4, 2025, the Democratic Republic of the Congo \(DRC\) signed a strategic partnership agreement with Washington, after President Félix Tshisekedi wrote to President Trump in February proposing to trade the country&\#x27;s critical minerals for a US security pact and military support. The two moves together show that energy resources and critical minerals are being treated as instruments of hard power, not just commercial commodities, which raises the risk of supply shocks for oil, copper and cobalt. For downstream industries such as batteries, electric vehicles and power infrastructure, this could mean more volatile prices and greater pressure on countries and companies to secure resources outside normal market channels. Venezuela holds the world&\#x27;s largest proven crude oil reserves, while the DRC is central to global copper and cobalt supply — the TFM copper-cobalt mine in its southeast, shown in the article&\#x27;s photo, is one of the largest such operations in the world. Notably, the DRC-US arrangement is structured as minerals-for-security rather than a conventional commercial mining contract, a sign that resource access is increasingly being negotiated through military and diplomatic guarantees.

**Background**: Critical minerals are raw materials such as cobalt, copper, lithium and rare earths that are essential to batteries, power grids, semiconductors and defense equipment, and demand for them is rising as the global economy shifts toward more energy-intensive and electrified growth. Much of the world&\#x27;s cobalt comes from the DRC, and much of the refining of these materials happens in China, so Western governments increasingly view dependence on a single supplier as a national security problem. Resource nationalism — the tendency of states to assert sovereign control over natural resources and demand local processing or equity stakes — has been spreading across resource-rich countries, reshaping contracts and supply chains. Venezuela&\#x27;s oil wealth and the DRC&\#x27;s mineral belt are therefore strategic prizes, and the United States has also been using trade tools such as the Section 232 national security investigation to justify measures on critical mineral imports.

## [UK 30-Year Gilt Yields Top 6% for First Time Since 1998](https://www.ft.com/content/17a502a2-f8cb-4d79-996e-f2c7018585de?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The yield on UK 30-year gilts rose above 6 per cent for the first time since 1998, according to the Financial Times. The move is drawing retail investors who are hunting for tax-efficient, income-generating assets amid frozen income tax thresholds. The 6 per cent milestone is a major macro signal for UK government borrowing costs, and higher long-dated yields directly affect pension fund funding levels, mortgage pricing and the cost of servicing the national debt. It also marks a shift in retail demand, as ordinary savers increasingly treat gilts as a tax-efficient alternative to cash savings and dividend-paying equities. Bond yields move inversely to prices, so a 6 per cent yield on 30-year gilts reflects sharply discounted bond prices rather than higher coupon payments on newly issued debt. Gilts held directly can be exempt from UK capital gains tax, which is a key part of their appeal to retail buyers, though long-dated bonds carry significant duration risk if yields continue to rise.

rss · FT Markets · Oct 3, 04:00

**Background**: Gilts are bonds issued by the UK government through the Debt Management Office on behalf of HM Treasury to finance the national debt. A bond&\#x27;s yield measures the return an investor earns relative to its price, and it moves in the opposite direction to price: when investors sell bonds and prices fall, yields rise. Rising long-dated yields typically signal investor concerns about inflation, fiscal sustainability or the outlook for interest rates, and because UK income tax thresholds have been frozen, more investors are looking at tax-efficient wrappers and directly held gilts to protect their returns.

## [China&\#x27;s New Offshore Trust Tax Rules: No Immediate Back Taxes for Lei Jun and Peers](https://database.caixin.com/2026-08-02/102470642.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Caixin reported on August 2, 2026 that offshore family trusts established by founders such as Lei Jun, Ma Yun, and Huang Zheng will not face immediate back taxes on the trust-formation stage. However, if these founders reduce their shareholdings in the future, the original cost basis of the assets will not be adjusted upward, meaning capital gains will still be taxed based on the original value. The clarification removes a major source of uncertainty for China&\#x27;s tech founders and their offshore wealth structures, easing fears that a 50 billion yuan tax bill for Lei Jun was imminent. It signals that regulators are deferring rather than eliminating the tax liability on offshore trusts, which affects how founders plan future share sales and how markets price that overhang. Because the tax at the trust-formation stage was not paid, the original value of the transferred property is not adjusted, so when shares are eventually sold, taxable gains are calculated from the original \(often very low\) cost basis rather than a stepped-up value. The policy therefore defers the tax obligation rather than cancelling it, and the eventual bill could be larger because gains accumulate over a longer period.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 3, 08:09

**Background**: An offshore trust is an arrangement in which a wealthy individual transfers assets to a trustee in a low-tax jurisdiction, which manages them for designated beneficiaries; it is widely used by top-tier billionaires for succession planning and asset protection, though it has no strict legal definition. China&\#x27;s revised Individual Income Tax Law, effective January 1, 2019, introduced anti-avoidance rules allowing tax authorities to adjust transactions between related parties that lack a reasonable commercial purpose. Combined with the expected implementation of the OECD&\#x27;s Common Reporting Standard \(CRS\), under which financial institutions automatically exchange account information across more than 100 jurisdictions, many entrepreneurs rushed to set up offshore trusts in 2018 out of fear that gratuitous asset transfers would be treated as taxable property transfers.
