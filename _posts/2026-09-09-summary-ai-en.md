## [OpenAI Claims AI Solution to Navier-Stokes Problem Amid Dispute](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/8/on-navier-stokes/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced that an unreleased internal model produced a proposed resolution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, specifically a counterexample showing a breakdown of smooth solutions, using a swarm of roughly 10,000 AI agents. The announcement was immediately shadowed by accusations of misconduct from NYU mathematics professor Tristan Buckmaster, who alleged that OpenAI had improperly drawn on work he conducted with Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge. If verified, this would be the first AI-produced resolution of one of the Clay Mathematics Institute&\#x27;s seven Millennium Prize Problems and a dramatic demonstration of frontier AI&\#x27;s mathematical capability. However, the result is unverified, and the allegations raise serious concerns about research ethics, secrecy, and the competitive dynamics between major AI laboratories such as OpenAI and Anthropic. OpenAI stated that the agents working on Navier–Stokes sent about 2.7 million messages and used roughly 130 billion output tokens, while the whole project across multiple problems used 4.9 million messages and about 300 billion output tokens; Lean formalization took an additional 17 hours via GPT-6 Astra. OpenAI said it started the effort on September 1 after hearing rumors later tied to Alpöge and Buckmaster, declared it would not claim the $1 million Clay Millennium Prize, and acknowledged that the result still awaits external verification.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 8, 23:55

**Background**: The Navier–Stokes equations are a system of partial differential equations describing the motion of fluids; whether their solutions in three dimensions always stay smooth and globally defined has been an open problem for decades. In 2000, the Clay Mathematics Institute made it one of its seven Millennium Prize Problems, each carrying a $1 million prize for the first correct solution, and as of 2026 only the Poincaré conjecture has been officially solved. OpenAI&\#x27;s claimed counterexample builds on a 2023 method by Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa for finding blow-up phenomena in related fluid equations. External mathematicians and the Clay Institute have not yet verified the result, which is also subject to a priority dispute with Alpöge and Buckmaster.

Open sharing of promising problems has underpinned centuries of scientific progress, so a shift toward secrecy could do serious long-term damage to mathematics and other fields. The warning highlights an emerging tension between AI-accelerated research and traditional open science values. Tao describes the pool of good, fruitful open problems as a non-renewable resource that is now being &\#x27;mined&\#x27; at an accelerating pace. He notes that even a rumor of activity on a problem can trigger a large-scale, AI-powered effort to solve it before the original researcher&\#x27;s project matures.
