## [China Releases National Standard on Automotive Software Quality and Defect Management](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2482016) ⭐️ 6.0/10

China&\#x27;s State Administration for Market Regulation \(Standardization Administration\) recently approved and released the national standard &quot;Specification for Automotive Software Quality and Defect Management.&quot; The standard covers the full software lifecycle — requirements analysis, design and implementation, integration, and verification and validation — requires producers, software suppliers and the supply chain to build quality and safety management systems, implements 10 key quality assurance activities, sets 5 key process review gates, and establishes rules for recalls carried out via over-the-air \(OTA\) updates. It gives China a unified, lifecycle-wide governance framework for intelligent connected vehicle software, shifting quality control from after-the-fact remediation to defect prevention and closing the loop on software defects delivered or fixed through OTA channels. Automakers, tier-one suppliers, software vendors and OTA platform providers selling into the Chinese market will need to align their development processes, review gates and recall procedures with the new requirements. The standard mandates 10 key quality assurance activities, 5 key process review gates, and a software risk assessment mechanism, and it explicitly addresses recalls performed through OTA updates so that defects can be closed out remotely. It is a management/process standard rather than a technical or functional-safety specification, so it complements rather than replaces existing functional safety work such as ISO 26262.

**Background**: Intelligent connected vehicles increasingly rely on software for driving assistance, infotainment, battery management and other functions, and many fixes are now shipped remotely rather than through dealer visits. OTA \(Over-the-Air\) technology lets vehicles download and install update packages wirelessly over a network, which makes it fast and cheap to fix bugs but also raises the risk of pushing faulty software to large fleets at once. In parallel, functional safety standards such as ISO 26262 define how automotive electrical and electronic systems should be designed to avoid unacceptable risk, and China has been steadily building out its regulatory oversight of connected-vehicle software and updates.

## [Trump Rebukes Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei Over Calls to Slow AI](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-14/trump-rejects-calls-for-ai-guardrails-blasts-anthropic-s-amodei) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 14, US President Donald Trump posted on social media attacking Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei for advocating slower development of frontier AI models, saying Amodei was &quot;pretending to be a perfect little angel.&quot; Trump also asserted that AI needs only a &quot;strong and smart&quot; president as its guardrail, rejecting formal AI safety restrictions. The US President publicly attacking a leading AI safety advocate marks a significant turn in the AI regulation debate, signaling that the White House favors rapid deployment over precautionary slowdowns. This stance could shape development norms for frontier labs and influence how other governments and companies justify their own regulatory choices. Trump&\#x27;s remark was a single social-media statement rather than a formal policy announcement, but it aligns with his administration&\#x27;s existing light-touch regulatory posture on AI. Amodei has argued that slowing advanced model development would give researchers time to better understand potential risks, a position reportedly shared by OpenAI&\#x27;s Sam Altman and SpaceX AI&\#x27;s Elon Musk.

**Background**: Frontier AI refers to the most advanced general-purpose models, such as leading large language models, which push the boundary of capability rather than serving a single narrow task. AI guardrails are the safety mechanisms — policies, controls and technical measures — that keep such systems operating within defined boundaries. The debate over whether to prioritize speed or safety has become a central fault line in AI policy, with the US government under Trump favoring deregulation to maintain competitiveness.
