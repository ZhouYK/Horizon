**Background**: Inflation expectations are the rate of inflation that households, businesses and markets anticipate over a future horizon; the Cleveland Fed, for example, publishes a widely watched 10-year expected inflation estimate based on market pricing. ## [China&\#x27;s Offshore Trust Tax Window Closes, Some Wealthy Holders Still Waiting](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-22/102496182.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The 90-day transition period for filing and paying individual income tax on offshore trusts expired on October 22, 2026, after which late-payment surcharges \(滞纳金\) apply to historical unpaid tax filed late. Ye Yongqing, a tax partner at Anli Law Firm, told Caixin that all of his team&\#x27;s affected clients had completed filings and payments, and estimated that most of the first batch of trust holders notified by tax authorities had already filed, with non-filers likely facing funding constraints or disagreeing with how the tax is being assessed. This marks the first time China is systematically enforcing individual income tax on offshore trusts, ending decades in which structures set up in the BVI, Cayman Islands, Hong Kong or Singapore were widely treated as outside the reach of Chinese tax. The enforcement directly affects high-net-worth entrepreneurs, family offices and the advisors who design cross-border holding structures, and signals that tax authorities now intend to use CRS data and domestic enforcement tools against undeclared offshore wealth. Under Announcement No. 21 of 2026 issued by the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration, placing assets into an offshore trust is treated as a deemed transfer taxed at 20% as property transfer income, the trust is looked through annually during its life with a further tax event on termination, and the retroactive period is officially characterized as &quot;in principle three years&quot; starting January 1, 2023. Caixin reports that disputes over the scope of taxation, the size of the assessed amounts and the retroactive pursuit of historical gains are precisely why some holders have chosen to keep waiting rather than file.

**Background**: An offshore trust is a legal arrangement in which a settlor transfers ownership of assets to a trustee established under the law of an offshore jurisdiction — commonly the BVI, Cayman Islands, Hong Kong or Singapore — which holds and manages them for designated beneficiaries. Chinese families have long used structures such as a &quot;BVI company plus offshore trust&quot; to hold shares in operating businesses, aiming for asset protection, succession planning, privacy and tax deferral. Because these trusts sit outside China&\#x27;s domestic Trust Law and their trustees are non-resident, income accumulating inside them was in practice rarely reported to Chinese tax authorities. The 2026 announcement closed that gap, and its enforcement relies heavily on the Common Reporting Standard \(CRS\), the OECD framework under which offshore financial institutions automatically exchange account information with tax authorities in a beneficiary&\#x27;s country of residence.
