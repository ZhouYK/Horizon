## [Wi-Fi 8 Shifts Focus From Speed to Ultra-High Reliability](https://www.xda-developers.com/wi-fi-8-first-wireless-upgrade-years-isnt-chasing-speed-home-networks-need-it/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

IEEE 802.11bn, also known as Wi-Fi 8, is being developed with an emphasis on ultra-high reliability rather than faster peak rates. The standard is expected to be finalized in May 2028 and targets a 25% throughput improvement across signal-to-noise ratios, a 25% reduction in 95th-percentile latency, and a 25% reduction in data-unit loss. This marks a strategic shift for Wi-Fi, acknowledging that real-world performance matters more than theoretical peak speed for most users. If successful, Wi-Fi 8 could significantly improve stability and throughput in dense homes and offices, especially in interference-heavy environments. Wi-Fi 8 keeps Wi-Fi 7-era physical-layer features such as 4096-QAM and 320 MHz channels, and new mechanisms include distributed-tone resource units \(DRUs\), interference-suppression pilots, and improved multi-AP coordination and roaming. Early devices could appear around the time the standard is ratified.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 23, 03:19

**Background**: Wi-Fi 8 is the marketing name for IEEE 802.11bn, following the naming introduced by the Wi-Fi Alliance for earlier generations such as Wi-Fi 6 \(802.11ax\) and Wi-Fi 7 \(802.11be\). Instead of pushing peak data rates higher, the standard focuses on Ultra High Reliability \(UHR\), including techniques to coordinate multiple access points and suppress interference in the unlicensed spectrum. Distributed-tone resource units scatter subcarriers across a wider bandwidth rather than grouping them together, which helps improve robustness.
