European government debt sold off sharply in a broad global bond rout, triggered after US 10-year Treasury borrowing costs climbed to their highest level since 2002. The move dragged eurozone yields higher as investors repriced rate expectations across major markets. Rising sovereign borrowing costs tighten financial conditions across the board, raising funding expenses for governments, banks and companies while pressuring equity valuations and currency markets. Because US Treasuries set the global benchmark for risk-free rates, a sustained rise in their yields transmits quickly to eurozone peripherals and emerging markets alike. rss · FT Markets · Oct 1, 21:45

**Background**: Government bonds are the benchmark assets that price almost everything else in finance, and their yields move inversely to their prices. The US 10-year Treasury yield is the single most watched number in global markets because it anchors mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and equity discount rates worldwide; a multi-decade high there tends to pull European and other sovereign yields up in its wake.

## [France&\#x27;s Surging Borrowing Costs Edge Toward a Debt Spiral](https://www.ft.com/content/e7c1da68-aed3-471d-911f-0b6086a327ec?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

According to the Financial Times, France is facing a sharp surge in borrowing costs that has pushed the country to the edge of a debt spiral, raising fresh concerns across eurozone markets. The report frames this as a moment of reckoning in which fiscal reality is catching up with the country&\#x27;s stretched public finances. France is the eurozone&\#x27;s second-largest economy and its government bonds \(OATs\) serve as a core benchmark asset, so a sustained rise in its yields can spill over into other sovereign debt markets, push up bank funding costs and weaken the euro. If investors begin to question French creditworthiness, the episode could test eurozone financial stability and the credibility of the bloc&\#x27;s fiscal framework. The available excerpt does not include specific yield levels, spread figures or policy responses, so the precise scale of the move and any government or ECB reaction remain unclear. rss · FT Markets · Oct 1, 17:05

**Background**: A debt spiral is a self-reinforcing cycle in which rising debt or deficits force a government to borrow more at ever-higher interest rates, so a growing share of the budget goes to servicing debt rather than to spending or investment, which in turn worsens the fiscal position. Governments fund themselves by issuing sovereign bonds, and when investors demand higher yields to hold those bonds, financing costs rise for the state and often for banks and companies benchmarked against it. France&\#x27;s public debt has been above 110 percent of GDP and its annual deficit has breached the eurozone&\#x27;s 3 percent of GDP limit, while a fragmented parliament since the 2024 snap election has made consolidation politically difficult. Rating agencies have repeatedly flagged these fiscal and political risks, and investors therefore watch the gap between French OAT yields and German Bund yields as a key gauge of eurozone stress.

## [Futu, Tiger Options Insider-Trading Suit Dropped by Market Makers](https://wenews.caixin.com/2026-10-01/102490265.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 28, 2026, U.S. options market makers Susquehanna and Citadel Securities told the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York that they will withdraw their civil insider-trading lawsuit, three months after filing it. The case, which initially involved more than 310 frozen accounts, had already been narrowed to 47 accounts held by 45 traders accused of making over $100 million by buying put options on Futu Holdings \(NASDAQ: FUTU\) and Tiger Brokers \(NASDAQ: TIGR\) ahead of Chinese regulatory news. The withdrawal removes a notable legal overhang from Futu and Tiger, two cross-border brokers whose shares were at the center of the alleged trading scheme. It also leaves the central question — whether regulatory news leaked to traders in advance — unresolved in civil court, which may shift attention to any parallel regulatory or criminal scrutiny. The case was filed in May 2026, when more than 310 accounts were identified as suspected of insider trading; that pool was later narrowed to 47 accounts held by 45 traders, some of whom allegedly positioned themselves as much as two weeks before the news broke. The withdrawal ends only the civil action brought by the two market makers, not any potential separate regulatory or criminal proceedings.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 1, 11:04

**Background**: Options market makers such as Susquehanna and Citadel Securities continuously quote buy and sell prices for options and hedge their resulting positions with stocks, futures or other options, so they are typically the counterparty when traders buy large volumes of put options. A put option gives the buyer the right to sell the underlying asset at a set price, so it profits when the stock falls — making it a natural tool for someone betting on bad news. &quot;Front-running&quot; here means trading on material non-public information ahead of a market-moving announcement, in this case an expected Chinese regulatory crackdown on cross-border brokers such as Futu and Tiger.
