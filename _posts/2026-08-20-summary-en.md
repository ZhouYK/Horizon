## [AI Lifts Homework Scores 18% but Hurts Exam Scores, Chinese Study Finds](https://www.economist.com/graphic-detail/2026/08/18/does-ai-stop-children-from-learning) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A six-month study of 27,000 Chinese students aged 12–18 found that students using AI tools such as Doubao saw homework scores rise by 18% and time per assignment fall from 64 to 45 minutes. However, their exam scores were 20% lower than those of classmates who did not use AI, with the decline concentrated among students who rushed through homework. This is large-scale evidence that AI&\#x27;s impact on learning depends heavily on how it is used, with major implications for schools and edtech policy. It suggests using AI as a shortcut undermines knowledge retention, while using it as a tutor can be neutral or even beneficial, informing debates on AI in classrooms worldwide. The study followed 27,000 students for six months, and about 80% used mainstream AI models like Doubao. Students who used AI as a personal tutor and spent the same time understanding concepts saw no score drop, and a separate university study found chatbot-assisted learners scored higher on tests, with the advantage persisting after a week.

Pricing is based on megapixel-seconds, and the flexible upscale\_factor accepts 1.5x, 2x, or 3x.

## [OpenAI previews private security processing, vows zero data retention for frontier models](https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OpenAI has reaffirmed a Zero Data Retention \(ZDR\) commitment for eligible API customers and previewed a new &\#x27;private security processing&\#x27; mechanism for frontier models. The capability detects potential abuse across related interactions while returning only limited safety signals, so OpenAI personnel never see raw customer content; it is being tested with early customers and will roll out gradually starting in September. This matters because data-handling policies are a major obstacle for enterprises wanting to use frontier models on sensitive or regulated workloads. If the preview delivers on its promises, it could raise the privacy bar for the AI industry and strengthen OpenAI&\#x27;s position as a provider for security- and compliance-focused customers. Under the ZDR pledge, prompts and outputs are not retained after request processing. Customer content is encrypted with customer-controlled keys, so even if content is flagged as abusive, OpenAI staff cannot access the original text; the company also plans to publish a technical whitepaper alongside the rollout.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 20, 02:33

**Background**: Zero data retention means an AI provider does not store, log, or train on prompts and responses after they are processed, a key requirement for enterprises with strict privacy and compliance needs. Private security processing builds on confidential computing concepts, in which data is protected while in use by executing computations inside a hardware-based trusted execution environment \(TEE\), rather than only encrypting data at rest or in transit.

## [Terence Tao Warns AI Could Trigger Mathematics&\#x27; Biggest Crisis Since Gödel](https://the-decoder.com/terence-tao-says-ai-could-trigger-maths-biggest-crisis-since-godel/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Terence Tao, in an article written for the 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians, warns that AI-generated proofs could overwhelm mathematics with unverifiable results. He points to the First-Proof project, where in a second round 10 unpublished research problems were tested by 4 AI systems and 7 were judged acceptable by at least one system, at a cost of tens to hundreds of dollars each. This warning matters because it reframes the AI debate from &\#x27;what AI can do&\#x27; to &\#x27;what mathematics should become.&\#x27; If AI proof generation makes verification and human understanding the bottleneck, it could reshape mathematical publishing, peer review, and how the community treats formal verification. Tao argues that a proof no one can clearly explain should be considered incomplete even if it passes formal verification. He compares the current moment to the foundational crisis of 1900–1930, triggered by Russell&\#x27;s paradox and Gödel&\#x27;s incompleteness theorems.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 20, 13:19

**Background**: The First-Proof project released 10 research-level mathematics questions taken directly from active mathematicians&\#x27; work to test whether current AI systems can autonomously produce rigorous proofs. In its second round, OpenAI submitted proofs for all 10 problems and at least five are believed correct. Formal verification systems such as Lean aim to check every logical step, but Tao&\#x27;s concern is that formal correctness alone does not guarantee that a proof is comprehensible to mathematicians. This touches on a longstanding debate in mathematics about the role of human understanding versus machine-checkable truth.
