## [Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s East-West Pipeline Reportedly Struck in Attack](https://www.ft.com/content/45dce79e-2758-43f2-a06c-3c56db392f39?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s East-West crude export pipeline, the kingdom&\#x27;s main outlet for shipping oil to the Red Sea, was struck in an attack, according to the Financial Times. The report comes amid repeated threats against the infrastructure from Yemen&\#x27;s Houthis and Iran-aligned Iraqi militias, and details on the extent of damage or supply impact were not immediately clear. The East-West line is the only large-scale way for Saudi Arabia to move crude to the Red Sea, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, so a successful strike raises the risk of a genuine supply disruption rather than a purely rhetorical one. Any confirmed damage would tighten global oil markets and lift prices, affecting energy importers, refiners and consumers far beyond the Middle East. The pipeline, known as Petroline, runs roughly 1,200 km from the Abqaiq area in the Eastern Province to the Red Sea port of Yanbu and has a nameplate capacity of about 5 million barrels per day, though actual throughput is typically lower. Even a partial outage would force Saudi Arabia to fall back on Hormuz-dependent loading, which is itself exposed to Houthi and Iranian threats.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 9, 18:19

**Background**: Saudi Arabia&\#x27;s oil fields and export terminals are concentrated in its Eastern Province on the Gulf coast, and most of its crude traditionally leaves through the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow chokepoint between Iran and Oman. To hedge against a Hormuz closure, Riyadh built the East-West pipeline in the 1980s to carry crude across the Arabian peninsula to Yanbu on the Red Sea, and it has since expanded the line and added parallel gas liquids capacity. The Houthis, who have waged a long war against the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen, have previously struck Saudi oil facilities, most notably the 2019 Abqaiq-Khurais attack that briefly knocked out about half of the kingdom&\#x27;s output.

Russia is one of the world&\#x27;s largest crude oil and refined-product exporters, so visible rationing at the pump signals a genuine downstream supply disruption rather than a headline risk, and it could force Moscow to restrict petrol exports or import fuel. Refineries are highly concentrated, capital-intensive facilities, so knocking out even a few distillation units can quickly reduce a region&\#x27;s petrol output, and repairs typically take weeks to months. Rosneft is Russia&\#x27;s largest oil producer, majority-owned by the state holding Rosneftegaz, and operates a large share of the country&\#x27;s filling stations, making its rationing a highly visible barometer of domestic fuel stress.

## [US Extends Section 301 Tariff Exemptions for 178 Chinese Products to January 2027](https://www.caixin.com/2026-11-09/102502862.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On November 6, the US Trade Representative \(USTR\) extended the Section 301 tariff exclusions for 178 Chinese products — which were due to expire on November 9 — by two months, to January 9, 2027, and simultaneously extended the pause on US port fees for Chinese-built ships and on tariffs for Chinese-made ship-to-shore cranes to the same date. China&\#x27;s State Council Tariff Commission responded by announcing that, from 13:01 Beijing time on November 10, it would continue to suspend its 24% retaliatory tariffs on US goods for two months while retaining the 10% rate, with several related measures rolled over for roughly two months as well. The two-month rollover averts the immediate reinstatement of 7.5%–25% Section 301 duties on a broad basket of electronics components and medical supplies, giving affected US importers and Chinese exporters near-term certainty through the year-end shipping and inventory cycle. It also signals that both sides are still implementing the trade understandings reached at the Washington meeting, though the short, repeat-extended nature of the relief means tariff risk remains unresolved rather than permanently removed. The exclusions have now been extended repeatedly in short increments instead of being made permanent, and the scope is defined by specific ten-digit HTSUS tariff codes and product descriptions, so exporters must verify that their own codes remain on the list. On the Chinese side, only the 24% rate is suspended while the 10% surcharge is retained, meaning a residual tariff burden stays in place even during the truce window.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 9, 13:24

**Background**: Section 301 of the US Trade Act of 1974 allows Washington to impose tariffs on imports from countries it finds to be engaged in unfair trade practices, and the office of the US Trade Representative used it from 2018 onward to levy additional duties of 7.5%–25% on thousands of Chinese goods. USTR can grant exclusions for specific products — typically those that cannot be readily sourced outside China — but such exclusions are time-limited and must be renewed. Ship-to-shore cranes are the large gantry cranes that load and unload containers at ports, and they became a separate flashpoint because of both tariffs and US concerns over Chinese-made port equipment. The current round of relief is tied to trade understandings reached at the Washington meeting between the two sides.

The two drivers — reduced geopolitical risk and post-election selling pressure — pushed the benchmark in the same direction. Oil prices feed directly into inflation expectations, energy-sector earnings, and broader macro sentiment, so a 10-week low signals that markets are unwinding part of the geopolitical risk premium baked into crude. A sustained decline would ease cost pressure on consumers and transport-dependent industries while weighing on oil producers and energy stocks. The drop compounds two distinct forces: fading fears of a Middle East supply disruption and positioning shifts after the US midterm results, which together produced the 10-week low. It is worth noting that a verbal signal about intent is not a policy guarantee, so the risk premium could rebuild quickly if tensions with Iran escalate again.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 9, 19:56

**Background**: Brent crude is the main international benchmark for oil prices, reflecting global supply and demand expectations, and traders typically add a &quot;geopolitical risk premium&quot; when conflict threatens production or shipping from major exporters such as Iran. US midterm elections determine control of Congress and shape expectations for energy policy, regulation, and the wider economic agenda, which in turn moves commodity markets. Because oil is priced globally and traded in US dollars, a shift in either geopolitics or US political outlook can ripple through energy stocks, inflation readings, and central bank decision-making.
