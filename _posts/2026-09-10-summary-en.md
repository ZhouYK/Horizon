## [Ant International, Visa, Mastercard Partner on AI Agent Payment Standards](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/ant-international-visa-mastercard-ai-agent-payment-standard.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Ant International announced a partnership with Visa and Mastercard to develop common standards for AI agent payments, built around a &quot;Know Your Agent&quot; \(KYA\) mechanism that links an agent to a valid legal entity, assesses its behavior, and monitors risk. The three parties cited a McKinsey forecast that AI agents could handle $3 trillion to $5 trillion of global consumer commerce transactions by 2030. If AI agents start buying things for people at scale, the lack of a shared way to identify and trust them could fragment payments across incompatible walled gardens; a standard backed by two of the world&\#x27;s largest card networks plus a major Chinese fintech could become the default ruleset for agentic commerce. Merchants, issuers, acquirers and payment service providers would all have to adapt their risk and fraud systems to non-human transacting parties. KYA essentially extends traditional Know Your Customer principles to autonomous digital entities, verifying an agent&\#x27;s identity, capabilities and operational limits; the announcement did not disclose technical specifications, timelines, or whether the standard will be open. It also lands in a field where Google has already proposed its own Agent Payments Protocol \(AP2\) with payments and technology partners, so interoperability between competing standards remains an open question.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 10, 03:00

**Background**: AI agents are software programs that can act autonomously on a user&\#x27;s behalf — browsing, comparing, ordering and increasingly paying — with limited human intervention at each step. Banks have long been required to run Know Your Customer checks to confirm who a human customer really is; KYA applies the same logic to software agents so that merchants, card networks and regulators can tell which real entity an agent represents, what it is authorized to do, and whether its behavior looks fraudulent. As agentic commerce grows, identity and authorization become the main bottleneck, because a payment network must be able to assign liability when something goes wrong.

## [DeepSeek V4.1 Flash: 552B-Parameter Multimodal Model Hits API](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/qg0NU3NNUbp1co2PdkAPAg) ⭐️ 8.0/10

DeepSeek officially released V4.1 Flash, the smallest model in its new architecture family, built on a 552B-parameter Causal-Encoder-Decoder design that activates only 8B parameters on input and 16B on output, with native multimodal visual understanding. The model is now live on the DeepSeek API under the name deepseek-flash, with new pricing taking effect on September 10, 2026, and requests to deepseek-v4-pro being routed to V4.1 Flash after September 14. Independent evaluation places V4.1 Flash as the new \#1 open-weight model on the Vals Index \(57.86%, \#15 of 56 overall\), narrowly ahead of Kimi K3 while costing only $0.30 per test versus $6.47. If those results hold, it signals that highly sparse, multimodal open-weight models can now compete at the frontier on both capability and cost, putting pressure on closed-model pricing and reshaping which models developers default to. Although the model totals 552B parameters, only 8B/16B are activated per token, so inference is cheap and fast but the full weights remain memory-hungry — a temporary test build reportedly processed up to 400 tokens per second. The oddly future-dated September 2026 pricing and rollout schedule is a notable caveat that raises credibility questions pending independent verification.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 10, 05:54

**Background**: Causal-Encoder-Decoder is a hybrid Transformer architecture that combines an encoder for reading input with a causal decoder for generating output, differing from the pure decoder-only designs used by most current LLMs. The 552B-total versus 8B/16B-activated split reflects a Mixture-of-Experts \(MoE\) style sparse design, in which a router selects only a few expert sub-networks per token, decoupling inference cost from total model size. DeepSeek is a Chinese AI lab known for releasing cost-efficient open-weight models, and &\#x27;Flash&\#x27; denotes the lightweight, high-throughput tier of its new architecture series.

## [DeepSeek merges fast, expert, and vision modes, plans V4.1 Flash release](https://www.ithome.com/1/000/602.htm) ⭐️ 7.0/10

DeepSeek announced that its fast, expert, and vision modes have been merged into a single unified intelligent mode, so users no longer have to switch manually: the model judges task complexity on its own and activates visual capabilities when an image is supplied. The company also said it plans to release V4.1 Flash around September 10, 2026, claiming that internal and external tests show it beats V4 Pro on performance, cost, speed, and total time. Collapsing three separate modes into one removes a long-standing usability friction for DeepSeek users and signals that routing between reasoning effort levels is becoming an automated, invisible part of the serving stack rather than a user-facing choice. If a cheaper &\#x27;Flash&\#x27; tier really matches or exceeds the flagship V4 Pro, it would put further pressure on inference pricing across the open-weight model ecosystem, where DeepSeek is already a major cost benchmark. The unified mode is described as automatically detecting input complexity and enabling vision only when an image is present; DeepSeek&\#x27;s experimental multimodal model deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp was already live on its API platform, matching V4 Flash on text tasks while improving on multimodal agent benchmarks, and V4.1 Flash is reported to be a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model with a 552B backbone and support for contexts up to one million tokens. Note that the news item&\#x27;s stated date of around September 10, 2026 conflicts with DeepSeek&\#x27;s own announcement that V4.1 Flash is already live on its API, so the availability timeline should be treated as uncertain.

## [China AI Chipmakers Raise Prices as HBM Shortage Bites](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-ai-chipmakers-raise-prices-high-bandwidth-memory-shortage-bites-2026-09-10/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Reuters reports that Chinese AI chipmakers including Huawei and Cambricon have raised prices because of a global high-bandwidth memory \(HBM\) shortage, with Huawei&\#x27;s Ascend 950DT quoted roughly 20%–50% higher than two months ago and some older Ascend chips up about 30%, while Cambricon&\#x27;s next-generation SiYuan 690 is expected to rise 20%–30%. The price hikes show that memory, not compute logic, has become the binding constraint on China&\#x27;s domestic AI capacity expansion, pushing up the cost of training and serving models for Chinese cloud providers and AI labs that are already cut off from Nvidia&\#x27;s top-end accelerators. It also signals that US export controls on advanced memory are tightening their grip on the entire domestic AI stack rather than just on GPUs. The Ascend 950DT is a training-focused accelerator with 144 GB of HBM and about 2 PFLOPS of FP8 compute, slated to debut in August 2026 and enter cloud use by the fourth quarter, so its higher price directly reflects HBM cost pressure rather than a spec change. The report is based on Reuters&\#x27; supply-chain sourcing rather than official vendor pricing announcements, so the exact magnitude of increases may vary by customer and order volume.

**Background**: HBM is a type of DRAM in which memory dies are stacked vertically and connected through silicon vias, giving AI accelerators far higher bandwidth and lower power consumption than conventional memory; it is packaged directly alongside the GPU or NPU. Huawei&\#x27;s Ascend line and Cambricon&\#x27;s SiYuan 590/690 series are the leading domestic alternatives to Nvidia accelerators, and their performance targets depend heavily on how much HBM they can secure.

## [Tencent Hunyuan Open-Sources AuK Audio Editing Model](https://x.com/TencentHunyuan/status/2097996926876795197) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Tencent Hunyuan announced the release of AuK, an open-source audio editing model that uses natural-language instructions together with a reference audio clip to unify speech generation and editing. Alongside it, Tencent shipped AuK-Flash, a distilled variant that performs 4-step inference and runs roughly 4.5x faster than the base model under matched conditions, with code, model weights, and demos now published. AuK gives the speech and audio AI community an open, immediately usable model that merges text-to-speech generation with instruction-driven audio editing in a single system, rather than forcing developers to stitch together separate TTS, voice-conversion, and separation pipelines. The 4-step Flash variant lowers the inference cost enough to make real-time or high-throughput audio production more practical. According to the technical report, AuK-Flash is produced by distilling the base model with consistency initialization and task-routed Decoupled DMD, and it performs 4-step inference without classifier-free guidance. The tasks span five families in one model: speech generation, content editing, enhancement and separation, paralinguistic editing \(timbre, style, emotion, accent\), and acoustic editing, with ComfyUI integration and ready-to-run inference commands provided in the repo.

**Background**: Zero-shot text-to-speech refers to synthesizing speech in a voice the model has never been explicitly trained on, using only a short reference audio prompt, which typically relies on speaker embeddings and autoregressive or diffusion-based decoders. Audio editing models extend this by taking an existing recording plus a natural-language instruction \(for example, removing an accent or changing emotion\) and regenerating the audio accordingly. Tencent&\#x27;s Hunyuan team is the AI research group behind Tencent&\#x27;s Hunyuan large-model family, and this release follows the broader trend of Chinese labs open-sourcing competitive speech and multimodal models.

## [DeepSeek-AI Releases DeepSelect v1.0.0 TopK Kernel for DSA and Samplers](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSelect) ⭐️ 6.0/10

DeepSeek-AI released DeepSelect v1.0.0, a high-performance TopK kernel library designed for DeepSeek Sparse Attention \(DSA\) and for samplers, published as an open-source GitHub repository. According to the release, the kernels are 2 to 20 times faster than the native torch.topk implementation. TopK selection is a latency-critical primitive in both long-context sparse attention and token sampling, so a 2-20x speedup on this step directly reduces end-to-end LLM inference cost for DeepSeek-V3.2-style models. Because it is released as standalone open source rather than being locked inside a vendor inference pipeline, other projects and frameworks can adopt the kernel independently. DSA, the mechanism the kernel is built for, lowers attention complexity from the naive O\(L²\) to roughly O\(L·k\) with k ≪ L, which makes fast TopK selection over large candidate sets essential. The release note does not specify supported GPU architectures, data types, or integration APIs, and the 2-20x figure is a relative claim that will vary with batch size, sequence length and k.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 10, 07:28

**Background**: TopK is the operation of picking the k largest \(or smallest\) values out of a large array, and on GPUs it is awkward to do efficiently because it involves sorting-like work and irregular memory access; PyTorch offers torch.topk as a general-purpose operator but it is not tuned for the small-k, huge-candidate workloads of LLM inference. DeepSeek Sparse Attention \(DSA\) is a content-driven sparse attention mechanism introduced with DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp that selects only the most relevant key/value entries for each query token instead of attending to the whole context, which both saves compute and makes token selection a hot path. Samplers, which pick the next token from a probability distribution, use TopK in the same way, and DeepSeek&\#x27;s DeepSeek-V3.2 architecture has been trained with this sparse attention, so its inference stack needs a fast TopK primitive.

## [OpenAI Ends $1-a-Year Government Pilot, Moves Federal Agencies to Usage-Based Pricing](https://www.gelonghui.com/live/2662128) ⭐️ 6.0/10

According to a statement from the U.S. General Services Administration \(GSA\), OpenAI is ending the pilot under which government agencies paid $1 per year to use its models and will instead charge federal agencies based on usage, with federal employees receiving a 50% discount off standard pricing. The GSA says roughly 3.5 million federal employees used ChatGPT during the pilot, which it credits with $1.4 billion in savings. The change marks OpenAI&\#x27;s transition from a symbolic, near-free government on-ramp to a full commercial billing relationship with the U.S. federal government, turning AI assistants into a recurring line item in public-sector budgets. Because GSA agreements typically set the template for other agencies and vendors, the move is likely to influence how rival AI providers price their own federal offerings and how agencies plan for AI spending. The pricing shifts from a flat $1-per-year fee to consumption-based billing, so agency costs will now scale with actual adoption and usage rather than remaining fixed and predictable. The 50% federal discount applies to federal staff usage, and the cited $1.4 billion in savings and 3.5 million users come from GSA&\#x27;s own accounting during the pilot, not from an independent audit.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 10, 15:51

**Background**: The GSA is the U.S. federal government&\#x27;s central procurement and shared-services agency, negotiating government-wide contracts that individual agencies can then buy through. The $1-per-year arrangement was a near-free on-ramp intended to accelerate adoption of ChatGPT Enterprise-style tools across federal agencies by removing cost as a barrier. Usage-based pricing mirrors how commercial large language model APIs are normally billed — by tokens or seats consumed — and discounted federal rates are a common feature of government volume contracts.
