The direction of the Fed&\#x27;s balance sheet is a key driver of dollar liquidity, short-term funding rates and bank reserves, so a shift from shrinking toward expanding would be a meaningful signal for rates and risk assets. rss · FT Markets · Nov 23, 05:30

**Background**: A central bank&\#x27;s balance sheet expands when it buys assets, such as Treasury securities, and creates reserves in the banking system; it shrinks, or engages in quantitative tightening, when it lets those holdings run off or sells them. The Federal Reserve operates under an &quot;ample reserves&quot; framework in which banks hold large volumes of reserves at the central bank, and short-term funding markets such as repo can become strained when reserves become scarce relative to demand. Because reserves are affected by factors the Fed does not fully control, such as the size of the Treasury&\#x27;s cash balance and demand for currency, the Fed sometimes needs to buy assets for purely technical reasons to keep money markets functioning smoothly, even while its broader policy direction is unchanged.

Ukraine confirmed on November 23, 2026 that its sea drones struck a Russian warship docked at the Libyan port of Benghazi in the early hours of November 22, sinking it after a large explosion, and hours later hit a second Russian Navy dry-cargo vessel identified as the &quot;Angara.&quot; All crew members were rescued and no fatalities were reported, and Libya&\#x27;s eastern government acknowledged the incident in an official statement. This is the first confirmed Ukrainian strike on Russian naval assets in North Africa, marking a clear geographic spillover of the Russia-Ukraine war beyond the Black Sea and Baltic into the Mediterranean. It raises escalation risk for Mediterranean shipping routes, war-risk insurance and freight rates, and energy supply chains moving through North African ports. The strikes were carried out with one-way attack sea drones, with Ukrainian security services releasing video footage of the attack on the &quot;Angara&quot;; the article also notes that a Russian cargo ship was attacked earlier near the Gulf of Finland in the eastern Baltic, suggesting multiple maritime flashpoints. No casualty or damage cost figures were disclosed, so the direct market impact remains limited for now.

## [World&\#x27;s first offshore RMB bond ETF debuts in Hong Kong](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-11-23/102507986.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On November 23, ChinaAMC \(Hong Kong\) listed the world&\#x27;s first offshore RMB bond ETF on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, tracking the Bloomberg Offshore RMB Index and investing in globally issued investment-grade offshore RMB bonds. The ETF has HKD, USD and RMB counters, with an initial issue price of RMB1,000 per unit. The listing gives global investors an exchange-traded, low-threshold vehicle for offshore RMB fixed income and marks another step in RMB internationalization. It coincides with 2026 offshore RMB bond issuance surpassing Chinese USD bond issuance for the first time, signaling a deepening offshore RMB financing and investment ecosystem. The ETF trades under 03551.HK for the HKD counter, 41551.HK for the USD counter and 83551.HK for the RMB counter, with a board lot of one unit and an initial HKD counter price of about HK$1,174.1. Because it tracks an investment-grade offshore RMB bond index, it excludes high-yield dim-sum bonds, and the secondary-market liquidity of a newly listed ETF remains to be tested.

**Background**: Dim-sum bonds, also known as offshore RMB bonds, are RMB-denominated bonds issued outside mainland China and settled in RMB, with coupons and principal paid in the currency. They are a key building block of the offshore RMB market, alongside offshore RMB deposits and trade settlement. Chinese USD bonds, by contrast, are USD-denominated debt issued offshore by Chinese entities. An ETF listed in Hong Kong pools such bonds into a single exchange-traded fund, and a multi-counter structure lets investors buy and settle the same fund in HKD, USD or RMB.
