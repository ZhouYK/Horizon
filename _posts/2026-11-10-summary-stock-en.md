## [Treasuries slide as hot October CPI fuels bets on Fed rate rise](https://www.ft.com/content/8b564134-dea6-4272-8ede-e0b12ef5aebd?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

October US consumer price inflation came in hotter than economists had forecast, triggering a sharp sell-off in US government debt. Two-year Treasury yields posted their biggest one-day jump since 2024 as traders raised their bets that the Federal Reserve will need to raise interest rates rather than cut them. The two-year yield is a key anchor for short-term borrowing costs and for pricing across mortgages, corporate credit and the dollar, so a jump of this size quickly feeds into broader financial conditions. If traders now see the Fed&\#x27;s next move as a hike rather than a cut, that reverses a major pillar of the rate-cut narrative that has supported risk assets. The move was concentrated at the short end of the curve, where yields are most sensitive to near-term Fed policy expectations, rather than being a uniform sell-off across maturities. Because bond prices move inversely to yields, existing lower-coupon Treasuries had to reprice at a discount to offer returns competitive with the new higher market rate.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 10, 18:53

**Background**: US Treasuries are government bonds whose fixed coupon payments mean their market price falls when the yield investors demand rises. The two-year note is watched closely because its yield largely reflects what markets expect the Fed to do with its policy rate over the next couple of years, while the consumer price index \(CPI\) is the headline gauge of inflation that the Fed weighs when setting that rate. Traders express these expectations through interest-rate futures, such as 30-day fed funds futures, whose implied probabilities show how the market is positioning ahead of each Fed meeting.
