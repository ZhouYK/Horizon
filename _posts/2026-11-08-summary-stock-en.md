## [PBOC Shifts Policy Rate Anchor to Overnight Reverse Repo](https://weekly.caixin.com/2026-11-07/102502087.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On October 30, 2026, the People&\#x27;s Bank of China announced that from November 2 it will conduct overnight reverse repo auctions every working day using a fixed-rate, quantity-tender format, making the overnight reverse repo rate the primary policy rate instead of the 7-day reverse repo rate. On the first day of the new framework, the PBOC injected 541 billion yuan via overnight reverse repo at an operation rate of 1.25%, fully satisfying primary dealers&\#x27; demand. This is one of the most significant changes to China&\#x27;s interest rate framework in years: by anchoring policy to the overnight rate, the PBOC aims to create a clearer, more unified short-end pricing benchmark that transmits more reliably along the yield curve. The shift affects how banks, bond investors and offshore institutions price short-term funding, and changes the signal the central bank sends about its monetary policy stance. Under the new arrangement, the 7-day reverse repo becomes a fixed-quantity, rate-tender, multiple-price auction used mainly around tax payment periods and month-end, losing its single-price policy signal. Other tools that previously added or subtracted spreads to the 7-day rate — including temporary overnight reverse repos and the repo facility for foreign central bank-type institutions — are now benchmarked off the overnight rate with unchanged spread widths.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 8, 22:48

**Background**: A policy rate is the anchor the central bank uses to signal its monetary stance and guide market pricing; in China the 7-day reverse repo rate had played this role since 2024. The PBOC has spent recent years streamlining its toolkit — introducing temporary overnight reverse repos in July 2024 and removing the medium-term lending facility \(MLF\) from its policy-rate role in March 2025 by switching MLF to multiple-price tenders. Overnight reverse repo operations are now the main channel for injecting or draining very short-term liquidity.
