## [Quad9 refuses French piracy DNS blocks, faces €580K daily fines](https://torrentfreak.com/dns-resolver-quad9-rejects-french-piracy-blocks-weighs-exit-as-bein-seeks-up-to-e580k-a-day/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Quad9, a Swiss nonprofit DNS resolver, is refusing to comply with a French court order obtained by beIN Sports that would require it to block 58 piracy-related sports streaming domains. A Paris court hearing was held last Thursday, and a ruling is expected within three weeks; beIN is seeking €10,000 per domain per day, or up to €580,000 per day in fines. This case underscores the growing tension between public DNS resolvers, user privacy, and state-mandated censorship, and it could set a precedent for how governments force global DNS providers to enforce national blocking orders. If Quad9 exits France or is fined, French users may lose a privacy-focused DNS option, while other resolvers could face similar demands. Quad9 says it does not collect user data and therefore cannot geofence the blocks to only French users; its only realistic options are a global block or exiting the French market. It also calls France’s July law allowing real-time automatic blacklisting of domains “reckless and dangerous.”

**Background**: DNS is the internet’s naming system that translates human-readable domain names into IP addresses so browsers can reach websites. DNS blocking is a technique that makes specific domains unreachable, often by returning an error or a different address, and it is used both for security filtering and for censorship. Quad9 is a free, nonprofit public DNS recursive service that emphasizes security and privacy, and it normally blocks known malicious domains using threat intelligence. France has increasingly required internet intermediaries to block piracy-related sites, and rights holders like beIN Sports are now trying to extend those orders to DNS resolvers.

## [2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine Awarded for Optogenetics](https://www.nobelprize.org/all-nobel-prizes-2026/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for the discovery of light-controlled ion channels and the founding of optogenetics. The technique allows researchers to switch individual nerve cells on or off inside a living brain, and it is now used in neuroscience laboratories around the world. Optogenetics turned neuroscience from a largely observational discipline into an experimental one, letting researchers causally test which neurons drive a given behavior rather than merely correlating activity with it. Its influence now extends beyond basic brain research into fields such as vision restoration, psychiatry and the design of new neuromodulation therapies. The key molecules are channelrhodopsins, light-gated ion channels that Hegemann and Nagel identified in single-celled algae and showed in 2003 could make cultured cells fire electrical signals when illuminated; Deisseroth then engineered them into a reliable optical switch for neurons. Because the proteins must be delivered by genetic means, the approach still depends on gene transfer, and light delivery in deep or large brains remains a technical constraint.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 5, 09:33

**Background**: Neurons communicate through electrical signals, but until recently there was no way to control a chosen cell type in an intact brain with millisecond precision. Channelrhodopsins are naturally occurring proteins in algae that open and let ions flow when struck by light, thereby changing the cell&\#x27;s electrical state. Optogenetics combines this with genetic targeting so that only specific neurons express the protein, meaning a pulse of laser light can activate or silence just those cells.

## [OpenAI to Add Invisible Watermarks to AI Text in the EU](https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OpenAI announced that over the coming weeks it will embed machine-detectable invisible watermarks in qualifying ChatGPT and Codex text outputs for users in the European Union, in order to comply with the EU AI Act&\#x27;s content transparency requirements. API customers can optionally enable watermarking for certain models, though it is off by default, and OpenAI is also opening applications for researchers and professional institutions to use its text watermark detector. This is one of the most concrete compliance moves by a major AI lab on AI content provenance, and it could set a de facto standard that other model providers follow in the EU and beyond. It directly affects EU ChatGPT users and developers building on OpenAI&\#x27;s API, and it signals that regulation is starting to shape how generative models mark their output. The watermark is described as invisible and machine-readable, so it should not alter the visible text a user reads, and API-side watermarking is opt-in rather than default, meaning most third-party applications will not carry it unless developers choose to enable it. Access to the detection tool is gated behind an application process for researchers and institutions, which means public verification of the mark is not broadly available yet.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 5, 15:25

**Background**: AI text watermarking typically works by introducing a statistical or character-level pattern during generation — for example, subtly biasing word choices — so that a matching detector can later estimate whether the text came from the model without changing how the text reads. Content provenance is a broader idea: an inspectable record of where a piece of content came from and how it was transformed, which regulators want in order to fight disinformation and misattribution. The EU AI Act imposes transparency obligations on general-purpose AI systems, one reason OpenAI and others are now rolling out marking and detection mechanisms.

## [OpenAI Boosts Default Speed for GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol by ~50%](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2107158998495748264) ⭐️ 6.0/10

OpenAI&\#x27;s Tibo \(Thibault Sottiaux\) said the default inference speed for the GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol models has been raised by roughly 50%, going from 30 TPS to 50 TPS across all products and for partners using Sign in With ChatGPT, with no user action required and the change becoming noticeable within about two hours. The same models also now use an improved tokenizer that cuts down the number of tokens needed to complete a task. A ~50% jump in default decode speed affects every user and every application built on these models, since faster token generation directly lowers latency and improves the felt responsiveness of chat, coding and agent workloads. The tokenizer improvement compounds this by reducing token consumption per task, which can translate into lower API cost and more usable context for developers. The claim comes from a short social-media post relayed secondhand, with no published benchmarks, methodology or evaluation details, and the model names appear forward-dated, so the numbers should be treated as unverified. TPS here refers to tokens generated per second during the decode phase, a measure of output speed rather than prefill or prompt-processing throughput.

**Background**: TPS \(tokens per second\) is a common way to express LLM inference speed: the model generates text autoregressively one token at a time, so a higher TPS means responses appear faster, but the metric only covers the generation step and excludes prompt processing. A tokenizer is the component that converts raw text into the discrete tokens the model reads, so a more efficient tokenizer packs the same meaning into fewer tokens, effectively reducing both cost and generation time. &quot;Sign in With ChatGPT&quot; is OpenAI&\#x27;s mechanism that lets third-party apps and services authenticate users and access ChatGPT capabilities on their behalf, which is why the speed change extends to partners as well.
