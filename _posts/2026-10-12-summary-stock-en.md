## [China Cuts NEV Export VAT Rebate from 13% to 9%](https://www.caixin.com/2026-10-12/102492637.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On October 12, 2026, China&\#x27;s Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration announced that the VAT export rebate rate for new energy vehicles will be cut from 13% to 9%, effective April 1, 2027. The adjustment applies only to new energy vehicles, while fuel-powered vehicles are not affected for now. The 4-percentage-point cut directly erodes export margins for Chinese NEV makers such as BYD, Tesla&\#x27;s China operations, NIO and Geely, and signals a broader taper of the tax and subsidy incentives that have fueled China&\#x27;s EV export boom. Industry speculation that the rebate could be eliminated entirely within two to three years adds medium-term earnings uncertainty for the entire high-growth export sector. China&\#x27;s export rebate rates currently fall into four tiers — 13%, 9%, 6% and 0% — so the change moves NEVs down a tier, and the roughly 18-month lead time before the policy takes effect softens its immediate price impact. The pace of any further reduction has not been announced, and the policy leaves fuel-vehicle exports untouched, effectively narrowing the relative tax advantage of electric models.

**Background**: Export rebates are a long-standing Chinese trade policy, in place since 1985, under which tax authorities refund the VAT and consumption tax that exporters already paid domestically, so goods can enter foreign markets at untaxed prices and avoid double taxation under cross-border trade rules. Manufacturing enterprises typically operate under the &quot;exempt, offset, refund&quot; method, while trading firms use the &quot;exempt and refund&quot; method calculated on purchase price. The rebate rate is not fixed but adjusted dynamically by the state, and it has become a key lever in China&\#x27;s broader wind-down of the tax breaks and subsidies that incubate the electric-vehicle industry.

## [UK High Court: Qian Zhimin Bitcoin Claims Governed by Chinese Law](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-12/102492610.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On October 12, the UK High Court ruled that the property-rights claims of Chinese investors over more than 61,000 bitcoins tied to the Tianjin Lantian Gerui illegal fundraising case must be decided under Chinese law rather than English law, rejecting the victims&\#x27; argument that English law should apply. As a result, roughly 16,000 Chinese victims who joined the UK recovery proceedings may only recover their principal plus interest, rather than sharing in bitcoin&\#x27;s enormous price appreciation. The decision is an important precedent for cross-border crypto asset recovery, because it determines whether victims of a collapsed scheme are treated as owners of the traceable cryptocurrency or merely as creditors limited to their original losses. It could shape how future seized-crypto cases are resolved in the UK and influence whether victims of similar overseas schemes can capture appreciation in assets recovered on their behalf. According to Caixin, Qian Zhimin bought bitcoin through the Huobi exchange in 2014 at an average price of about 2,815 yuan, while as of October 12, 2026 bitcoin was still trading at roughly 560,000 yuan — about 200 times that purchase price. The ruling addresses the choice-of-law question rather than the final distribution of the assets, so the victims&\#x27; recovery outcome is not yet fully settled.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 12, 13:37

**Background**: Tianjin Lantian Gerui was an illegal fundraising scheme whose operator, Qian Zhimin, used investor money to buy large amounts of bitcoin before fleeing abroad; UK authorities later seized more than 61,000 bitcoins linked to the case, one of the largest cryptocurrency seizures ever made in the country. The dispute turns on choice of law: under Chinese law, the proceeds of illegal fundraising are typically handled as restitution of principal plus interest, whereas English law&\#x27;s tracing and trust doctrines can give claimants a beneficial interest in the specific assets and their appreciation. Huobi, mentioned in the case, was a major Chinese-founded cryptocurrency exchange where Qian made the original purchases in 2014.
