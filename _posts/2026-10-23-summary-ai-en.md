## [LSEG Spends $3.5 Billion on Data and AI Capabilities for Wall Street](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiqAFBVV95cUxOVVFEYTJBeDJrREtRZW5PbHdJOWh3dkJ1dUkxV29zYTNXbEJrWGtNMXVlSG9WbjIyYnAwOWxIenBXVkV5RWNzVVNNdFBsNTR1TkNOTGM0X1NndURzMjRGenMxOXRQaUZoZkNEdElDZ2JfcEloU0xYYVJpX0NCVVJmYV9QQjdBdjl1SEJyNG9la2xOa1B5bkpNbHhITVphc0tJSmFhOExiNjc?oc=5) ⭐️ 7.0/10

London Stock Exchange Group \(LSEG\) is spending $3.5 billion on assets and data capabilities aimed at supplying Wall Street&\#x27;s fast-growing demand for AI, according to a PYMNTS.com report. The move positions the exchange-and-data group as a supplier of the underlying information that banks and asset managers feed into their AI models and tools. The deal underscores how financial data has become core infrastructure for AI in finance: banks need licensed, high-quality, machine-readable data before they can build research assistants, trading signals, or compliance automation. A multi-billion-dollar commitment from one of the largest market-data vendors signals that data ownership and AI-ready delivery are becoming the main competitive battleground among exchanges and data providers. The report is based on a headline-level summary, so the specific breakdown of the $3.5 billion — how much goes to acquisitions versus organic data and platform build-out — has not been detailed in the available material. What is clear is the strategic direction: packaging and licensing financial data so that it can be consumed directly by AI systems, not just read by humans on a terminal.

google\_news · PYMNTS.com · Oct 23, 17:52

**Background**: LSEG is the parent of the London Stock Exchange and, since its roughly $27 billion acquisition of Refinitiv in 2021, one of the world&\#x27;s largest providers of financial market data, indices \(FTSE Russell\) and trading infrastructure. Market-data vendors like LSEG, Bloomberg and S&amp;P Global sell licensed feeds that banks, funds and fintechs use for pricing, risk models and regulatory reporting. As generative AI spread through finance, demand shifted toward bulk, machine-readable, rights-cleared data suitable for training and grounding models — which is why data vendors are now investing heavily in AI-oriented products and infrastructure.

## [Leaked Ransomware Infrastructure Shows Affiliates Using AI Agents for Intrusions](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMifkFVX3lxTE9RdS16cTVMVVc1bWUweEI5OHJ5aDUwUFhnMG4xMW0xUHNEYTVQcjYwdWZQNERYWG03YkVuNXhva2pYY1dtcl9fczZDYi1XTl9VcmJQU0F5cXJ2bzEyYTNrbWJEeFVVWlMwQjFjM0YtWURDM0VZdG9JbXViZ1ZKdw?oc=5) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Hacker News reports that exposed infrastructure tied to ransomware operations has revealed how affiliated operators used AI agents to carry out parts of the intrusion process. The finding comes from threat-intelligence analysis of that exposed backend rather than from a single victim incident, suggesting AI-assisted intrusion work is now visible in real criminal tooling. If AI agents are handling hands-on steps of an intrusion rather than just drafting phishing lures, affiliates can operate at machine speed and at lower skill cost, shrinking the window defenders have to detect and respond. It also means blue teams must hunt for agentic, high-tempo behaviors across identity, cloud, and endpoint telemetry, not just familiar malware signatures. The reporting is based on analysis of exposed or leaked operator infrastructure, which typically includes command-and-control panels, credential stores and affiliate records, so the evidence is indirect and attribution may be uncertain. It also remains unclear how autonomous the agents were — whether they ran unsupervised or merely assisted a human operator — which is a key distinction for assessing real-world risk.

google\_news · The Hacker News · Oct 23, 17:50

**Background**: Ransomware is largely organized as Ransomware-as-a-Service \(RaaS\): a core operator develops the encryptor and leak site, while affiliates break into victim networks, escalate privileges and deploy the payload in exchange for a share of the ransom. Affiliates historically relied on phishing, stolen credentials and hands-on-keyboard work, so automating those steps with AI agents would remove a major bottleneck. &\#x27;Exposed infrastructure&\#x27; means servers, panels or leaked data belonging to these crews that researchers can inspect to reconstruct their tools and workflows.

## [CISA and Allied Agencies Warn of &\#x27;Denial-of-Wallet&\#x27; Attacks on AI Token Billing](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMijAFBVV95cUxOS2cwOXBfeEtKaEMwVFlybk9KaFlMb05JVXMxYU5hc2NYUmZOTGkwR0RjMkFqVVdpQm1sdFA2dExpRkFiSUYtdEFtRXhwQUJ0bG96QmQ5bUlnX0xZNi14SEJ6ZXphVDB4anpQVDQ0ZDZld0h0aG43cHcwVFl0eHdMb09kcG9iZ2hualV0NA?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

CISA, together with international partner agencies, issued joint guidance warning that attackers can exploit token-based billing for large language model services to run up victims&\#x27; costs, a pattern the guidance labels &\#x27;denial-of-wallet.&\#x27; The advisory reframes this as a distinct financial attack class rather than a conventional availability outage. Any organization exposing a publicly reachable, pay-per-token LLM endpoint now has a financial attack surface in addition to a technical one, and the costs can escalate far faster than traditional incident response can react. Official guidance from CISA and allies signals that regulators and insurers will increasingly expect cost-abuse controls to be part of baseline AI security posture. A denial-of-wallet attack succeeds precisely by keeping the service up and busy: the bill, not the outage, is the payload, so conventional uptime monitoring and DDoS defenses may never trigger. Because a single looping agent or scripted client can consume tokens unpredictably, defenses generally need per-user or per-key budgets, aggressive rate limits, output-length caps, and anomaly alerts on spend rather than only on latency or error rates.

google\_news · ExecutiveGov · Oct 23, 21:15

**Background**: Large language models process text as tokens — small chunks of characters or words — and most commercial AI APIs charge per token consumed for both input and output. That pay-per-request pricing model means an adversary who can send requests can directly cause the victim to spend money, without needing to break encryption or crash anything. Traditional denial-of-service attacks aim to exhaust server capacity and take a service offline; denial-of-wallet attacks invert that logic by exhausting a budget while the service keeps working normally.

## [Anthropic launches Claude Duet, a proactive AI agent app to rival Meta Muse and OpenAI Dots](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiuwFBVV95cUxNaWxGVHJ5dVhwMXhhQlBPTnZnUFpjQ01FLWswc0pzS1o3cFNONlJ5Y3c4bHdBa0NaajFkRzVfT29oNVI3MlhLanJ6TmFmajJ2X0NZSmwtMURFWnB3VG5wdzlENGdzN0k4XzUwLTVXSFpPY3c4QlFGT01rSU1XNW5VSmdseUpvdE9iYURGTE1kTExEQlFNQlZRbkp0MVZhLVZEbFdDTXFGQm5QY2NrLXBaNTNaemtZbkJJWkFB?oc=5) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Anthropic announced Claude Duet on Wednesday, a new standalone app in which Claude operates as a &quot;proactive agent&quot; that can run tasks in the background rather than waiting for one-off prompts. It opened as a research preview with a waitlist for Pro and Max subscribers in the United States, and ships as dedicated Android and iOS apps powered by Anthropic&\#x27;s Opus 5.5 model. The launch places Anthropic directly in the emerging personal-agent race alongside Meta&\#x27;s Muse and OpenAI&\#x27;s Dots, shifting the competitive focus from chat quality to agents that autonomously carry out long-running, real-world tasks. For subscribers, it signals that AI assistants are becoming persistent background workers tied into apps and services rather than single-turn chatbots. Duet is initially limited to a US research preview with waitlist access for Pro and Max tiers, so broad availability is not yet guaranteed. It runs on Opus 5.5 and is delivered through native Android and iOS apps, positioning it as a consumer-facing product rather than an API-only capability.

google\_news · Computerworld · Oct 23, 21:29

**Background**: A &quot;proactive agent&quot; differs from a conventional chatbot: instead of answering a single query, it takes a goal and works on it over time, running in the background and often connecting to other apps and services. Meta&\#x27;s Muse, announced in September 2026, is described as a personal AI agent for long-running tasks and is available on iOS, Android and the web, while OpenAI&\#x27;s Dots are always-on agents reportedly powered by GPT-6 Astra and connected to thousands of apps. Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude is its family of large language models, and Opus is the most capable tier; Duet packages that model into an agent-style consumer app.
