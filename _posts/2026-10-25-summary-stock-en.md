## [PBOC Drafts Rules to Tighten Interbank Bond Issuance, Curb Fee Rebates](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-25/102497195.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On October 23, 2026, the People&\#x27;s Bank of China published a draft of the &quot;Interbank Bond Market Bond Issuance Management Rules&quot; for public comment, setting out conduct standards for issuers, underwriters and investors across the entire issuance process. Feedback is due by November 15, 2026. The draft elevates interbank bond issuance oversight from self-regulatory rules set by the dealers association to a formal PBOC administrative regulation, creating a closed loop linking frontline monitoring by local PBOC branches, association self-discipline, administrative supervision and unified enforcement. That shift could materially change how issuers, underwriters and investors price and participate in China&\#x27;s largest bond market. The rules target the long-standing &quot;structured issuance plus fee rebates&quot; practice, distorted issuance pricing, manual interference in bookbuilding and low-price underwriting, and are built around &quot;full-chain penetration and closed-loop management&quot; of the issuance process. The draft does not yet spell out the specific penalties, so how the measures will be enforced remains a focus for the market.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 25, 22:59

**Background**: China&\#x27;s interbank market is the country&\#x27;s largest bond market, and issuance of non-financial corporate debt financing instruments there has long been governed mainly by self-regulatory rules of the National Association of Financial Market Institutional Investors \(NAFMII, known in Chinese as the dealers association\). &quot;Structured issuance&quot; refers to issuers arranging affiliates or wealth-management products to subscribe to their own bonds, often compensating holders with fee rebates \(返费\) — a practice that fakes demand and distorts pricing. Bookbuilding \(簿记建档\) is the process of collecting investor bids to set the coupon, while low-price underwriting means underwriters quoting below cost to win mandates; NAFMII has repeatedly investigated and penalized such conduct, including a high-profile case where underwriting fees fell as low as 700 yuan.

## [Black Sea Overtakes Gulf as World&\#x27;s Deadliest Shipping Route](https://www.ft.com/content/8c874f60-0041-476d-9c53-8524c374443f?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 6.0/10

The Financial Times reports that more commercial sailors died in the Black Sea this year than in any other body of water, with fatalities there now outstripping those in the Gulf, which has drawn far more international attention. Crews transiting the route are routinely exposed to drone and missile strikes linked to the Russia-Ukraine war. The shift makes the Black Sea the single riskiest corridor for commercial shipping, a status that feeds directly into war-risk insurance premiums, freight rates and the cost of moving grain, oil and other commodities out of Russia and Ukraine. Higher costs and crew reluctance can reroute trade flows and push food and energy prices up for import-dependent economies far from the conflict. The FT report does not provide specific casualty figures or name individual vessels and operators, limiting its immediate market read-through. It follows the London marine insurance market&\#x27;s Joint War Committee widening its Black Sea &quot;high risk&quot; zone to cover almost the entire sea, a designation that triggers additional war-risk premiums and crew war bonuses.

rss · FT Markets · Oct 25, 05:00

**Background**: The Black Sea is a critical export corridor for Ukrainian and Russian grain, as well as Russian oil products, and shipping there has been disrupted repeatedly since the 2022 full-scale invasion. Because standard marine insurance excludes acts of war, vessels entering conflict zones must buy separate war-risk cover, priced by designated high-risk zones such as those set by the London market&\#x27;s Joint War Committee. When a zone is widened or an attack escalates, premiums, crew bonuses and freight rates rise, and some owners simply stop calling at the affected ports.

## [Shenzhen adds over 100 billion yuan in computing-power-linked loans](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-25/102497147.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

At its third-quarter 2026 press conference on October 23, the People&\#x27;s Bank of China Shenzhen branch reported that medium- and long-term loan balances in four industries closely tied to computing-power construction rose by more than 100 billion yuan compared with the start of the year, as of end-September 2026. The branch also said it has guided local financial institutions to launch dedicated credit products such as &quot;computing power loans&quot;, &quot;token loans&quot; and &quot;computing power leasing loans&quot; to support Shenzhen&\#x27;s computing network and next-generation communications network buildout. The figures show that China&\#x27;s policy push behind the &quot;six networks&quot; infrastructure plan and AI industry development is being translated into concrete bank credit at the local level, with Shenzhen manufacturing loan balances up 22.8% year on year and IT/software services loans up 68.8%. It matters most for asset-light AI companies, which traditionally struggle to borrow because they lack collateral, and it signals a template that other cities&\#x27; PBOC branches may copy. The innovation lies in underwriting: instead of requiring hard collateral, banks assess AI borrowers using computing-industry data such as monthly token consumption, the value of computing-service contracts, receivables and API call logs. Some of the products already in the market nationally are pure-credit loans with credit lines of up to 30 million yuan, terms of up to 10 years and annualized rates as low as 2.5%, though the reported Shenzhen figures cover only four sectors in one city rather than the whole economy.

## [Caixin: RMB Up 9% Against Dollar as 18-Month Rally Extends](https://weekly.caixin.com/2026-10-24/102496749.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Caixin Weekly reports that the RMB has been in an appreciation cycle against the dollar for roughly 18 months, gaining about 9% over the period, after recovering from a trough near 7.35 per dollar in April 2025 to below 7.0 by the end of 2025 — a 4.6% rise from that April low. Market expectations now point to continued moderate, orderly appreciation at least through year-end. A currency move of this magnitude over 18 months materially shifts the calculus for China&\#x27;s exporters, who face tighter margins when foreign-currency earnings are converted back into yuan, and for foreign investors allocating to A-shares and yuan bonds, where currency gains add to local returns. A sustained, orderly appreciation also signals that capital-flow pressure has eased, giving Beijing more room to maneuver on monetary policy. The article distinguishes between two measures: the ~9% figure covers the full roughly 18-month cycle, while the 4.6% figure measures only the rebound from the April 2025 trough, so the two numbers should not be conflated. Notably, the appreciation resumed despite the global tariff war launched by the Trump administration in early 2025, which initially pushed the yuan to its weakest point of the prior depreciation cycle.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 25, 22:59

**Background**: China&\#x27;s onshore yuan \(CNY\) is not freely floating; it trades within a band around a daily reference rate set by the People&\#x27;s Bank of China, which heavily influences the currency&\#x27;s direction. The 7.0 level against the dollar is widely watched as a psychological line that policymakers have historically defended in both directions. Because the yuan has spent much of the past few years under depreciation pressure — driven by US-China rate differentials and capital outflows — a sustained rebound changes the framing for exporters, importers, and cross-border investors alike.

The threatened November 5 general strike, backed by the CGT and framed around the government&\#x27;s budget proposals, could disrupt transport, schools, energy and public services far beyond weekend marches, raising political and economic disruption risks for France. Authorities said the October 24 demonstrations were largely peaceful and markedly less violent than the previous weekend&\#x27;s arson, looting and clashes on October 17, though around a thousand people were arrested. The Interior Ministry and CGT figures diverge sharply \(410,000 versus 800,000 nationally\), a common discrepancy in French protest counts that makes turnout trends hard to pin down precisely.
