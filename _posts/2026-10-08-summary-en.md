## [China&\#x27;s Tsinghua Team Builds World&\#x27;s First Nuclear Clock](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11122-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A research team led by Tsinghua University has built and stably operated what it says is the world&\#x27;s first nuclear clock, using a self-developed 148-nanometer continuous-wave vacuum ultraviolet \(VUV\) laser together with thorium-229-doped calcium fluoride \(CaF2\) crystals; the results were published in Nature. Nuclear clocks based on the thorium-229 nuclear transition could become a new generation of time and frequency standards, potentially surpassing today&\#x27;s best optical atomic clocks, with applications in satellite navigation, deep-space exploration and precision tests of fundamental physics; this marks the first time such a device has been operated as a working clock, giving China a leading position in a young and highly competitive field. The thorium-229 isomer transition lies at an unusually low energy of about 8.3557 eV \(roughly 148.4 nm\), and CaF2 is used as the host crystal because its ~12 eV bandgap is large enough to accommodate that transition; the key technical hurdle is generating an intense, narrow-linewidth continuous-wave VUV source at that wavelength, since earlier four-wave-mixing and second-harmonic-generation schemes produced only nanowatt-level power.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 8, 05:19

**Background**: Today&\#x27;s most precise clocks, such as optical lattice and single-ion clocks, count oscillations of laser light that drive electron transitions in atoms or ions, and their ticking rate can be perturbed by stray electric and magnetic fields. A nuclear clock instead uses a transition inside the atomic nucleus, which is far more tightly bound and much less sensitive to the surrounding environment. Thorium-229 is uniquely suited for this because it is the only known nuclide whose first excited state is a long-lived isomer lying just a few electron volts above the ground state, rather than the kilo-electron-volt to mega-electron-volt range typical of nuclei, putting it within reach of vacuum-ultraviolet lasers. Researchers have also proposed using such clocks to search for possible slow drift of the fundamental constants over time.

## [OpenAI&\#x27;s Annualized Revenue Said to Be $20B Below Reported Figure](https://www.ft.com/content/b66a9858-f8fb-46cb-b506-44bfe26fca2a?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Financial documents shared with investors show that OpenAI&\#x27;s annualized revenue reached roughly $50 billion at the end of September, about $20 billion below the widely circulated $70 billion figure. Part of the gap comes from different accounting conventions: Anthropic counts revenue generated through cloud partners such as AWS and Google Cloud, while OpenAI does not, and OpenAI declined to comment on the discrepancy. The correction matters because the $70 billion figure has been used as headline evidence that enterprise and consumer demand for AI is accelerating, so a $20 billion haircut could temper the optimism that has been propping up AI-related valuations and investment plans. It also highlights how loosely reported AI revenue numbers can be compared, since OpenAI and Anthropic are often cited side by side even though they measure revenue differently. The core caveat is definitional: &quot;annualized revenue&quot; can mean an annualized run rate \(monthly revenue multiplied by 12, extrapolated from a short period\) or the stricter annual recurring revenue that only counts contracts of 12 months or longer, and the two produce different numbers. The gross-versus-net question on cloud-partner sales is also material, since recognizing partner channel revenue gross rather than net can inflate a top-line figure substantially without changing underlying economics.

## [ICANN receives 1,615 new gTLD applications, including AI-themed .agi and .asi](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1007132/icann-domains-2026-ai-agi) ⭐️ 6.0/10

ICANN&\#x27;s new gTLD application round drew 1,615 applications from 481 applicants, among them a cluster of AI-related strings: 10 different companies \(including Meta and OpenAI\) applied for .agent, seven applied for .agi, six applied for .asi and four applied for .intelligence. OpenAI filed for 15 top-level domains in total, while Meta applied for 21, and both are among the contenders for the AI-themed strings. A domain string is a scarce, essentially first-come branding asset, so whichever company wins .agi or .asi would control a namespace that could become the default address for AI products and services. The filings also show how AI labs are publicly aligning their brands with the AGI and ASI vocabulary, and the results will shape naming competition and pricing for years. Because several applicants want the same strings, .agent, .agi and .asi are far from settled — ICANN resolves such conflicts through objections, evaluation panels and, if necessary, auctions. The round is still early: final decisions are not expected until 2027, and applicants must also pass financial, technical and operational checks before they can actually run a registry.

## [CrowdStrike: Hacker Used Chinese Open-Source ARTEX and AI Relay Against South Korean Banks](http://xcai.pro/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

CrowdStrike said on October 7 that an unidentified, financially motivated attacker breached South Korean financial institutions and stole data between late September and early October, using the China-developed open-source agentic penetration-testing tool ARTEX. CrowdStrike assessed with medium confidence that the operator is Chinese-speaking but has not attributed the activity to any known group. The case is an early concrete example of LLM coding agents and cheap third-party API relays being folded into real offensive operations, lowering the skill and cost barrier for a single intruder to run what used to require a team. It also puts pressure on financial-sector defenders in South Korea and elsewhere, since the tooling involved is open source, freely downloadable and hard to fingerprint, while attribution remains weak. CrowdStrike found Claude Code session logs and ARTEX configuration files in an attacker-controlled open directory; the sessions indicated suspected use of DeepSeek v4.1-flash through the relay station xcai.pro, alongside Zhipu GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 for assistance. One session reportedly asked the model to draft a security-researcher résumé containing details such as age 26, a South China University of Technology background and Guangdong Maoming, which CrowdStrike says likely describe the attacker himself, though identity and the scale of the data theft are unconfirmed and the relay has since been shut down.

**Background**: ARTEX is an open-source, China-developed autonomous penetration-testing platform written in Go that drives LLM agents to find and re-test vulnerabilities, and it can be self-hosted via Docker or a single binary; it won Baidu&\#x27;s &quot;agent+&quot; attack-defense challenge. ## [Walda Smart Aquarium Power Strip Hack Kills Fish, Compensation Unclear](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/hcIs7ox_1BzMTWtsV_TwSg) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Multiple fish keepers in Guizhou and Guangdong reported that Walda Technology&\#x27;s aquarium smart power strip malfunctioned, automatically cutting power to their equipment and refusing to restart through the companion mini-program, resulting in the mass death of ornamental fish. Walda issued a public apology stating the incident was caused by a malicious hacker network attack rather than product quality or user error, and said it has repaired its servers, patched the vulnerability and reported the matter to police. The case exposes a core risk of cloud-dependent smart home devices: a single server-side or network compromise can disable physical hardware, and here it caused irreversible losses that no firmware update can undo. It also raises unresolved questions about liability and consumer rights when an IoT vendor&\#x27;s cloud is attacked, which could push the industry toward mandatory offline fallback controls for aquarium, pet and other life-support-adjacent devices. Walda&\#x27;s strips are widely used by marine and planted-tank hobbyists to power core equipment such as pumps, wave makers, protein skimmers and heaters, so an outage quickly halts water circulation and oxygenation and can kill fish and shrimp within a short time. The company promised to upgrade its firewall, add an offline fallback mechanism and arrange 24-hour monitoring, but it has not specified any compensation plan for the economic losses, which has drawn widespread user dissatisfaction.

**Background**: Smart power strips typically connect to the cloud through Wi-Fi and are controlled via a phone app or WeChat mini-program, so switching commands often depend on a vendor&\#x27;s servers rather than being executed locally. This architecture creates a single point of failure: if the cloud account, server or communication channel is compromised or goes down, the device may be unable to respond to any user input.
