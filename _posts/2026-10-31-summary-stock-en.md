## [PBOC Shifts Policy Rate Anchor to Overnight Reverse Repo at 1.25%](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-30/102499195.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On October 30, 2026, the People&\#x27;s Bank of China announced that starting November 2 it will conduct overnight reverse repo auctions every working day using a fixed-rate, quantity-based tender, with the current operation rate set at 1.25%. The 7-day reverse repo, the policy anchor since 2024, is being converted into a fixed-quantity, rate-auction, multi-price tender used mainly around tax payment dates and month-end, and all other tools that were priced off the 7-day rate now price off the overnight rate with unchanged spreads. This is a structural change to China&\#x27;s monetary policy framework: the overnight rate becomes the pricing benchmark for the entire money market and the short end of the yield curve, affecting banks, asset managers and anyone exposed to short-term funding costs. Market participants note the practical result is a downward shift in the rate center, so the change eases short-end funding conditions even without a headline policy rate cut. The 7-day reverse repo now uses a multi-price allotment model similar to the medium-term lending facility \(MLF\) and outright reverse repos, and its operation rate is expected to stop being disclosed, meaning the 7-day rate will no longer serve as a public policy signal. Because the overnight rate carries no term premium and reflects the shortest-end liquidity conditions most directly, the PBOC considers it a cleaner signal for conveying its policy stance, while the unchanged add-on spreads preserve the existing interest rate corridor structure.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 31, 13:24

**Background**: A reverse repo is an open market operation in which the central bank buys securities from primary dealers and agrees to sell them back later, injecting short-term liquidity into the banking system. The &quot;policy rate anchor&quot; is the rate the central bank uses to signal its stance and around which it builds an interest rate corridor to guide market rates. China has been moving from quantity-based monetary control, where it targets money and credit aggregates, toward price-based control, where it steers the level of interest rates; making the overnight reverse repo the anchor is a key step in that transition.
