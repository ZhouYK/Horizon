## [EU CBAM Rules Recognize China&\#x27;s Carbon Market, But Free Allowances Don&\#x27;t Count](https://www.caixin.com/2026-11-18/102506358.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On November 16, 2026, the European Commission formally published Implementing Regulation \(EU\) 2026/2498, which sets out how a carbon price effectively paid in a third country is converted into a deduction against CBAM certificates, along with the requirements for payment evidence and the qualifications of independent verifiers. The key point for China is that only carbon costs actually paid in cash for allowances can be counted — allowances received for free do not qualify. The rules directly determine the CBAM bill facing Chinese exporters of steel, aluminium, cement and fertilisers, since a large share of their emissions is covered by freely allocated allowances that now yield no deduction. They also create a strong incentive for China to shift its national carbon market from free allocation toward auctioning, because only paid carbon costs translate into lower EU border charges. Deductions are calculated from the carbon price effectively paid, evidenced by payment documentation and verified by independent verifiers meeting the regulation&\#x27;s qualification criteria, with the value converted into CBAM certificates whose price tracks EU ETS auction prices. The practical effect is that free allocation under China&\#x27;s national ETS — currently the dominant allocation method — provides no offset, so only auctioned or otherwise purchased allowances reduce the CBAM liability.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 18, 15:15

**Background**: CBAM \(the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism\), often called the EU&\#x27;s &quot;carbon tariff&quot;, requires importers of carbon-intensive goods into the EU to buy CBAM certificates reflecting the embedded emissions of those goods, so that foreign producers face carbon costs comparable to those borne by EU producers under the EU ETS. After a transitional reporting-only phase, the mechanism moved into its definitive regime in 2026, with certificate prices linked to EU ETS allowance auction prices. To avoid double charging, the regulation allows importers to subtract carbon costs already paid in the country of origin — which is why the design of China&\#x27;s own emissions trading system, where allowances have so far been mostly handed out for free rather than auctioned, now matters for export competitiveness.

JPMorgan is attempting to offload roughly $2bn of stalled software buyout debt by moving it into the high-yield bond market at steep price cuts and double-digit yields. The move marks a willingness by banks to crystallize losses rather than continue holding the debt on their balance sheets. Although $2bn is modest relative to the broader credit market, the sale sets a new clearing price for similar hung deals and signals deteriorating risk appetite in leveraged finance. It pressures private-equity-backed software companies and matters to credit and equity investors in tech as well as to bank capital markets desks. The debt is being repackaged from leveraged loans into high-yield bonds, a route banks use when syndicated loan buyers have stepped back; the double-digit yields reflect the speculative, sub-investment-grade risk buyers are being asked to absorb. rss · FT Markets · Nov 18, 16:30

**Background**: &quot;Hung debt&quot; refers to underwritten loans or bonds that a bank promised to finance for a buyout but could not syndicate to investors, leaving it stuck on the bank&\#x27;s own balance sheet. Leveraged finance is the business of arranging high-yield debt for acquisitions and leveraged buyouts \(LBOs\), typically sponsored by private equity firms. High-yield, or junk, bonds are rated below investment grade and pay higher yields to compensate for greater default risk. When buyers balk at the risk, banks must cut prices and accept losses to clear the backlog.
