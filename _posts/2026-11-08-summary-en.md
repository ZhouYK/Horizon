## [Democrat Lawmaker Urges Congress to Ban AI Recursive Self-Improvement This Year](https://x.com/AlexBores/status/2118494668769878037) ⭐️ 6.0/10

US Democratic Representative Alex Bores publicly called on Congress to pass legislation this year banning AI recursive self-improvement \(RSI\) — the process of letting AI systems automatically build their next-generation successors. He cited OpenAI&\#x27;s stated goal of an automated AI researcher by March 2028, Anthropic staff predictions that AI could handle roughly 80% of frontier model development by the end of 2027, and METR evidence of accelerating AI R&amp;D as reasons the ban is urgent. This is one of the most direct legislative calls yet targeting RSI, a capability frontier labs themselves describe as a potential route to loss of human control, so it could push the issue into formal US policy debate. If such a ban gained traction it would directly constrain the roadmaps of OpenAI, Anthropic and other frontier developers, and shape global AI governance norms. Bores argued that errors or misalignment in current AI systems could be amplified with each successive generation, and he noted that figures such as Bernie Sanders, Steve Bannon and Yoshua Bengio have also raised concerns or pushed for regulation. The proposal is advocacy rather than enacted law, and as of 2026 RSI-related projects are ongoing with AI increasingly used for coding and research, though human work remains a bottleneck.

**Background**: Recursive self-improvement \(RSI\) describes an AI system iteratively designing a more capable successor, which some researchers argue could trigger an &quot;intelligence explosion&quot; culminating in artificial superintelligence. METR \(Model Evaluation and Threat Research\) is a Berkeley-based nonprofit that evaluates frontier models on long-horizon agentic tasks and works with AI companies on pre-deployment testing. AI alignment, the subfield of AI safety concerned with building systems that reliably do what their designers intend, is central to the concern: if systems build their own successors, securing and monitoring them becomes far harder.

## [Menlo Ventures: Chinese models claim 89% of enterprise open-source AI spend](http://z.ai/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A report released this week by Menlo Ventures found that Chinese models account for 89% of global enterprise spending on open-source AI models, with Moonshot&\#x27;s Kimi leading at 61%, followed by DeepSeek at 15%, Z.ai&\#x27;s GLM family at 11%, and Meta&\#x27;s Llama at just 7%. The figures suggest that the US open-weight ecosystem, long anchored by Meta&\#x27;s Llama, has largely lost the enterprise market to Chinese labs, reshaping which vendors global companies build on and where open-model tooling and cost advantages are concentrated. The headline share comes with an important caveat: open-source models represent only 4% of total model spending, while Anthropic and OpenAI together take 86% of spending through their APIs. The data comes from a September survey of nearly 400 US enterprise decision-makers, as reported by the South China Morning Post.

**Background**: Open-source \(more precisely, open-weight\) models are released with downloadable parameters that companies can self-host or fine-tune, as opposed to closed models accessed only through paid APIs. Moonshot AI&\#x27;s Kimi, DeepSeek&\#x27;s V3/V4 series, and Z.ai&\#x27;s GLM family are Chinese open-weight model lines that have become widely used by developers and enterprises. Menlo Ventures is a Silicon Valley venture capital firm whose periodic surveys of enterprise AI adoption are commonly cited as a market benchmark.
