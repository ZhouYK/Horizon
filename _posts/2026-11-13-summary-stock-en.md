The ruling addresses the adequacy of the government&\#x27;s impact assessment rather than setting a specific emissions cap, and the report cites no company-level cost figures or immediate effect on individual fields.

Those groups argue that a state that licenses petroleum exports must count the emissions released when that oil is eventually burned abroad, not just the emissions from extraction and processing at home. The country&\#x27;s Supreme Court previously upheld Arctic drilling licences in a 2020 climate case, so this ruling marks a shift in how far Norwegian courts are willing to scrutinise the climate consequences of export-oriented production.
