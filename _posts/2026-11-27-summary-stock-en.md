## [EU Weighs Tighter Export Controls on Battery Waste Bound for China](https://www.ft.com/content/a8ecccda-f986-4ae9-9f45-9470d2be8c65?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The European Union is considering tightening export controls on battery waste — the shredded, mineral-rich powder known as &\#x27;black mass&\#x27; — in order to stop critical minerals from being shipped to China for processing and then re-imported into Europe as refined metals. According to the Financial Times, the measure is still under discussion and no final rule, scope, or threshold has been set. If adopted, the rules would reshape the economics of battery recycling in Europe and could force recyclers to build domestic refining capacity rather than selling intermediate material abroad. It is part of a broader EU push under the Critical Raw Materials Act to reduce dependence on China for lithium, cobalt, nickel and manganese processing, and would directly affect EV supply chains and recycling startups. Black mass is the concentrated mixture of electrode materials produced when lithium-ion batteries are shredded and separated, and it still requires hydrometallurgical or pyrometallurgical refining before metals can be reused. The EU&\#x27;s Critical Raw Materials Act lists 34 critical raw materials, 17 of them deemed strategic, giving Brussels a policy framework to justify restricting outflows — but the proposal&\#x27;s timing, legal instrument, and whether it would cover all black mass or only certain mineral fractions remain undefined.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 27, 13:26

**Background**: Lithium-ion batteries contain valuable metals such as lithium, nickel, cobalt and manganese. Recycling them usually happens in two steps: mechanical shredding into &\#x27;black mass&\#x27;, then chemical refining to extract usable metals. China currently dominates the global refining stage, so much of Europe&\#x27;s black mass is exported there and the recovered metals are bought back — a loop that EU policymakers see as a strategic vulnerability.

## [PBOC Halves Large-Denomination CD Thresholds: 100,000 Yuan for Individuals, 5 Million for Institutions](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-11-27/102509400.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 27, 2026, the People&\#x27;s Bank of China published the revised &quot;Administrative Measures for Large-Denomination Certificates of Deposit,&quot; cutting the individual subscription minimum from no less than 200,000 yuan to no less than 100,000 yuan, and the institutional minimum from no less than 10 million yuan to no less than 5 million yuan. The new rules also broaden transfer channels for the first time by explicitly allowing both third-party platforms and banks&\#x27; own channels to handle CD transfers, early withdrawals and redemptions. The change applies across the whole banking industry and touches a deposit market worth tens of trillions of yuan, so it could shift banks&\#x27; funding costs, intensify competition for retail deposits and pull demand away from some retail wealth-management products. Halving the entry threshold also brings large-denomination CDs, which are legally deposits and thus principal-protected, within reach of a much wider group of ordinary savers. The measures were published as PBOC Announcement No. 30 of 2026 and take effect on March 21, 2027, following a draft released for public comment in June 2026. PBOC officials said the central bank will study supporting arrangements for third-party platforms so that CD transfers can proceed in an orderly way, meaning the trading infrastructure is not yet in place.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 27, 14:53

**Background**: A large-denomination certificate of deposit \(大额存单\) is a bank deposit certificate that, unlike a wealth-management product, is legally a deposit but pays more interest than an ordinary time deposit, typically priced at a markup over the benchmark rate. Banks issue them to companies and individuals at market-based prices, usually in limited volumes, and they can be traded in the secondary market. Compared with ordinary time deposits they require a much larger minimum subscription but offer better liquidity because they can be transferred, so the entry threshold directly determines how many retail savers can access the product.
