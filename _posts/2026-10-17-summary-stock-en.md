The disclosure is a narrative account from a former official rather than an audited balance sheet, and it does not break out how much of the 18% decline came from outright repayment versus maturity rollover, restructuring, or asset sales.

**Background**: Local governments in China borrow heavily through financing vehicles known as chengtou, which issue standardised bonds as well as &quot;non-standard&quot; products such as trust loans and local exchange placements that are costlier and less transparent. ## [Li Auto Spins Off Chip Unit, Raising 625 Million Yuan From Outside Investors](https://www.caixin.com/2026-10-17/102494521.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Li Auto disclosed on October 16 that its chip business has been incorporated as an independent company, Shanghai Mahexin Technology Co., Ltd., which was registered in August 2026 and is wholly owned by Li Auto through its subsidiary Shanghai Mahezhi Xin. The new entity simultaneously raised 625 million yuan in external financing, while Li Auto itself injected a further 1 billion yuan via Mahezhi Xin, and it will serve as the company&\#x27;s primary platform for chips and domain controllers in China. The spin-off signals that Chinese automakers are moving toward vertically integrated, self-developed intelligent-driving silicon to cut costs and secure supply, following the path already taken by Tesla, NIO and BYD. For Li Auto, which has been under pressure on margins, in-house chips could eventually cut a major bill-of-materials item by half or more. According to Caixin, intelligent-driving chips in premium models cost roughly $1,600–$2,000 each and could climb to $2,500–$3,000, while self-developed silicon can cut that cost by at least half. The financing and investment amounts are modest relative to Li Auto&\#x27;s overall scale, so the near-term financial impact is limited and the move is better read as a medium- to long-term supply-chain and intelligence bet.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 17, 13:44

**Background**: Intelligent-driving chips are the specialized processors that run perception and planning algorithms for advanced driver assistance, distinct from cockpit chips that power infotainment; automakers increasingly design them in-house to gain cost and supply control. These chips sit inside domain controllers, high-performance control units that consolidate functions previously spread across many separate electronic control units in a vehicle&\#x27;s electrical architecture. Self-developed silicon is not new — Tesla, NIO and BYD have all pursued it — and the common motivation is escaping expensive merchant chips and aligning algorithm and hardware design under one team.
