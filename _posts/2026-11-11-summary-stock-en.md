The FT reports that the US oil major is targeting the deadlines attached to the EU framework rather than the underlying capture-and-storage standards themselves. The case marks an escalation in the use of investor-state arbitration against climate and energy regulation, potentially giving fossil-fuel companies a template for delaying or reshaping EU green rules. A suspension of the timing requirements could shift the economics of CCS projects across Europe and complicate compliance planning for energy companies and industrial emitters that have built business cases around the EU&\#x27;s CCS timetable. rss · FT Markets · Nov 11, 21:26

**Background**: Investment treaties such as the Energy Charter Treaty \(ECT\) let companies from one signatory country sue another signatory&\#x27;s government in international arbitration — a mechanism known as investor-state dispute settlement \(ISDS\) — if they believe regulation has harmed their investments. In parallel, the EU has built a CCS legal framework, including the CCS Directive and its integration into climate legislation, that sets out permitting and safety requirements for CO2 capture, transport and storage, and the deadlines attached to that framework are what ExxonMobil is contesting.
