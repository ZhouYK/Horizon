## [Ofgem raises UK household energy price cap 26% from January](https://www.ft.com/content/e9dea6f2-dcf8-4ec5-96ec-0415694bf897?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

UK energy regulator Ofgem has confirmed a 26% increase in the household energy price cap, taking effect from 1 January, lifting the typical annual dual-fuel direct-debit bill from £1,723 to roughly £2,166. Ofgem said the new three-month cap reflects a sharp surge in wholesale gas costs. The jump feeds directly into UK CPI and household disposable income, squeezing consumer spending just as the economy faces weak growth, and it raises political pressure on the government over energy affordability. It also shapes earnings for energy suppliers and utilities, since the cap determines how much of the wholesale cost surge can be passed through to customers. The cap is reviewed quarterly and sets a maximum unit rate and standing charge for standard variable tariffs rather than a hard ceiling on total bills, so households using more energy will pay more than the headline figure, and it applies to England, Scotland and Wales while Northern Ireland&\#x27;s equivalent is set by its own Utility Regulator. Because the cap only lasts three months, another change is due on 1 April, and analysts have already flagged the possibility that the elevated rate may ease by then.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 25, 07:58

**Background**: Ofgem&\#x27;s price cap limits how much energy suppliers in Great Britain can charge per kilowatt hour and for the daily standing charge on default standard variable tariffs, with the headline figure expressed as what a typical dual-fuel household paying by direct debit would spend per year. Suppliers buy gas and electricity wholesale in advance, so when wholesale gas prices spike, the cap is recalculated to let them recover those costs — which is why cap changes lag and then amplify moves in the gas market. The cap does not apply to fixed-rate deals, which is why consumers are often advised to compare tariffs.
