## [China Slows Humanoid Robot IPOs With Informal Approval Hurdles](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/china-slows-humanoid-robot-ipo-rush-hype-outruns-reality-2026-09-21/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Chinese regulators are slowing down the rush of humanoid robot IPOs by informally raising approval hurdles through &quot;window guidance,&quot; according to people familiar with the matter, with at least six Chinese humanoid robot companies currently preparing to list. The intense share-price swings in Unitree Robotics&\#x27; early trading after its listing were cited as a trigger for the tightened scrutiny, though no formal ban has been issued. The move signals a shift in China&\#x27;s robotics and AI investment landscape away from hype-driven valuations toward demonstrable commercial viability, which could reshape fundraising timelines and valuations for a wave of humanoid robot startups and the venture capital backing them. It also affects public-market investors, who are being pushed to weigh actual deployment and order volumes rather than narrative-driven growth stories. Officials are not abandoning the sector but are pressing companies to prove deployment scale, order volumes, and commercial viability, and are scrutinizing whether revenue from government-supported projects reflects genuine market demand rather than policy subsidy. The mechanism is informal &quot;window guidance&quot; rather than a published rule change, so the timing and outcome for individual listings remain uncertain.

**Background**: Unitree Robotics \(Hangzhou Yushu Technology\), founded in 2016 by Wang Xingxing, began with consumer quadruped robots and moved into humanoid robots in 2024, pricing a second-generation humanoid at roughly US$16,000. &quot;Window guidance&quot; is a long-standing informal channel through which Chinese regulators steer IPO timing, pricing, and approval in the A-share market without issuing formal rules. The scrutiny comes after a sharp contraction in Chinese listings — IPO proceeds fell about 80% in 2024 to just under $9bn, with only 96 companies going public, down 60% from 2023.

## [AWS Bedrock Adds Moonshot AI&\#x27;s Kimi K3 in First Chinese-LLM Revenue-Share Deal](https://36kr.com/newsflashes/3992769217428488) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Amazon Web Services announced that its model marketplace Amazon Bedrock now hosts Moonshot AI&\#x27;s open-weight flagship model Kimi K3, allowing enterprise developers worldwide to call it directly through Bedrock&\#x27;s APIs. The move formally confirms the previously rumored revenue-sharing arrangement in which Moonshot AI splits model-usage revenue with overseas cloud providers. This is reportedly the first time a Chinese large-model company has exported its model capabilities to the world&\#x27;s top three cloud providers under a revenue-share model, rather than a one-off licensing or self-hosted arrangement. It signals that Chinese open-weight models are becoming standard inventory on Western cloud marketplaces, which could intensify price competition and reshape how AI model makers and cloud vendors split value. Kimi K3 is described as a sparse mixture-of-experts \(MoE\) flagship with roughly 2.8 trillion parameters, a 1M-token context window and MIT-licensed open weights, released in mid-July across Kimi App, Kimi Work, Kimi Code and the Kimi API. Under the model, cloud vendors charge for inference and share a portion of the usage revenue with Moonshot AI based on call volume, though the exact split ratio has not been disclosed.

**Background**: Amazon Bedrock is AWS&\#x27;s fully managed service that lets developers access leading foundation models through a single API without managing infrastructure, and it also supports custom and third-party models. Moonshot AI \(月之暗面\) is the Chinese developer behind the Kimi assistant and its K-series models, with K3 being its open-weight flagship for long-horizon coding and knowledge work. &\#x27;Revenue sharing&\#x27; here means the cloud provider hosts and serves the model on its own platform, then passes a cut of the per-token usage fees back to the model developer. Industry analysis suggests cloud vendors typically capture a substantial share of AI model revenue as inference compute costs, which is why distribution deals and their split ratios matter so much to model makers.

## [Ant Group merges Alipay, digital payment and Sesame Credit into new business group](https://stock.10jqka.com.cn/20260921/c680135771.shtml) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Ant Group CEO Han Xinyi issued an all-staff letter announcing that the Alipay business group, the digital payment business group and the Zhima \(Sesame\) Credit business unit will be consolidated into a single new Alipay business group. Wu Minzhi will serve as president of the new group while simultaneously holding the role of Ant Group CPO. The reshuffle puts Ant&\#x27;s flagship app, its core payment business and its consumer credit-scoring arm under one leader, which should shorten decision chains as Alipay faces intensifying competition from WeChat Pay and Douyin Pay in China&\#x27;s mobile payment market. It also signals how Ant is organizing itself in the post-regulatory-rectification era, and will affect how partners and merchants interface with Alipay&\#x27;s product and credit offerings. Wu Minzhi joined Ant Group in August 2021 after more than 20 years at Alibaba, where she served as president of the international trade business group, president of the B2B business group and chief customer officer. The merger folds Zhima Credit, Ant&\#x27;s private credit-scoring and loyalty system that draws on Alibaba transaction and behavioral data, into the same unit that runs Alipay, the group&\#x27;s consumer-facing app.

**Background**: Ant Group is the fintech affiliate of Alibaba Group and the operator of Alipay, one of China&\#x27;s two dominant mobile payment platforms. Zhima Credit \(芝麻信用\), launched in 2015, is a private credit-scoring and loyalty program that computes scores from users&\#x27; purchases and interactions across Alibaba-affiliated services, and its scores are used for deposit-free rentals, travel and other perks. China&\#x27;s big tech firms routinely reshuffle business units to concentrate related products under a single leader, and Ant&\#x27;s own restructuring follows a period of heavier regulatory oversight of its payments and consumer-lending businesses.

## [xAI Releases Grok 4.7, Focused on Coding and Knowledge Work](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-7) ⭐️ 6.0/10

xAI announced Grok 4.7, describing it as its strongest model yet for coding and knowledge work, with the same price and speed as Grok 4.6 at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens. The model is already live in Cursor, Grok Build, and the xAI API. By holding pricing flat while claiming capability gains, xAI is pushing an aggressive value-for-money pitch against rival coding models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google. Developers who already build on Grok can upgrade with no cost or integration change, which lowers the friction for adoption in agentic coding tools. xAI says Grok 4.7 uses a larger base model with extended reinforcement learning, making it better at self-verification and long-context handling, and claims the strongest refusal and jailbreak-resistance behavior of its models so far. Red-teaming access to those safety capabilities is limited to a set of cybersecurity partners by invite only, and no benchmark numbers were published in the announcement.

**Background**: Grok is the large language model family developed by Elon Musk&\#x27;s xAI, and point releases like 4.6 to 4.7 typically refine training rather than change the underlying architecture. Grok Build is xAI&\#x27;s terminal-based AI coding agent, which runs as a full-screen TUI that reads codebases, edits files, and executes shell commands, while Cursor is a popular VS Code-derived AI code editor. Red teaming is the practice of adversarially probing a model for prompt injection, jailbreaks, and other safety failures before and after release.

## [M6 Mac mini leak: multi-core matches Intel&\#x27;s flagship, GPU nearly doubles M4](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1JQhz6fE1x) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A Geekerwan-sourced leak claims Apple&\#x27;s M6 Mac mini uses TSMC&\#x27;s N2 \(2nm-class\) process with a 2+4+6 CPU core layout, a 4.8 GHz super core, and a 12-core GPU. In the reported benchmarks, multi-core performance ties Intel&\#x27;s Panther Lake Core Ultra X9 388H, single-core stays ahead, multi-core is over 50% faster than M4, and GPU/gaming performance comes close to double the M4&\#x27;s. If accurate, this would be one of the first concrete signs of Apple shipping a 2nm-class chip, putting Apple Silicon on par with Intel&\#x27;s latest Panther Lake flagship in multi-core for the first time in a desktop Mac mini. A near-doubling of GPU performance would also strengthen Apple&\#x27;s pitch for gaming and GPU-heavy workloads on entry-level desktops. The leak specifies a 2+4+6 core configuration \(2 performance, 4 mid, 6 efficiency\), a 12-core GPU with heavily improved ray tracing, roughly 25W CPU full-load power and about 65W for the whole system under a combined CPU+GPU stress test. The limits are significant: the figures come from a Telegram repost of a Bilibili video with no independent verification, and Intel&\#x27;s Panther Lake scales from 25W to 80W+, which makes cross-platform power and performance comparisons inherently uneven.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 21, 16:32

**Background**: Apple Silicon chips integrate CPU, GPU and unified memory on a single die, and each generation is tied to a TSMC process node; N2 is TSMC&\#x27;s first node to move from FinFET to gate-all-around \(GAAFET\) nanosheet transistors, which TSMC says began volume production in Q4 2025. Intel&\#x27;s Panther Lake \(Core Ultra X9 388H\) is a 16-core chip \(P, E and LP-E cores, no hyperthreading\) using Intel&\#x27;s Xe3 integrated graphics with 12 cores, positioned as a rival to Apple&\#x27;s M-series in laptops. Because Mac mini benchmarks are typically run through Geekerwan&\#x27;s public testing, leaks of this kind usually trace back to that channel before official confirmation.
