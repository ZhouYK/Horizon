## [US Treasury Rout Lifts 10-Year Yield Half a Point in Worst Month in Four Years](https://www.ft.com/content/39de7709-7b5b-42f6-ad90-df50f1308ea2?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Selling pressure in US government debt intensified over the past month, driving the yield on 10-year Treasuries up by half a percentage point — roughly 50 basis points — in the worst monthly move in four years. Market participants described the price action as a self-reinforcing &quot;vicious loop&quot; of selling, in which falling bond prices push yields higher and trigger yet more selling. The 10-year Treasury yield is the global benchmark for long-term borrowing costs, so a move of this speed feeds directly into mortgage rates, corporate bond yields and equity valuation models. A self-reinforcing selloff also raises systemic concerns about market liquidity and duration risk, with the potential to spill over into credit markets and other global risk assets. A half-point move in a single month is unusually large for Treasuries, which are normally the world&\#x27;s deepest and most liquid government bond market. Moves of this kind typically reflect a combination of shifting inflation expectations, heavy government issuance and the Federal Reserve&\#x27;s policy path, and they are often amplified by forced selling from leveraged positions, such as duration-heavy funds facing margin calls.

rss · FT Markets · Sep 30, 21:32

**Background**: Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions: when investors sell Treasuries, their prices fall and the yield they offer to new buyers rises. The 10-year yield is the most closely watched single number in the bond market because it anchors long-term US interest rates, and it is used as a discount rate in models that value stocks, bonds and other assets. When selling accelerates, losses on existing holdings can force leveraged investors to sell more, creating the kind of feedback loop that market participants call a &quot;vicious loop.&quot;
