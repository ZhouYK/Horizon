## [Diamondback nears $5bn deal to expand Permian Basin drilling](https://www.ft.com/content/a56fc76f-af32-45b2-8ae1-d5e211cb9643?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Diamondback Energy is reportedly close to a roughly $5bn acquisition that would expand its drilling footprint in the Permian Basin, according to the Financial Times. The deal has not been confirmed and no target company has been publicly identified. The move would mark another sizeable consolidation step in the US shale heartland, where producers have been buying up acreage to gain scale and cut costs amid volatile oil prices. A deal of this size is market-moving for Diamondback and its energy peers, and further concentrates ownership of prime Permian acreage. The reported price tag of about $5bn is large but the target, deal structure and financing mix remain unknown, so the valuation of the acreage cannot yet be assessed. Because the report is unconfirmed, terms could still change or the talks could fall through.

rss · FT Markets · Nov 15, 18:27

**Background**: The Permian Basin, spanning West Texas and southeastern New Mexico, is the highest-producing oil field in the United States, with output of roughly 4.2 million barrels of crude per day in 2019. It has produced hydrocarbons for about 100 years, supplying more than 35.6 billion barrels of oil as of January 2020, and its stacked formations allow multiple oil-bearing zones to be fractured from a single wellbore. In recent years US shale producers have pursued consolidation — acquiring rivals and adjacent acreage — to build scale and improve returns under investor pressure, a trend this reported deal would extend.

## [China&\#x27;s October New Loans and Total Social Financing Both Miss Expectations](https://deepview.caixin.com/topic/BQ02.000006405.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

China&\#x27;s October 2024 new RMB loans and new total social financing \(TSF\) both came in below market expectations, according to Caixin&\#x27;s deep-reading report on the monthly financial data. The miss points to softer-than-expected credit demand rather than a supply-side constraint on lending. Credit and social financing are the main channels through which Chinese policy stimulus reaches the real economy, so a broad shortfall signals weak private borrowing appetite and raises questions about the pace of any growth recovery. It also strengthens the case for further monetary easing, including reserve requirement ratio cuts or policy rate reductions, which affects expectations for bonds, the yuan and bank profitability. The headline figures were not accompanied by detailed breakdowns in the available summary, so the composition matters: whether the weakness came from household medium- and long-term loans \(mortgages\), corporate bill financing substituting for real loans, or a slowdown in government bond issuance within TSF. A common caveat with October data is seasonal distortion from the National Day holiday, which shortens the working period.

rss · 财新网 · Nov 15, 10:02

**Background**: Total social financing \(TSF, 社会融资规模\) is the People&\#x27;s Bank of China&\#x27;s broad measure of credit flowing to the real economy; it covers RMB loans, foreign-currency loans, entrusted and trust loans, undiscounted bankers&\#x27; acceptances, corporate and government bonds, and equity financing. New RMB loans are the single largest component, so the two indicators are usually read together: TSF shows the total financing picture, while loans reveal how much comes from the banking system. Because Chinese growth has long been credit-intensive, monthly credit data is one of the most closely watched gauges of underlying demand.
