## [US Long-Term Borrowing Costs Hit Highest Since 2004 on Treasury Sell-Off](https://www.ft.com/content/2d87f8bf-d529-4997-90c5-393ef65d280c?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

US long-term borrowing costs climbed to their highest level since 2004 after a brutal sell-off in the US Treasuries market drove long-dated yields sharply higher. The move strains public finances by raising the interest bill on outstanding and newly issued government debt. Treasury yields are the benchmark price of money across the global financial system, so a sustained rise lifts borrowing costs for governments, households and companies, pressures equity valuations, and tightens financial conditions worldwide. It also narrows the Federal Reserve&\#x27;s room to cut rates, since higher long-term yields already do some of the tightening for it. The 10-year Treasury yield has climbed toward roughly 5.18%, a level last seen around 2007, before the global financial crisis. A key caveat is that higher yields simultaneously reflect stronger growth expectations, persistent inflation concerns and worries about the sheer volume of government debt issuance — meaning the cause matters as much as the level.

rss · FT Markets · Sep 24, 20:32

**Background**: US Treasuries are debt obligations issued by the US government and sold at auction to investors; they are widely treated as the world&\#x27;s safest asset. Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions, so when investors sell Treasuries en masse — a &\#x27;sell-off&\#x27; — prices fall and yields rise. Long-dated yields such as the 10-year and 30-year serve as the reference rate for mortgages, corporate borrowing and many other assets, which is why their level is watched so closely. Rising yields therefore mean the government must pay more to roll over and issue debt, worsening an already large fiscal deficit.
