## [First Experimental Confirmation of Glueballs by Chinese-Led BESIII Team](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/pvyNR1lN7QPx3IrpB3WtUg) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Beijing Spectrometer III \(BESIII\) collaboration, led by Chinese scientists at the Institute of High Energy Physics, announced on August 6 that after 15 years of research they have experimentally confirmed the existence of glueballs. The team established that the particle X\(2370\), first discovered in 2011, has quantum properties and a flavor-singlet nature consistent with a glueball, making it the first confirmed form of matter composed entirely of gluons. This result is a landmark in particle physics, as glueballs were predicted by the Standard Model decades ago but had never been observed experimentally. Confirming the glueball provides a direct test of quantum chromodynamics \(QCD\) and contributes to verifying the Standard Model&\#x27;s description of the strong force. The BESIII experiment operates at the Beijing Electron-Positron Collider II \(BEPCII\), a large magnetic spectrometer weighing about 800 tons. The identification relied on measuring X\(2370\)&\#x27;s quantum state properties in 2024 and uncovering multiple new decay modes confirming its flavor-singlet character, which researchers call the most definitive glueball search result in nearly fifty years.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 6, 07:31

**Background**: In quantum chromodynamics, the theory of the strong force, quarks and gluons carry a property called color charge. Gluons, unlike photons, interact with each other because they themselves carry color charge, so they can bind together into particles called glueballs that contain no valence quarks. The Standard Model predicts the existence of glueballs, but for decades no experiment had conclusively identified one. The BESIII collaboration at IHEP has been searching for glueball candidates in the decay products of particle collisions at BEPCII.

Security researchers Tommy Mysk and Talal Haj Bakry discovered that Apple&\#x27;s iCloud Private Relay can be bypassed when websites use or claim to support passkeys, exposing users&\#x27; real IP addresses. 404 Media confirmed the flaw with a test site. This undermines the core privacy promise of a paid iCloud+ feature and affects all iOS browsers, since they must use WebKit. The root cause is that passkey authentication requests are issued by the system credential service rather than Safari, bypassing the Private Relay proxy path. Last month, Apple&\#x27;s &\#x27;Hide My Email&\#x27; was also reported to leak users&\#x27; real email addresses.

Passkeys are a passwordless authentication standard based on WebAuthn that often use the operating system&\#x27;s credential manager.
