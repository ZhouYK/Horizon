## [US and China Agree to AI Incident Channel in Eight-Point Deal](https://international.caixin.com/2026-09-26/102488946.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

## [Foreign inflows into US stocks hit record $940bn as demand for US debt fades](https://www.ft.com/content/a1a1318b-8051-4539-a485-5a4e1d021578?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Overseas purchases of US equities topped $940bn in the 12 months to July, a record high for foreign capital flows into the US stock market. The surge coincided with strong gains in the S&amp;P 500, while foreign appetite for US debt declined over the same period. The record inflow shows foreign investors are increasingly channelling capital into US equity risk rather than US government debt, a shift that can influence the dollar, Treasury yields and the cost of financing America&\#x27;s external deficit. It also reinforces the concentration of global savings in a handful of US mega-cap stocks, which raises the stakes for any reversal in those flows. The $940bn figure covers the year to July and is a gross measure of overseas purchases of US equities, so it does not necessarily represent net foreign positioning after accounting for sales. The divergence between equity inflows and fading debt demand can reflect factors such as relative return expectations, currency-hedging costs for foreign bond buyers, and concerns about US fiscal trajectories.

rss · FT Markets · Sep 26, 10:00

**Background**: The US runs persistent current-account and fiscal deficits that must be financed partly by foreign capital, and the US Treasury publishes data tracking who holds US securities. When foreign investors favour equities over bonds, it changes the mix of demand for US assets: equities benefit from growth and earnings optimism, while Treasuries are more sensitive to interest-rate and currency-hedging considerations. The S&amp;P 500 is the benchmark index tracking 500 large US companies and has been driven higher in recent years largely by a small group of technology and AI-related megacaps.

Caixin&\#x27;s financial column reported on September 25, 2026 that the &quot;Yahao&quot; group of companies, led by Zhejiang second-generation entrepreneur Chen Hao, has been formally placed under investigation on suspicion of illegally absorbing public deposits, with roughly 400 investors affected. Chen himself has reportedly fled, leaving investors who had placed money in his products since 2017 without clarity on what their funds were actually invested in. The case illustrates how private investment and asset-management networks in China can raise money from ordinary retail investors for years on promises of high yields and a polished public image, only to collapse when the money runs out. It signals that regulators are still pursuing unlicensed fundraising schemes well after the headline P2P lending crackdown, and it is a concrete example of investor-protection risk in the loosely supervised private wealth sector. Chen Hao&\#x27;s &quot;Yahao group&quot; spanned dozens of companies, and its marketing relied heavily on near-identical 2018 media &quot;interviews&quot; that portrayed him as a self-made young talent, along with claims that he had gathered more than 200 fellow &quot;second-generation rich&quot; people into a club and joined 15 young entrepreneurs to launch the BU Group for real estate and financial investment. Investors say interest was paid annually at attractive rates from 2017 onward until the scheme failed, and many bought in simply because friends had met Chen or visited the company&\#x27;s campus.

**Background**: In China, &quot;illegally absorbing public deposits&quot; \(非法吸收公众存款\) is a criminal charge under Article 176 of the Criminal Law, typically applied to unlicensed fundraising from the general public, and it carries a lower threshold than outright fraud charges. The term &quot;second generation&quot; \(富二代\) refers to the children of first-generation private entrepreneurs, some of whom moved away from their families&\#x27; traditional manufacturing businesses into investment and asset management, often under the slogan &quot;industry-finance integration&quot; \(产融结合\). ## [Beijing Sets Pre-Sale Transition Window for Legacy Housing Projects](https://www.caixin.com/2026-09-25/102488720.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 24, 2026, Beijing&\#x27;s Municipal Housing and Urban-Rural Development Commission, the Municipal Planning and Natural Resources Commission, the Beijing Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the Municipal Housing Fund Management Center jointly issued an implementation opinion on the national notice on improving the commodity housing sales system. Under it, projects that obtained land \(or were in the process of land transfer\) before August 28, 2026 but have not yet obtained a construction planning permit may still apply for pre-sale under the old conditions until the end of 2027, provided the district government confirms on-time delivery, pre-sale fund supervision is strengthened, and the project is filed with municipal departments. The policy gives developers of existing projects a concrete, dated runway to keep pre-selling, easing near-term cash-flow and project-delivery risk at a time when China is moving toward stricter regulation — and eventually curtailment — of the pre-sale model. Because it is a local implementation document rather than a national policy shift, its impact is sector-specific and incremental, but it offers a template other cities may follow when applying the national rules. The transition is conditional rather than automatic: projects must win district government confirmation of on-time completion and delivery, strengthen pre-sale fund supervision, and be filed with municipal departments. Even for these legacy projects, the new requirements on pre-sale escrow fund supervision and mortgage loan disbursement still apply, so the leniency is limited to pre-sale eligibility conditions rather than to fund oversight.

rss · 财新网 · Sep 26, 13:59

**Background**: In China, commodity housing is commonly sold before it is finished under the pre-sale \(预售\) system, which lets developers collect buyer payments early to fund construction; this has long been paired with pre-sale fund supervision \(预售资金监管\), a tripartite arrangement among the housing authority, a supervising bank and the developer that restricts how escrowed funds can be used. A construction planning permit \(建设工程规划许可证\) is a mandatory legal document issued under the Urban and Rural Planning Law confirming that a project complies with planning and technical standards — without it, construction cannot legally proceed. Beijing&\#x27;s new opinion implements a national notice on improving the commodity housing sales system, and its transition clause is aimed at projects that are stuck between the old and new regimes.

## [Qinhuangdao Auto Dealers Accused of Nationwide Trade-In Subsidy Fraud](https://www.caixin.com/2026-09-25/102488746.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On September 23, a group of car owners from across the country traveled to the Qinhuangdao Public Security Bureau&\#x27;s Economic Development District branch to file reports against Qinhuangdao Zhongdian Auto Trade Co. and Qinhuangdao Mingtian Auto Sales Co., alleging a scam the two dealers had run for more than a year using national trade-in subsidies as bait. Zhang Xiaolei, founder of a financial risk-control firm, told Caixin that preliminary estimates put the number of victims at over 1,000, and that the figure could keep growing as word spreads. The case shows how government subsidy programs and consumer auto-finance channels can be weaponized, leaving victims holding loans they never intended to take on or vehicles they cannot legally transfer title to. Because multiple banks and automaker captive finance companies are reportedly entangled, it raises broader credit-risk, compliance and know-your-customer concerns for lenders active in subsidy-driven car sales. Caixin notes the scheme&\#x27;s methods were not novel, but the firms repeatedly succeeded by exploiting victims&\#x27; eagerness for a bargain, with victims spread across many parts of the country. A risk-control specialist estimates more than 1,000 victims so far, and no listed company is directly named in the report, which limits its immediate market impact.

rss · 财新网 · Sep 26, 13:59

**Background**: China has promoted auto trade-in \(以旧换新\) subsidies under which consumers who scrap or trade in an old vehicle and buy a new one can receive a state subsidy, with dealerships typically handling the application paperwork on their behalf. Auto finance in this context refers to loans from banks as well as installment plans from automakers&\#x27; captive finance arms, and buyers often have to sign loan contracts and complete vehicle title-transfer \(过户\) registration as part of the purchase. In this case, the two Qinhuangdao dealerships are accused of abusing consumers&\#x27; desire for subsidies and low prices to get them to sign loan or pledge documents, ultimately leaving them with unexplained debts or cars whose ownership cannot be transferred.
