## [Mathematician Claims Claude Helped Prove Riemann Hypothesis](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/levent-alpoge/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

On November 20, 2026, mathematician Levent Alpöge published a PDF claiming that he and Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, extended the method to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and formalized the arguments without assumptions. He also said Claude far outpaced him near the finish line. If substantiated, a machine-checked proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and its generalizations would be one of the most important results in mathematics and a landmark for AI-assisted mathematical discovery. It would reshape number theory and set a new benchmark for large language models in theorem proving and formal verification. The claim is currently self-published in a personal PDF and has not been peer-reviewed or independently confirmed. Secondary reports say the formalization was encoded in Lean and runs to more than 250,000 pages, but those details still require verification.

rss · Simon Willison · Nov 20, 16:30

**Background**: The Riemann Hypothesis, proposed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859, conjectures that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2; it is deeply tied to the distribution of prime numbers and is among the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics. The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis extends the same statement to broader L-functions, such as Dirichlet L-functions. Formal verification uses proof assistants like Lean to machine-check every logical step, so a formalized proof can in principle be verified by software rather than relying only on human review.

rss · Simon Willison · Nov 20, 19:21

**Background**: The Riemann Hypothesis, one of the Clay Mathematics Institute&\#x27;s Millennium Prize Problems, asserts that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2. The Generalized Riemann Hypothesis extends this assertion to the broader family of global L-functions — including Dirichlet L-functions, Dedekind zeta-functions of number fields, and L-functions attached to elliptic curves and modular forms. Many number-theoretic results are known to follow from GRH, yet the conjecture has only been proven in the analogous function-field setting \(work associated with Weil and Deligne\), not over the rationals. Brian Conrad is a Stanford number theorist and algebraic geometer known for exceptionally rigorous expository and verification work, and Ravi Vakil, also at Stanford, is an algebraic geometer who writes the widely read &quot;Math 216&quot; blog and the Foundations of Algebraic Geometry notes.

## [Tencent Open-Sources Hy4, Completing the Full Hunyuan LLM Series](https://www.aibase.com/news/32446) ⭐️ 7.0/10

On November 20, Tencent published the Hy4 weights for download and deployment on GitHub, Hugging Face, ModelScope, and GitCode, completing the open release of the entire Hunyuan LLM series \(Hy3 preview, Hy3, Hy4 preview, Hy4, and Hy4 flash\). Hy4, originally announced on November 4, is a Mixture-of-Experts model with 770B total parameters, 49B active parameters, and a context window exceeding 1M tokens. Releasing a 770B-scale flagship as open weights puts Tencent alongside other Chinese labs that have made large MoE models freely downloadable, giving the open-model ecosystem a new top-tier option that can be self-hosted instead of accessed only via API. The completion of the whole series, from flagship to cost-efficient variants, means teams can standardize on one model family across both quality-sensitive and budget-constrained workloads. As a sparse MoE model, Hy4 only activates 49B of its 770B parameters per token, so inference compute is far lower than a dense model of the same total size, but all 770B parameters still have to be loaded into memory. Tencent has so far positioned the release around safety checks and openness of the weights rather than publishing benchmark comparisons or license terms in this brief.

aibase · AIbase · Nov 20, 17:01

**Background**: Mixture-of-Experts \(MoE\) is an architecture in which a model&\#x27;s layers contain many separate sub-networks, or &quot;experts,&quot; and only a few of them fire for each token instead of the whole network. This splits the parameter count into two numbers: total parameters, which determines how much memory is needed to hold the model, and active parameters, which determines speed and inference cost — the same distinction popularized by models such as DeepSeek&\#x27;s 671B/37B. ModelScope, one of the four distribution channels used here, is a Chinese model-as-a-service platform that hosts downloadable models and related tooling.

## [ChatPRD Ends Free Trials After Token Fraud and Agent Tunneling](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/token-fraud-is-here-and-its-bad/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Claire Vo, founder of the AI product-management platform ChatPRD, said in a YouTube video that the service has faced a flood of token fraud, with groups looping free trials and promo codes and tunneling through its agent to resell AI usage or run commercial operations. She is now winding down free trials entirely. For AI SaaS companies, free trials are not just a marketing cost but a direct subsidy of GPU inference, so abuse can turn a trial funnel into cheap compute and model access for resellers. The decision signals that more AI product builders may have to tighten trials, identity checks, and usage controls to protect margins. The abuse included free-trial and promo-code loops that let operators use ChatPRD&\#x27;s agent for commercial work, such as building Roblox and Fortnite maps, while other prompts asked for SVGs of pelicans riding bicycles—likely a technique for fingerprinting the underlying model family. ChatPRD has been live since the GPT-4 era, and Vo&\#x27;s response was to shut down free trials rather than keep absorbing the cost.

rss · Simon Willison · Nov 20, 02:49

**Background**: Token fraud, sometimes called LLMjacking when stolen credentials or hijacked accounts are involved, is the unauthorized use of an AI platform&\#x27;s inference capacity; the victim pays for the compute while the abuser gets free or resold model access. In this case, fake or repeated free-trial accounts are used to tunnel requests through ChatPRD&\#x27;s agent, effectively turning a product feature into an unofficial API. AI companies have responded with device and account-linking checks, risk scoring, rate limits, and trial restrictions. The &\#x27;pelican riding a bicycle&\#x27; prompt is an ad hoc benchmark: because different model families render it in distinctive ways, it can help identify which model sits behind an API.
