## [49 Brain Imaging Studies Show Widespread Brain Changes After COVID-19](https://www.psypost.org/brain-scans-reveal-widespread-structural-and-functional-changes-in-patients-foll/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A systematic review published in Cerebral Cortex analyzed 49 brain imaging studies and found widespread structural and functional brain alterations in COVID-19 patients. These changes involve regions linked to emotion, memory, and executive function, and are associated with symptoms such as brain fog and fatigue. This review consolidates evidence that COVID-19 can have measurable effects on the brain, potentially explaining persistent cognitive and emotional symptoms in long COVID. It also highlights the need for long-term follow-up studies to clarify whether these changes are reversible or permanent. The review reports gray matter volume reductions or cortical thinning in frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes, as well as white matter microstructural abnormalities detected by diffusion tensor imaging. Some studies also found altered resting-state functional connectivity and abnormalities in limbic structures such as the insula, hippocampus, and amygdala, with some imaging measures correlating with cognitive and emotional performance.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 10, 00:02

**Background**: Voxel-based morphometry \(VBM\) is a computational technique that measures local brain tissue concentrations from structural MRI scans, allowing whole-brain comparisons of gray matter volume. Resting-state fMRI evaluates spontaneous brain activity and functional connectivity without requiring an explicit task, often revealing networks like the default mode network. Diffusion tensor imaging \(DTI\) maps white matter tracts by measuring water diffusion, which is sensitive to microstructural damage. These techniques are commonly used to investigate how diseases, including infections, might alter brain structure and function.

## [China&\#x27;s Top AI Models Still Rely on Nvidia; Huawei Migration Costly](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3363491/chinas-top-ai-still-trained-nvidia-chips-what-delaying-switch-local-tech) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Chinese AI developers report that their most advanced models are still trained on Nvidia chips, because Nvidia&\#x27;s CUDA software does not run directly on Huawei&\#x27;s Ascend hardware. Migrating models to Ascend requires substantial code rewriting and optimization, with one team estimating time and cost increases of at least 50%. This highlights the practical limits of China&\#x27;s push for semiconductor self-sufficiency in AI despite U.S. export controls. The software ecosystem lock-in around CUDA remains a major barrier, meaning Nvidia&\#x27;s influence persists until migration tooling and native ecosystems mature. An engineer said migrating an open-source model to Ascend takes about two or three engineers one extra month, while models with only weights released may require about 10 engineers for over half a year. Meituan announced in June that its LongCat-2.0 was fully trained and run on a cluster of 50,000 domestic AI chips, but did not name the supplier.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 10, 09:44

**Background**: Nvidia&\#x27;s CUDA is a proprietary parallel-computing platform and API that lets software use Nvidia GPUs for AI and general-purpose processing, and it has become the default software stack for AI development. Huawei&\#x27;s Ascend series chips, such as the 910B and 910C, are domestic Chinese AI accelerators that require a different software stack, so CUDA code must be rewritten. Model weights are the numerical parameters inside a neural network; when only weights are released, developers lack the full source code needed to adapt the model to new hardware, making migration harder.
