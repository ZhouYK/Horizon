A Bloomberg analysis of Stanford University&\#x27;s AI Index shows 84% of Chinese respondents feel excited about AI, versus only 38% of Americans. The article attributes the gap to differing perceptions of benefits, trust, and regulatory effectiveness, with Chinese respondents reporting 72% trust in AI compared to 32% in the US. This reveals how national context shapes AI adoption and policy expectations. As AI deployment accelerates globally, understanding these perception gaps helps predict divergent consumer behavior, workforce attitudes, and regulatory approaches between the US and China. The article notes the difference is not that Chinese people see fewer risks, but that they judge whether AI benefits will reach them and whether regulation works differently. Chinese consumers more readily link technology to expanding opportunities and improving life, so anxiety often drives adoption, while Americans worry more about job loss, misinformation, and concentration of tech power.

**Background**: Stanford University&\#x27;s AI Index is an annual report tracking trends in AI research, deployment, economics, and public opinion globally. ## [Researchers Use AI to Identify Telegram Piracy, Leading to 524 Channel Shutdowns](https://torrentfreak.com/researchers-hunt-telegram-pirates-with-ai-tool-flag-hundreds-of-channels/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

Researchers developed an AI tool called Anti-RIP that scanned approximately 249,000 Telegram channels and flagged 802 suspected piracy channels with 98% test accuracy. After the findings were shared with Telegram and rightsholders, 524 previously unknown piracy channels were shut down within 61 days. This demonstrates a practical AI application for copyright enforcement on messaging platforms, where automated scanning can scale takedown efforts significantly. It also highlights an emerging AI-driven cat-and-mouse game between rightsholders and pirates on Telegram. The study is based on an analysis of 1,057 Telegram channels and roughly 209,000 posts, identifying 983 piracy channels that collectively drew 4.85 billion views and involved 19,033 film and TV titles. The researchers have open-sourced Anti-RIP and released the dataset on GitHub, though the tool still produces false positives.

**Background**: Telegram has become a major hub for video piracy, with public channels that can be easily found and joined, alongside private channels that are harder to detect. The scale of the problem is significant, with an estimated $18 billion lost to piracy via Telegram, which motivated the development of Anti-RIP. The tool is designed as a real-time framework for detecting emerging video piracy communities on Telegram, and sharing findings with the platform and rightsholders can lead to channel closures. However, pirates may also adopt AI tools to evade detection, creating an ongoing technological arms race.
