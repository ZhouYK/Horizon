## [Diesel prices jump after IEA rules out additional fuel release](https://www.ft.com/content/2761b6af-df11-49e8-a348-792a9597c9af?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The International Energy Agency told its members that no additional fuel will be released beyond the 400mn-barrel collective stock release already agreed in March, and clarified that the 100mn barrels promised by the G7 will come out of that same 400mn-barrel reserve. Diesel prices jumped immediately after the clarification, as traders had been expecting the G7 pledge to represent fresh supply on top of the earlier release. Diesel is the fuel that moves trucks, tractors, trains and heavy industry, so its price feeds quickly into freight rates, food and goods prices and headline inflation. By confirming that no new barrels are coming, the IEA removed the hope of near-term supply relief for a distillate market that is already tight, effectively telling refiners and traders they must cope with what they have. The key detail is accounting rather than volume: the G7&\#x27;s headline 100mn-barrel pledge is not additive but a carve-out from the March 400mn-barrel release, so the net amount of new oil reaching the market is unchanged. Moreover, strategic stock releases are largely crude oil, which offers only indirect and delayed relief to diesel, since distillate supply depends on refinery capacity, maintenance schedules and blending economics.

rss · FT Markets · Oct 7, 19:02

**Background**: The International Energy Agency is an organisation of major oil-consuming economies that coordinates emergency releases of strategic petroleum reserves when supply is disrupted; its members, including the United States and European countries, hold government-controlled crude stocks for exactly this purpose. The G7 is the group of leading advanced economies that often aligns its energy pledges with IEA action. Such collective releases have historically been used to blunt price spikes, for example after Russia&\#x27;s invasion of Ukraine in 2022, but they mostly inject crude rather than refined products. Diesel, sometimes called gasoil, is a middle distillate refined from crude and is the benchmark fuel for road freight, agriculture and industry, which is why distillate tightness is watched closely as an inflation indicator.

## [China&\#x27;s PBoC Buys Gold for 23rd Straight Month, Largest Monthly Increase](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-07/102491004.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

On October 7, 2026, China&\#x27;s State Administration of Foreign Exchange \(SAFE\) reported that official gold holdings rose by 740,000 ounces in September to 77.47 million ounces, the 23rd consecutive month of purchases and the largest single-month increase of this cycle. Over the same period, foreign exchange reserves fell by $38.1 billion to $3.4003 trillion. A 23-month buying streak with the largest monthly addition of the cycle is a meaningful structural demand signal for gold, reinforcing the trend of central banks diversifying reserves away from dollar-denominated assets. At the same time, the headline drop in FX reserves is not evidence of capital flight, since it is largely explained by currency translation and asset-price valuation effects rather than actual outflows. Cumulative purchases during this cycle have reached 4.67 million ounces, and China&\#x27;s gold reserves are now 2.3 times the level of 17 years ago. Before this cycle the PBoC had completed three earlier rounds of accumulation, adding 5.92 million, 3.40 million and 10.16 million ounces respectively.

**Background**: Official reserve data are released monthly by SAFE and, since 2015, have been aligned with the International Monetary Fund&\#x27;s Special Data Dissemination Standard so that holdings such as gold are disclosed on a comparable international basis — a change made when China sought to have the renminbi included in the IMF&\#x27;s Special Drawing Rights \(SDR\) basket. Foreign exchange reserves are reported in US dollars, so when the dollar strengthens or non-dollar assets fall in price, the dollar value of reserves declines even without any buying or selling by the central bank; analysts call this a negative valuation or currency-translation effect. Gold, by contrast, is a reserve asset whose quantity changes only when the central bank actually buys, making the monthly increase a direct measure of official demand.

## [Bank of China Mastercard Holders Hit by Upgraded Credential-Stuffing Fraud Abroad](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-07/102491010.html) ⭐️ 6.0/10

In late September 2026, a number of Bank of China Mastercard cardholders reported unauthorized overseas transactions, many of them in Brazil, even though their cards never left their possession, and some of the fraudulent charges were linked to cards provisioned in Apple Pay. Mastercard confirmed to Caixin that it and Bank of China&\#x27;s credit card center immediately traced the transaction path, closed the loophole and reversed the abnormal transactions, saying an agreement had been reached with the affected cardholders; Bank of China effectively blocked nearly half of the attempts, while China Merchants Bank and Agricultural Bank of China blocked essentially all of theirs. The case shows that credential-stuffing attacks have been upgraded and can now reach card-not-present transactions and even tokenized wallet payments, which are usually considered a safer way to pay. It puts pressure on issuers, the Mastercard network and wallet providers to strengthen transaction monitoring and account-takeover defenses, and it reminds cardholders that overseas fraud risk can materialize without any physical card loss. Caixin did not disclose the total amount or the number of cards involved, and Bank of China&\#x27;s interception rate of nearly half implies that a substantial share of the fraudulent transactions initially went through before being reversed. The victims&\#x27; transactions were concentrated in Brazil, a region long flagged as high-risk for card fraud, and the fact that some charges ran through Apple Pay suggests the attackers bypassed or exploited token-based payment paths rather than simply copying magnetic-stripe data.

**Background**: Credential stuffing \(known in Chinese as &quot;撞库&quot;\) is an attack in which criminals take username-and-password pairs leaked from one breached service and automatically try them against other services, betting that users reuse the same credentials; the same logic can be applied to stolen card or account data tested against payment endpoints.
