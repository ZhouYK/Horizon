This intervention could trigger significant global market volatility, as the unwinding of the yen carry trade may cause abrupt currency moves and forced selling of risk assets. The move signals a major shift in Japan&\#x27;s monetary stance with broad implications for global investors and financial stability. The yen carry trade involves borrowing yen at extremely low interest rates to invest in higher-yielding assets abroad, and Japan&\#x27;s yen-buying intervention reverses these flows. This may force investors to unwind positions quickly, leading to sharp yen appreciation and potential losses for leveraged traders.

rss · The Economist Finance · Aug 13, 08:49

**Background**: A currency carry trade profits from the interest rate spread between borrowing a low-yielding currency and lending a high-yielding one. Currency intervention is a monetary policy operation in which a central bank buys or sells foreign exchange to influence the exchange rate. When Japan buys yen, it effectively strengthens the currency and disrupts the profitability of the carry trade, forcing these bets to be unwound.
