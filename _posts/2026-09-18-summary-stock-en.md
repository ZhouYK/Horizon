After the Federal Reserve&\#x27;s latest rate hike took effect, the RMB/USD exchange rate moved in the opposite direction from the usual pattern, strengthening past the 6.7 level instead of depreciating. The strength is described as short-term and flow-driven: heavy settlement by exporters plus an appreciation-leaning midpoint fix, supported by improving China-U.S. ties. rss · 财新网 · Sep 18, 14:59

**Background**: The RMB&\#x27;s daily trading price is anchored by a central parity rate, or midpoint, that the People&\#x27;s Bank of China sets each morning, with the onshore rate allowed to move within a band of about ±2% around it; because the midpoint is a policy signal, a stronger fix is often read as official comfort with appreciation. &quot;Settlement&quot; \(结汇\) refers to exporters and households converting foreign-currency earnings — mostly U.S. dollars — into RMB at banks, and a burst of such conversions creates real demand for the yuan. The China-U.S. interest rate differential is the gap between Chinese and U.S. bond yields; when U.S. rates are higher, capital tends to favor dollar assets, which normally pressures the yuan weaker.

**Background**: A Ponzi-like scheme is one in which returns paid to earlier participants are funded not by genuine investment profits but by money contributed by newer investors, so the structure collapses once new inflows slow or redemptions surge.
