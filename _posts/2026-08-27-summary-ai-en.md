## [Qwen3.8-Flash-Next: Open-Weight MoE Previews Qwen4 Architecture](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/26/qwen38-flash-next/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Qwen has released Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts model with 125B total parameters and only 6B active, positioned as an early preview of the Qwen4 architecture. Simon Willison shared initial hands-on impressions, testing Unsloth quantized versions on an NVIDIA DGX Spark. This matters because it gives the AI community an early, open-weights look at the architecture that will power Qwen4, including hybrid linear-sparse attention and built-in speculative decoding. Developers can experiment with these design choices now and prepare for the official Qwen4 release. The model uses a Mixture-of-Experts design with 125B total parameters but only 6B active, which provides a significant performance boost. Simon Willison tested the 72.5GB UD-IQ1\_S and 78.9GB UD-Q2\_K\_XL quantizations; he reported his favorite results came from the UD-Q2\_K\_XL version at xhigh reasoning effort.

rss · Simon Willison · Aug 26, 23:52

**Background**: Mixture of Experts \(MoE\) is a technique that uses multiple specialized sub-models, called experts, with a router that activates only the most relevant ones for each token, enabling much larger models with less computational cost than dense models. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is described in the announcement as an early preview of the Qwen4 architecture, reportedly including Qwen Sparse Attention \(QSA\) and Gated DeltaNet. Unsloth provides quantized GGUF versions of the model, such as UD-IQ1\_S and UD-Q2\_K\_XL, which reduce the model footprint for local inference on hardware like NVIDIA DGX Spark.

This article from qz.com provides a comprehensive explainer on the European Union&\#x27;s Artificial Intelligence Act, detailing its scope, regulatory rules, and the risk-based tier system that classifies AI systems. It walks readers through the key obligations for various types of AI applications. The EU AI Act is the world&\#x27;s first comprehensive horizontal regulation of artificial intelligence, setting a potential global benchmark for AI governance. It will directly affect any organization that develops, deploys, or uses AI systems within the EU market, and is likely to influence legal frameworks in other jurisdictions. The legislation categorizes AI systems into risk tiers: unacceptable risk \(prohibited\), high risk \(subject to strict obligations such as data governance, documentation, and human oversight\), limited risk \(transparency requirements\), and minimal risk \(voluntary codes of conduct\). It also includes specific provisions for general-purpose AI models like GPAI, with transparency and copyright-related requirements.

google\_news · qz.com · Aug 27, 04:23

**Background**: The European Commission first proposed the AI Act in April 2021, and after negotiations between the European Parliament and Council, it was approved in 2024. The regulation follows a risk-based approach, meaning the level of regulatory burden is proportional to the potential harm of the AI system. It applies not only to EU-based providers but also to providers and deployers outside the EU whose systems affect people in the EU. Implementation is phased, with bans on prohibited practices taking effect earlier than obligations for high-risk systems.

## [Nature Paper Proposes Interoceptive AI for Adaptive Autonomous Agents](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiX0FVX3lxTE00VnlsRnlfa2ZyNGJpLTdEdDNiWFJ6VnRpeVVxcFRvaWE5V2JCR0Z3dC1KRVUxSk9GM1RYcm1yVFBvb0p0Y3BEcFVHV2Y1T0dxcWFqS3hyNkpDS1BqeXJv?oc=5) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper from Nature introduces a life-inspired interoceptive AI framework designed to help autonomous agents choose goals based on their internal needs and survive in changing environments. The framework factorizes internal and external environmental states and applies mathematical properties inspired by biology. This research addresses a longstanding challenge—creating AI that is truly autonomous and adaptive rather than only reactive to external cues. It could advance robotics, embodied AI, and other systems that must operate under uncertainty and changing conditions. The proposal emphasizes the need to separate internal state variables from external environment states, and to incorporate life-inspired mathematical properties into the design. The paper appears to build on the concept of interoception, the biological sense of internal bodily signals such as hunger and fatigue.

google\_news · Nature · Aug 26, 10:00

**Background**: Interoception, a term coined by neurophysiologist Charles Sherrington in 1906, refers to the sense of the internal state of the body. Biological organisms use interoceptive signals to maintain homeostasis and guide behavior. In artificial intelligence, most agents rely primarily on external perception and task-specific rewards, lacking a sense of their own internal conditions. This paper proposes that endowing machines with a form of interoception could make them more autonomous and adaptive.

The surgery was performed on a tumor in the pituitary gland, and the AI system worked alongside the surgeon while the surgeon remained in full control.
