The latest development is that Chapanda \(02555.HK\), one of the stocks involved, was suspended from trading before the market opened on October 13 and was then notified by Hang Seng Indexes Company on October 14 that it will be removed from the Hang Seng Index series effective October 23. **Background**: &quot;Bao pan&quot; \(包盘\) is a Hong Kong market term for an arrangement in which a party effectively agrees to take up or guarantee an entire new-share offering, gaining control over how allocations are distributed; when combined with fabricated subscription demand, it can distort IPO pricing and set up post-listing trading gains at the expense of ordinary investors. The Hang Seng Index series is a family of benchmarks compiled by Hang Seng Indexes Company, and inclusion or removal matters because passive funds and ETFs tracking those benchmarks must mechanically buy or sell the affected stock. ## [US FCC Expands Import Curbs to Huawei, ZTE Components](https://www.caixin.com/2026-10-15/102493706.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new rule from the US Federal Communications Commission \(FCC\) took effect on October 13, 2026, barring devices containing &quot;logic-bearing hardware&quot; made by Huawei, ZTE and other entities on the FCC Covered List from obtaining FCC certification needed to enter the US market. The FCC adopted the rule on July 23, 2026, extending restrictions that previously applied only to finished equipment down to the component level. The change closes what regulators call a &quot;component loophole&quot;: even if a device is assembled by a non-restricted company, it is now disqualified if any logic-processing chip inside comes from a covered entity. This raises compliance and sourcing costs across the global electronics supply chain and could push assemblers to redesign products or sever ties with Huawei and ZTE semiconductor suppliers. The rule applies to &quot;logic-bearing hardware components&quot; rather than finished goods, and FCC certification is a practical prerequisite for selling radio-frequency devices in the United States. The FCC justified the measure by arguing that such logic components could be maliciously controlled for surveillance, interference, sabotage or unauthorized access, regardless of who assembles the final product.

rss · 财新网 · Oct 15, 13:13

**Background**: The FCC&\#x27;s Covered List names companies deemed national-security risks, with Huawei and ZTE among the most prominent; equipment from those firms has been barred from US networks and subsidy programs for years. Logic-bearing hardware refers to chips and modules that perform computing or processing functions, as opposed to passive parts such as resistors or connectors. The new rule shifts oversight from the finished device to the bill of materials, making component-level provenance a compliance issue for the whole supply chain.
